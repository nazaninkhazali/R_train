# 3.1 Gold nanoparticle-based labels: how engineering improves the colorimetric signal

## 3.1.0 Where the colour of a lateral flow strip comes from, and where it is lost

The result of a colorimetric lateral flow immunoassay (LFIA) is a coloured line. It forms when the labelled sandwich is captured at the test line and the label absorbs enough visible light to be seen against the white nitrocellulose membrane. The strength of that line depends on three things: how many labelled particles reach and are held at the line, how strongly each captured particle changes the appearance of the membrane, and how clearly that change can be distinguished from the background, by eye or by a reader. Gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) became the default label because they absorb visible light strongly, are simple to make and are easy to couple to antibodies (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Their weakness is a high limit of detection (LOD) when only a few particles reach the line (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229).

Each of these three dependencies can be attacked separately, and this is how the part is organised. Section 3.1.1 deals with the particle itself and with what is attached to its surface, because a particle that is badly sized or badly conjugated cannot give a good line however it is read. Section 3.1.2 deals with making more of the gold that has already been captured, by enlarging it after the sandwich has formed. Section 3.1.3 deals with particles designed to have a larger optical effect each (clusters, dimers, shells, composites). Section 3.1.4 deals with gold structures that carry a second signal, for which only the colour channel is evaluated here. Sections 3.1.5 to 3.1.7 leave the particle and look at the binder and antigen, the assay format and the optics of the strip. Section 3.1.8 describes the conventional gold strips that apply none of these changes and serve as the baseline, and Section 3.1.9 states what the evidence supports. The other labels, the post-capture catalytic amplification, the changes to the strip, the nucleic-acid amplification before the strip and the reading by devices and algorithms are covered in Sections 3.2 to 3.6.

For every study the same four questions are asked: what limited the signal, which technology addressed it, what the paper reports against its own baseline, and what the improvement costs. A change is only called an improvement when the same paper reports a conventional or unmodified strip run beside it; fold-changes from different papers use different baselines, matrices and readouts and are never ranked against each other. The target virus is named for every method. Where a paper also reports a SERS, photothermal or fluorescence readout, that readout is marked in the tables with ‡ and is not counted. A dagger (†) marks a paper that was read in full; all other papers were assessed from the abstract only, and a figure is then given as the abstract states it.

**Scope and reading mode.** The part covers 132 included records (2015 to 2026): 119 were assigned to it at the abstract stage, 14 were added after the full text showed a naked-eye or colour result, and one (a thermoplasmonic gold nanoprism strip) was removed after the full text showed that its viral RNA readout was thermal only and that its visual limits were for cancer markers (DOI: 10.1002/smll.202514946). Of the 119 records assessed from abstracts, 49 state that the strip is read by eye, 1 that a device is used, 5 either eye or device, 63 describe a strip without saying how it is read and 1 has no abstract; 29 of them report a second readout or a non-colour comparator. "Not stated" means only that the abstract is silent. Fifty-one of the 132 papers have been read in full, and for these the reading mode is taken from the full text.

## 3.1.1 The particle and its surface: getting the standard label right

**Particle size.** Large AuNPs carry more colour, but they also move more slowly through the membrane and may not reach the test line in useful numbers (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229; DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). Size is therefore a trade-off, and two studies treat it directly. For the hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), gold particles of 34.2 to 137.8 nm were grown stepwise from a seed (up to 14 growth steps). Particles of 42.7 nm gave the strongest test-line signal at 10, 100 and 500 ng/mL HBsAg, and in human whole blood spiked with 500 ng/mL HBsAg they gave a clearer line than gold made by the Turkevich-Frens method or a commercial HBV kit (Humasis). No LOD value is stated in the text read, and the line was read with an image analyser (DOI: 10.3390/s16122154). For dengue virus NS1, 40 nm colloidal gold, 80 nm gold nanospheres and 150 nm gold nanoshells were compared with the same antibody: the 80 nm particles gave the most distinct and consistent test lines, the 150 nm shells gave false-positive signals in every buffer, and no fold gain over 40 nm is stated (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics16030395).

**Antibody orientation.** What sits on the gold matters as much as the gold. Attached at random, an antibody may present its binding site toward the particle. On 100 nm AuNPs for HBsAg, a thiolated hydrazide ligand attached the antibody through its Fc region. This needed about 7 times less antibody (0.2 against 1.5 mg per pmol of AuNPs), shortened conjugation from 90 to 10 minutes, and gave an LOD of 2 ng/mL against 5 ng/mL for carbodiimide coupling in the same study, a 2.5-fold gain. A portable strip reader measured optical density, and 35 HBsAg-positive and 15 negative sera agreed with a Roche chemiluminescence immunoassay (R2 = 0.9527, no false positives) (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2021.338623).

**Sample matrix and the surface of the particle.** The surface of colloidal gold is also reactive towards the sample. For SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG on a colloidal gold strip read by eye, the anticoagulant EDTA-K2 changed the result: in EDTA-K2 plasma from 112 patients the lines were stronger and more IgM-positive patients were found than in serum (for example 17 weak and 15 clear IgM positives in plasma that were negative in serum), and 40 IgG samples read as strongly positive that were positive only in serum. The authors attribute this to EDTA-K2 chelating and aggregating colloidal gold, which sodium citrate and lithium heparin did not do. No false positives were found in a blank or in 200 healthy controls, and EDTA-K2 plasma agreed best with the chemiluminescence assay (DOI: 10.2147/ijn.s281594).

Three mechanistic studies show that the protein layer adsorbed onto the probe, the protein corona, changes how the probe binds in NS1 sandwich assays. For Zika virus NS1 on gold nanostars, strips run in buffer or bovine serum albumin showed false-positive test lines, whereas strips run in human serum or on serum-pretreated nitrocellulose did not, and a preformed corona restored the specificity of antibody-antigen binding (LOD 1.6 nM, effective dissociation constant 12.7 nM in serum) (DOI: 10.1021/acs.bioconjchem.6b00523). For dengue virus serotype 2 NS1, a preformed apolipoprotein A1 corona gave an LOD of 1.24 nM and a dissociation constant of 0.83 nM, whereas a preformed albumin corona lowered the test-line intensity by 33% at a given NS1 concentration (dissociation constant 0.14 nM) so that a meaningful LOD could not be determined (DOI: 10.3390/pharmaceutics14112439). For model antibody-antigen pairs (mouse IgG, Zika NS1 and dengue NS1) in albumin and human serum, adsorbed PEG-backfilled probes aggregated as the antigen rose and lost signal, chemically grafted probes (NHS, hydrazide) stayed stable although the NHS probes performed worst in serum, and sonication or a single pre-wash restored signal (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.0c08628). A comparison of antibody-conjugation strategies in dengue NS1 sandwich assays is also mechanistic and gives no values in its abstract (DOI: 10.1080/15321819.2016.1269338). These studies explain why nominally identical strips perform differently, but they report few diagnostic data (Table A1a).

**Different particles and different ligands on the gold.** Several studies change the particle or put a different molecule on it. Hollow gold nanoparticles have a molar extinction coefficient that the authors state (1.48 x 10^10 M^-1 cm^-1) is much larger than that of solid gold particles of the same size. For the HIV-1 p24 antigen, the antibody adsorbed through hydrogen bonding and van der Waals forces at one main binding site, and the strip gave a darker blue test line and higher optical density in serum than the commercial Alere HIV-1/2 Ag/Ab Combo (LOD 0.02 ng/mL), a sensitivity about 2 times higher; among six preclinical samples the lines were darker and one sample missed by the commercial strip was detected (DOI: 10.1039/d4ra05277c). Laser-ablated gold nanoparticles, made in water without chemical reductants and conjugated with an in-house monoclonal antibody, detected 10 ng of commercial SARS-CoV-2 spike antigen on a dot-format dipstick, exactly as commercial gold particles did, so the colour result shows no gain over commercial gold; no LOD and no patient samples are reported (DOI: 10.5599/admet.2079). Gold magnetic nanoparticles in a TORCH IgM strip (rubella virus, cytomegalovirus and herpes simplex virus) reported 100% sensitivity and specificity in 162/162 sera and higher sensitivity than commercial colloidal gold strips, with the value not given in the abstract (DOI: 10.3892/ijmm.2015.2333). Gold-oligonucleotide nanoconstructs designed for conserved enterovirus sequences detected 96% of known enteroviruses according to the abstract (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070238).

Other studies match the ligand to the target. An oseltamivir derivative on gold distinguished oseltamivir-resistant (H275Y) influenza virus from wild-type virus: the particles turn from deep red to purple with the resistant neuraminidase, and the lateral flow strip detected the resistant virus down to 5 x 10^2 to 10^3 PFU per test (1 x 10^4 PFU/mL) in virus preparations, also in mixtures with wild-type virus; no colour comparator and no patient samples are reported in the text read (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-018-31311-x). For dengue-1 RNA, dextrin-capped gold carried the reporter probe in a nucleic-acid sandwich strip. The visual cut-off, defined as the lowest concentration with a visible red band, was 0.01 uM of synthetic target after the membrane, reagent volumes and buffer were optimised. With RNA extracted from dengue-1 virus in pooled human sera and amplified by NASBA (nucleic acid sequence-based amplification) the strip gave a cut-off of 1.2 x 10^4 PFU/mL in 20 minutes, so that figure depends on the amplification step. No comparison of dextrin-capped with citrate-capped gold is reported in the text read, and the other dengue serotypes cross-reacted from 0.02 uM (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics9030074). Bacterial protein A and protein G on gold served as flexible detectors for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies and were tested on 105 sera (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-022-03939-2), and a recombinant streptavidin fusion protein was the signal reporter in a hepatitis C virus antibody strip tested on patient sera (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.22701).

**Putting more gold on each captured target.** The most direct way to a stronger line at this level is to attach several particles to one captured molecule. A single-stranded DNA-binding protein (replication protein A domain RPA70A) conjugated to AuNPs lets several particles bind each aptamer. For the influenza A nucleoprotein (and, as a non-viral second target, cardiac troponin I) the line was visible to the naked eye at 0.01 ng/mL in buffer, and the LOD in 10-fold diluted human nasal fluid was 0.26 pg/mL (mean plus 3.3 SD of the test-line intensity). A two-step comparator in the same study, with streptavidin-conjugated AuNPs and a biotinylated aptamer, was visible only at 10 ng/mL, 1000-fold worse (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.9b01827).

**What this level contributes.** The limit addressed here is the number of active particles that reach the line and the activity of what they carry. The clearest like-for-like gains are the 1000-fold of the multi-particle aptamer design, the 2.5-fold of oriented conjugation and the 2-fold of the hollow gold against a commercial strip. The cost is that most gains from conjugation chemistry are hard to see in abstracts, because few of these papers give a value against their own baseline strip.

## 3.1.2 Making more of the captured gold: enlargement and assembly at the line

Once the sandwich has formed, the captured particles can act as seeds. Depositing more metal on them enlarges them, and larger or more irregular particles colour the line more strongly (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229). The most careful comparison is that of Panferov and colleagues, who placed silver enhancement, a new gold-after-silver step based on galvanic replacement, and gold enhancement on one strip format for the SARS-CoV-2 receptor-binding domain, with the same antibodies and the same AuNPs. Gold enhancement gave the lowest LOD, by more than two orders of magnitude over the unenhanced strip, and used stable reagents, but it brought nonspecific colouring and needed an optical instrument to reach the lowest value (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229).

The same idea has been implemented in several practical forms. Growing 20 nm particles into much larger ones after the assay turned most of the conventional strip's false negatives into positives for potato virus X in potato leaf extracts (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-018-3052-7). For the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein in saliva, a gold-enhancement pad carrying Au3+ and hydroxylamine, fixed between the sample and conjugate pads of the ordinary strip, lowered the naked-eye limit from 5.0 to 0.5 ng/mL, a 10-fold gain against the same strip without the pad, and the calibration-curve LOD was 0.10 ng/mL. The antigen was spiked into saliva, and 207 clinical saliva samples gave a sensitivity of 96.10% and a specificity of 99.23% against RT-PCR (positives were Ct 35 or lower). The pad adds no operating step; the authors compare its 10-fold gain with 5- to 488-fold reported for other gold-enhancement strips, which differ in format and target (DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2023.133898). Gold nanoflowers combined with gold staining have been tested for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein in buffer and artificial saliva only (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-024-06502-1). For two sugarcane mosaic-associated viruses, positively charged cysteamine-gold nanoparticles served as a stable colourimetric label, and a further nanogold signal enhancement brought the LOD from a linear range of 10^-6 to 10^-9 g/mL down to 10^-12 g/mL (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-022-07950-6).

A different route builds up the label at the line instead of growing it. Layer-by-layer assembly of AuNPs through biotin-streptavidin binding, driven by electrophoresis, lowered the LOD of an HBsAg strip by more than 60-fold. The design is a two-stage workflow: the conventional strip is read first, and enhancement is applied only to samples that look negative or weakly positive. On 118 clinical samples this raised diagnostic sensitivity against a laboratory chemiluminescent assay without changing specificity, but it needs a small power supply, a holder and an extra reagent step (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202215548). In SARS-CoV-2 antibody strips, a complex of antibody-gold and antigen-gold conjugates increased the test-line signal over antigen-gold alone and brought the visual limit to the cut-off of the reference ELISA (DOI: 10.1002/gch2.202200008).

**What this level contributes.** The limit addressed is the amount of colour per captured sandwich. The gains are real and often large, from 10-fold for the single-step pad to more than two orders of magnitude for gold enhancement and more than 60-fold for layer-by-layer assembly. They are paid for with extra reagents, extra time, more background or dependence on instrument reading (Table A1b), which limits their use where the strip must work without equipment. The alternative is to design a better particle in advance, which is the subject of the next section.

## 3.1.3 Better particles: clusters, dimers, shells and composites

The optical response of a gold particle depends on its size, shape, composition and its distance to neighbouring particles. Several groups use this to give each captured particle more effect on the line, and they do so in four ways.

**Clusters** put several particles at each binding event. The difficulty is plasmon coupling: when particles sit closer than about 10 nm, the absorption shifts towards the near-infrared and the visible colour fades (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Streptavidin-core clusters held about 15 nm apart by biotinylated antibodies, tested on the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, avoid this and keep the red colour; the 40-40 cluster outperformed single particles, whereas the 40-15 cluster did not (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Gold dendrimer nanochains with a red-to-blue shift have also been tested for HIV-1 p24 (DOI: 10.1039/c7tb01394a). Core-satellite SiO2@Au particles, in which a silica core carries many small gold satellites, lowered the LOD by two orders of magnitude against 40 nm gold colloids in the same assay, although only recombinant SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein and inactivated culture virus were tested and the visual limit was much higher than the calculated LOD (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.2c13303).

**Dimers** use the opposite logic. Two particles joined with a sub-nanometre gap couple strongly and show two plasmon peaks, giving a stronger signal than single spheres. For porcine epidemic diarrhea virus they gave a clearly lower naked-eye LOD than a commercial strip, with full agreement with RT-PCR on faecal samples; the authors note that dimers are hard to prepare at scale and that their long-term stability has not been tested (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06013-5).

**Hollow alloy shells** widen the absorption band. Silver-rich Ag-Au nanoshells have two plasmon peaks and a larger integrated absorbance than AuNPs of the same size. As the label in a bovine viral diarrhea virus (BVDV) strip they gave a lower visual LOD than the authors' own AuNP strip with the same antibodies, and good agreement with a commercial ELISA on field samples (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-026-08241-x).

**Composites** combine gold with a second material. In an avian influenza H7 strip, Au/Fe3O4 core-shell particles gave a naked-eye limit of 10^3.5 EID50 against 10^4.5 EID50 for gold particles on the same antibody in the same study, a 10-fold gain; a strip with Fe3O4 alone was invalid, and 200 clinical samples were tested, of which 7 were positive, with no reference method stated in the text read (DOI: 10.1039/c8ra00185e). Magnetic-gold nanostars for the Rift Valley fever virus nucleoprotein add magnetic pre-concentration to a spiked plasmonic shell and gave a very large gain over the authors' own gold-sphere strip in buffer and spiked serum, but they require a magnet and a pre-incubation step and were not tested on patient samples (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.5c21544). Composite particles imaged at two wavelengths for the HIV capsid protein give a large gain but depend on an imager rather than the eye (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114133).

**What this level contributes.** The limit addressed is the optical effect of one captured particle. The strongest evidence comes from papers that compare their particle with a conventional strip made with the same antibodies (clusters, shells, the Au/Fe3O4 composite). Others compare with commercial strips, which mixes the effects of label and antibody (dimers), or were tested only in buffer (nanostars) (Table A1c). All of these designs still read absorbed colour against a white membrane. Section 3.1.7 describes one study that changes that.

## 3.1.4 Gold structures that carry a second signal: what the colour channel gains

A large group of recent studies builds a structured gold label that serves two signals at once, the colour of the line and a second signal (SERS, photothermal or fluorescence) read with an instrument. The headline sensitivity of these papers usually belongs to the second signal. Under the rule of this review only the colour channel is counted, so each study is described by the structure it built and by what its colour channel reports; the second-channel figures are given in the tables, marked ‡, and are not counted. The structures are still informative, because they show which engineering choices change the colour itself.

**Shaped gold.** The strategy is to change the shape of the gold so that each particle absorbs more light, or absorbs at a more useful wavelength.

- A gold nanocrown, a gold shell decorated with external nanospheres, gave an intense dark-blue colour for the SARS-CoV-2 spike S1 protein. Against spherical gold nanospheres in the same study, the line was visible to the naked eye down to 0.1 ng/mL instead of 10 ng/mL, and the colorimetric LOD was 91.24 pg/mL against 5.9 ng/mL, a gain of about 65-fold by calculation (the paper states 100-fold). The SERS limits (57.21 fg/mL in buffer, 39.65 fg/mL in spiked saliva) are not counted, and the antigen was spiked, not taken from patients (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04361).
- Gold nanocages, whose size and core-to-shell gap were tuned (the larger cage GNC-4 was brightest), gave a colorimetric LOD of 1.8 ng/mL for influenza A (H1N1) against 12.5 ng/mL for spherical gold nanoparticles in the same study, a 7-fold gain (the discussion says nearly 10-fold). A test line was visible at 0.005 nM of probe for the cage but not at 0.05 nM for the sphere, and the LOD in spiked saliva was 1.7 ng/mL. The photothermal gain of over 8000-fold (1.51 pg/mL) is not counted (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.4c05384).
- Gold nanorods with a tunable absorption peak detected the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein by eye at 2 ng/mL, and 40 nasal swabs matched nucleic acid detection in 87.5% (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay00347k).
- One-pot Au@Pt nanostars detected antibodies to the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein by eye at 1 ng/mL (10^-6 mg/mL), which the paper puts at about 100 times more sensitive than colloidal gold test strips run in the same study. In serum from 83 vaccinated and 11 unvaccinated volunteers the photothermal channel, which is not counted, found 56 positives and the colloidal gold strips 32, and the unvaccinated were negative by both. The colour count alone is not given, and the dual-mode signal kept 93% of its intensity after 8 weeks (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2024.342241).
- Ag-coated gold nanostars detected influenza A antibody at 0.1 ng/mL by colour (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2024.127351), and Ag@Au nanoplates gave a very low background for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, with the photothermal limit 25-fold lower than the colorimetric one and 35 clinical samples tested (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c05787).
- Gold nanoantennas, the same sub-nanometre-gap gold dimers described in 3.1.3, were read both by eye and by SERS for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus. The naked-eye LOD was 1.0 x 10^2 TCID50/mL, against 5.9 x 10^3 TCID50/mL which the paper states for traditional colloidal gold LFIA (a value quoted, not run in the study), about 59-fold; the SERS limit of 1.0 TCID50/mL is not counted. The antigen was spiked into negative porcine faeces (recoveries 94.9% to 102.1%, RSD 3.3% to 8.1%, n = 6), no cross-reaction with other pathogens was seen and no clinical samples were tested (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2025.117699).
- Gold-silver alloy hollow nanoshells served as a tri-mode label for SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies in a competitive format, in which the blue test line fades as the antibody concentration rises. The naked-eye limit was 160 ng/mL (the colour faded visibly at 0.1 ug/mL); the 20 ng/mL limit belongs to the photothermal and SERS channels, which are not counted and which the paper calls ten times lower than visual reading (the stated values give a factor of 8). Of 107 sera, 79 of 98 vaccinated volunteers showed a significant neutralising effect and none of 9 unvaccinated did; no neutralisation assay was run on the same sera (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2023.341102).

**Silver-gold core-shell particles.** The strategy is to combine the stronger absorption of silver with a gold shell that keeps the particle stable and easy to conjugate.

- Ag(MBA)@Au particles with a gold shell of about 2 nm keep the stability of gold while the silver core adds absorption. In a SARS-CoV-2 IgG strip the test line became visible to the naked eye at 10^-7 mg/mL in buffer and 10^-6 mg/mL in serum, whereas the SERS channel, which is not counted, reached 0.22 and 0.52 pg/mL; the abstract calls this four orders of magnitude lower than visual reading, but the full-text values correspond to about 2.7 and 3.3 orders. In 98 vaccinated volunteers the clear colour band identified 26 strong positives, 6 more weak positives were found only by Raman, and commercial colloidal gold strips run on the same sera found 11 positives, against 34 by commercial ELISA (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c01286).
- The same particles assembled on Fe3O4 gave a visual limit of 10^-8 mg/mL for antibodies to the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein; there is no gold-strip comparator in the same study, and in 98 vaccinated volunteers the visible test line identified 49 strong positives, while 18 weak positives were found only by Raman (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-022-04437-1).
- Au@Ag particles gave a colorimetric LOD of 300 pg/mL for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, against 2.16 pg/mL in the SERS channel that is not counted, with negative accuracy 100% and positive accuracy 85% on 20 patients and 20 healthy subjects (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2025.344149). Core-shell Au@MBA@Ag particles gave visual LODs of 6.25 x 10^2 TCID50/mL for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus and 7.42 x 10^2 copies/uL for porcine rotavirus A in faecal samples, with 93.3% compliance with RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.saa.2025.125863).
- Integrating polydopamine into Au@Ag gave a colour channel twofold better than the same strip with plain Au@Ag tags (colour disappeared below 0.5 ng/mL for H1N1 and 0.05 ng/mL for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein) and, for inactivated virus, visual limits of 3.3 x 10^5 copies/mL (H1N1) and 1.23 x 10^5 copies/mL (SARS-CoV-2), 5-fold and 2-fold better than commercial gold strips. The "2 to more than 750 times" of the abstract mixes these colour gains with the SERS gains (up to 756-fold, not counted). Of 32 positive and 4 negative nasopharyngeal swabs verified by qRT-PCR, the colour band was visible in most positives, and four weak positives (H10, S6, S13, S14) were seen only by SERS (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07587-y).

**Gold combined with other materials.** The strategy is to add a second function (magnetic separation, a porous carrier, a high-loading core) while gold provides the colour.

- Fe3O4-Au heterodimers gave better coloration than ordinary gold nanoparticles in a strip that read the nucleic acids of influenza H1N1, H3N2 and H9N2 on three test lines (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay01010h). Janus Au(shell)-Fe3O4 particles gave naked-eye LODs of 50 ng/mL for both H3N2 influenza and SARS-CoV-2 in a multiplex strip, against 2 and 7 pg/mL for the photothermal channel that is not counted (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2024.117030). Fe3O4-Au(shell) heterodimers for mpox virus gave a 5 ng/mL naked-eye threshold, against 0.04 ng/mL for photothermal readout (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2025.128670).
- High-density gold nanoshells on Fe3O4 nanoclusters (MagAu(shell)) added magnetic enrichment before the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid strip. The naked-eye limit was 1 ng/mL with enrichment and 100 ng/mL without (100-fold, same probe), against 40 ng/mL for a self-made colloidal gold strip (about 40-fold, by calculation); the photothermal limit of 43.64 pg/mL, which the paper calls 1000-fold lower than colloidal gold strips, is not counted. The test used spiked artificial saliva and 18 real samples (10 negative, 5 positive with known Ct, and 3 positive by a commercial strip) (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2023.115688). A Fe3O4/Au/quantum-dot probe modified with wheat germ agglutinin captured viral membrane proteins broadly and gave a visual sensitivity of 0.1 to 0.5 ng/mL for SARS-CoV-2 and mpox virus on 33 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/smll.202406053).
- Silica cores carrying a layer of gold and quantum dots show that a second signal need not improve the colour. For SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG the paper states that the colorimetric ability of the label is equivalent to a traditional gold LFIA (purple bands visible at a 1:10^4 serum dilution), and the 100-fold gain belongs to the fluorescent channel (1:10^6), which is not counted; in 8 early-stage sera the colour bands were hard to see, and the reported sensitivity and specificity of 100% on 16 positive and 41 negative sera rest on fluorescence cut-offs (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.0c03484). For the SARS-CoV-2 S and N proteins the colorimetric limit was 50 pg/mL, 5 times better than a standard AuNP strip, against 2.2 pg/mL by fluorescence, which is 113 times better (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.2c21042). For the mpox virus A29L antigen the colorimetric limit of 0.5 ng/mL equalled that of the conventional AuNP strip, while the fluorescent mode reached 0.0021 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1186/s12951-023-02215-4).
- Layers of 30 nm AuNPs on MoS2 nanosheets gave a visual limit of 0.2 ng/mL for the mpox virus A29L antigen against 1 ng/mL for a standard AuNP strip in the same study, a 5-fold colour gain; the "over 500 times" of the abstract is the SERS limit (0.002 ng/mL) compared with the visual limit of the AuNP strip, and a MoS2@Au tag without the second gold layer had similar visual ability. Real samples were throat swabs from healthy volunteers and lake water spiked with the antigen, not patient samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.jhazmat.2023.132136).
- Rhodamine B-loaded UiO-66 with AuNPs on the outside gave a colorimetric LOD of 31.2 pg/mL for potato virus S against 462.4 pg/mL for a conventional AuNP strip, about 15-fold (by calculation), with 7.6 pg/mL by fluorescence (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2026.1876426). Dual-signal readout nanospheres (RNs@Au) detected Ebola virus glycoprotein by naked eye at 2 ng/mL in 20 minutes, with a quantitative limit of 0.18 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.7b02222). A PDA@Au core-shell nanoprobe for influenza A (H1N1) gave visual sensitivity of 93.9% and specificity of 95.8% on 90 throat swabs, with the SERS and photothermal channels, which are not counted, at 98.5% and 97.0% (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07611-1).

**Surface stabilisation.** Tris-stabilised AuNPs allowed simultaneous visual detection of porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (1.25 x 10^3 TCID50/mL) and porcine rotavirus (3.13 x 10^2 pg/mL), with concordance with RT-PCR of 95% and 100% on clinical samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.saa.2024.124670).

**Raman-tagged gold strips.** In eleven further papers the gold label carries a Raman reporter (ten papers) or a lanthanide (one paper). The abstract headline belongs to SERS or fluorescence, and what the colour channel reports is much less. They are described in two groups.

- *Colour limit reported beside a much lower second-channel limit.* Gold nanostars with a Raman reporter for group A rotavirus gave a naked-eye limit of 1.02 x 10^3 copies/uL (the line was described as faintly visible) against 3.73 x 10^2 copies/uL by SERS; no colour comparator was run, agreement with RT-PCR was 95% (76 of 80 faecal samples from 40 pigs, 20 cattle and 20 poultry; the channel behind this figure is not specified), and stability was tested over 0 to 60 days (DOI: 10.1016/j.saa.2026.127441). Au/DTNB@Ag/DTNB particles for human rotavirus gave a naked-eye limit of 80 pg/mL against 8 pg/mL by SERS (a factor of 10, not counted); in 95 clinical faecal samples the SERS strip agreed 100% with RT-qPCR and with a commercial colloidal gold strip, so the colour channel shows no gain over a commercial gold strip on these samples (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-020-04670-4). The same type of tag for West Nile virus gave a visual limit of 10 ng/mL for recombinant NS1 protein against 0.1 ng/mL by SERS (100-fold, not counted); the visual limit for inactivated virions is not given in the text read, the SERS limit (0.2 x 10^2 copies/uL) matched fluorescence RT-PCR, and no patient samples are reported (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-021-04857-3). For the SARS-CoV-2 spike RBD, gold particles carrying a Raman reporter first served as an ordinary colour strip: a red band was visible above 1 ng/mL and the scanner-quantified colour limit was 1.2 ng/mL (working range 4.2 to 60.4 ng/mL), against 0.1 ng/mL by SERS (not counted) and 0.4 ng/mL by ELISA with the same immunoreagents; the membrane with the highest protein-binding capacity gave the strongest colour, and the matrix was standard solution (DOI: 10.3390/bios11120510). For SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG in patient serum, SERS-active gold nanostars were compared with a conventional strip read by eye: the test line was visible at a serum dilution of 1:100, barely visible at 1:200 and absent at 1:1000, whereas SERS still showed a line at 1:5000; in four patient sera (11 and 13 days and 3.5 and 4 months after infection) the naked eye saw a very faint IgM line only at 11 and 13 days, the 3.5- and 4-month IgG lines were detectable only by SERS, and in a purified system the naked-eye limit was about 1 ug/mL against about 100 fg/mL by SERS (the SERS figures are not counted) (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.1c02305). For HIV-1 DNA captured by hybridisation on a strip (no amplification), DNA-conjugated gold gave a red band visible by eye at 80 pg/mL against 0.24 pg/mL by SERS (not counted); a commercial HIV-1 DNA fluorescence assay kit run in the same study reached 80 pg/mL, and the comparison table of the paper gives 8 pg/mL for SERS where the text gives 0.24 pg/mL, which is not reconciled (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2015.11.099). A strip with two test lines for viral and bacterial DNA markers, in which Kaposi's sarcoma-associated herpesvirus (KSHV) DNA was the viral target (the bacterial marker is not counted), showed a red test line at 100 pM; the line turned light brown at 20 pM and no obvious colour change was seen below that, so the visual limit is about 20 pM against 0.043 pM by SERS (about 460-fold, by calculation, not counted). The comparator was a commercial fluorescence dsDNA kit, not a colour strip, and the sample was synthetic DNA (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.6b03536).
- *Colour compared with, or measured against, a commercial or reference colour strip.* Magnetic Fe3O4@Au nanotags modified with wheat germ agglutinin captured influenza A virus (and Streptococcus pneumoniae, which is not a virus) before the strip. SERS limits were 14 copies/mL for influenza A, stated as about 100 times lower than the visual signal, which implies a visual limit of about 1.4 x 10^3 copies/mL (by calculation; the visual value is not given in the text read). In 80 positive and 30 negative throat swab or sputum samples the SERS accuracy was 100%, 19% higher than a commercial colloidal gold strip (81%); the accuracy of the colour channel of the authors' own strip is not reported (DOI: 10.1016/j.nano.2025.102853). Gold-nanobridged nanogap particles for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein gave a wine-coloured test line visible at 0.1 ng/reaction, whereas 0.5 to 100 pg/reaction was visible only by Raman; applied to a commercial rapid antigen kit, the same particles gave a visual limit of 0.5 ng/reaction for both kits, so the colour channel did not change and the 10-fold gain stated in the paper belongs to SERS (the text gives the commercial limit once as 0.5 and once as 1 ng/reaction; the recombinant protein was used, and patient samples are not reported in the text read) (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07179-w). A digital SERS strip for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein is the one case where the colour channel of the paper's own strip is less sensitive than commercial strips: only the highest protein concentration (0.1 ug/mL) was seen by eye on the authors' strip, whereas three commercial colorimetric tests reached 10^3 to 10^4 pg/mL; for inactivated virus the test line was visible by eye at 1 x 10^5 and 1 x 10^4 TCID50/mL only, against a digital SERS limit of 0.03 TCID50/mL and 0.9 fg/mL for the protein (neither counted); samples were spiked nasal swab extracts (DOI: 10.1002/smsc.202400259). The one lanthanide composite, a cysteamine-modified gold-europium nanocomposite for H5N1 avian influenza (colour and fluorescence), gave a naked-eye limit of 10 HAU/mL against 20 HAU/mL for commercial gold nanoparticles in the same study, a 2-fold colour gain, with 2.5 HAU/mL in the fluorescent mode (5 HAU/mL for commercial europium particles; not counted); the virus was spiked into normal swab specimens, and clinical samples were not tested because too few were available (DOI: 10.3390/ijms23147957).

**What this level contributes.** The limit addressed is the optical effect of one captured particle and, in some designs, the number of particles that reach the line (magnetic enrichment). What the colour channels show is modest. Where a baseline is stated in the same paper, the gains are 2- to 5-fold for polydopamine-integrated Au@Ag, MoS2@Au-Au, the gold-europium composite and SiO2/AuNP/QD (SARS-CoV-2 S and N), 7-fold for nanocages and about 15-fold for RhB/AuNP UiO-66. The Au@Pt nanostar strip is about 100-fold more sensitive than colloidal gold test strips run in the same study, the gold nanoantennas about 59-fold better than a quoted traditional gold LFIA, and the gold nanocrown about 65-fold by LOD (100-fold by visible end-point) against gold nanospheres in the same study; magnetic enrichment added 100-fold on the colour channel. Two designs give no colour gain at all (mpox SiO2-Au/DQD; the Au-NNP in a commercial SARS-CoV-2 kit), and one gives less than commercial strips (the digital SERS strip). None of the ten Raman-tagged papers reports a colour gain against a gold baseline run in the same study. In all 22 papers of this group that were read in full, the headline figure of the abstract belongs to the second channel (SERS, photothermal or fluorescence). The cost is a multi-step synthesis of the particle and an instrument for the second channel, which is where most of the sensitivity of these labels lies.

## 3.1.5 Replacing the binder and choosing the antigen

A brighter label cannot help if the binder is weak, and a parallel line of work therefore replaces the conventional antibody with a binder that is smaller, more stable or easier to produce. The studies fall into four groups.

**Nanobodies** are small single-domain fragments. In a SARS-CoV-2 spike S1 strip, a nanobody-gold detector with an ACE2-gold capture probe gave an LOD for gamma-irradiated virus of 0.4 x 10^4 copies/mL against 1.6 x 10^4 for a monoclonal-antibody version of the same strip, a 4-fold gain. In 320 saliva samples (180 RT-PCR-positive) the nanobody strip had a sensitivity of 97.14% and a specificity of 98.57% at Ct of 30 or below, against 90.04% and 97.86% for the antibody strip (Table 3 of the paper); across all 180 positives the values were 91.66% and 83.33% (Table 2). The 83.33% quoted in the results text for the Ct <= 30 group is the all-positive value (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-37347-y). Other nanobody designs, including multivalent and Y-shaped assemblies, have been reported for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid and spike S1 proteins and for the chikungunya virus E protein, and a nanobody strip has been used on human norovirus outbreak specimens (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.4c00509; DOI: 10.1039/d4nr02762k; DOI: 10.1039/d5nr02568k; DOI: 10.3390/bios16020132; DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2025.1707358; DOI: 10.1128/msphere.00219-16). Yeast cell-wall nanofragments that display anti-spike nanobodies were used as a universal reagent on four platforms, one of them a colorimetric strip for SARS-CoV-2 in which the nanofragment-nanobody conjugate was linked to gold nanoparticles through the SpyTag/SpyCatcher system and gave a red test line. The strip showed a concentration-dependent trend but failed below 2 x 10^4 virions/mL. No colour comparator was run: the two commercial strips (Abbott and SD Biosensor) served as controls for the SERS platform of the paper, where they missed three of the positive clinical samples that SERS found, and the paper notes that strip signals did not follow the Ct values consistently. Only one Omicron and one healthy clinical sample were tested on the nanofragment strip (DOI: 10.1038/s41565-023-01415-1).

**Aptamers** are chemically synthesised and can be produced more reproducibly and cheaply than antibodies (DOI: 10.3390/molecules29081776). A competitive aptamer strip for SARS-CoV-2 spike protein on food packaging used an aptamer chosen for broad binding to variant spike proteins, but its sensitivity was lower than that of other methods and it was tested only with recombinant protein (DOI: 10.3390/molecules29081776). Other aptamer strips for SARS-CoV-2 (spike protein, inactivated virus and nucleocapsid protein) were evaluated with inactivated virus or spiked samples (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202107730; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c00554; DOI: 10.1186/s12951-023-02191-9). A pair of sandwich aptamers detected whole avian influenza H5N2 particles with enhanced specificity over other H5Nx viruses (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2019.03.061); an aptamer-based isolation step was combined with a strip for grouper nervous necrosis virus (5 ng/mL coat protein) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2020.00886); and a nanogold-and-aptamer proof of concept was proposed for at-home detection of SARS-CoV-2 mutants (DOI: 10.1016/j.nano.2022.102590). Two further aptamer papers, one selecting an H3N2 aptamer by virus-based SELEX and one developing a DNA aptamer for the Omicron receptor-binding domain for multiplexed detection and pseudovirus neutralisation, describe no strip data in their abstracts and are listed in the table only (DOI: 10.1166/jbn.2019.2789; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c01993).

**Synthetic binders and ligands.** A hydrogel nanoparticle acting as an abiotic antibody mimic on a gold core, built from an aromatic amino-acid polymer shell grown on gold, gave a visual limit of 30 ng/reaction of spike trimer in a direct format (a clear test line in at least 95% of replicates) and detected pseudovirus at 1 x 10^5 TU/mL in saliva in a sandwich format with no significant matrix effect. No antibody comparator was run; the paper compares its direct-format limit with published sandwich strips (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2025.129324). Further binders include sialic-acid glyconanoparticles that recognise spike through glycans, a lectin for severe dengue virus infection and a miniprotein binder aimed at SARS-CoV-2 variants of concern; their abstracts give no accuracy data (DOI: 10.1021/acscentsci.0c00855; DOI: 10.1080/15321819.2023.2260480; DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06113-2) (Table A1d).

**Antibody pairs and antigens.** Two further parts of the strip decide what the particle can capture: the pair of antibodies, and the antigen used in serology strips. For the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, two antibody pairs (NP-mAb-7 and -40) reached 8 pg of nucleocapsid protein and 625 TCID50/mL of virus on 60 clinical samples (DOI: 10.3390/ijms222212412), and a detection antibody aimed at a linear epitope reached 10 pg/mL (DOI: 10.3390/ijms25084436). One study compared the nucleocapsid and the spike as targets for strips (DOI: 10.1016/j.molimm.2021.01.005). In serology strips the antigen can be the variable: an acid-treated p72 antigen on gold detected African swine fever virus antibodies at a 1:10,000 serum dilution (DOI: 10.3389/fchem.2021.804981); the protrusion domain of the infectious bursal disease virus VP2 protein was used as a highly immunogenic diagnostic antigen (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-020-10458-6); plant-produced receptor-binding domain gave a sensitivity of 94.1% and a specificity of 98% on 51 sera (DOI: 10.3877/cma.j.issn.2096-112x.2021.01.006); and baculovirus-expressed nucleocapsid and receptor-binding domain were tested on 103 convalescent sera (DOI: 10.1007/s10529-022-03316-0). The shell domain of norovirus was chosen as the antigen for a broad-range strip tested on 122 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1186/s12866-020-02084-z).

**What this level contributes.** In principle, binder replacement is promising for supply independence and for coping with sequence variation. In this group the abstract usually gives a detection limit or a specificity statement, not a like-for-like comparison with an antibody version of the same strip; the exception is the nanobody strip, with its 4-fold gain (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-37347-y). The evidence is therefore mostly analytical.

## 3.1.6 Assay format: what the line is able to show

Even with a good label, the format of the immunoassay limits what the colour can mean. In a sandwich strip, too much antigen can suppress the line (the hook effect). A study of lumpy skin disease virus investigated this effect directly, reporting a detection limit of 10^3.4 TCID50/mL, a coefficient of variation below 5.3% and no cross-reaction with 12 viruses (DOI: 10.3390/bios12090739). In competitive formats the line gets weaker as the target increases, which suits small analytes and functional assays: a competitive gold strip for SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies gave 20.66 IU/L with 100% specificity (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.2c03677), and a biomimetic mock-virus particle with trimeric SARS-CoV-2 spike measured neutralising antibody at 59 IU/mL, benchmarked to the WHO standard (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04372). Quantitative colloidal-gold assays have also been used to compare kits: one measured the detection limit of nine approved SARS-CoV-2 antigen kits against the nucleocapsid protein and found nucleic-acid testing at least 1835 times more sensitive than antigen testing on a Delta strain (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2023.124462). A Japanese encephalitis virus NS1 strip read with a smartphone colorimeter reached 10 pg/mL by eye in spiked serum and was stable for one month at 4 C (DOI: 10.1039/d2na00463a). Values are in Table A1f.

**What this level contributes.** The limit addressed is interpretation: whether the colour of the line has a one-to-one relation with the amount of target. The cost is that each format has a different dynamic range, which most of these abstracts do not state.

## 3.1.7 Changing the optics: plasmonic scattering as a boundary case

All the strategies above increase absorbed colour. One study instead removes the white background that hides a different optical signal. When the nitrocellulose membrane is made transparent by refractive-index matching and placed over a black backing card, large AuNPs appear as orange lines by scattering rather than absorption and the background almost disappears. The study targeted the influenza A virus nucleoprotein; its authors report a very large gain against their own conventional strip and against commercial strips, a higher sensitivity on a small set of clinical swabs and a naked-eye or smartphone readout (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). The approach needs an extra reagent step and sufficient light, and the authors place it beyond absorbance-based colorimetric detection. It is therefore kept as a flagged boundary case and its figures are reported separately from those of absorption-based strips (Table A1c).

## 3.1.8 Conventional gold strips without engineering

Many included records describe new colloidal-gold strips that apply none of the steps above. They serve as the baseline: they show what the unmodified format achieves, and they are the strips that the engineering papers set out to improve. Their abstracts rarely give a within-paper change, so they are summarised by target here, with values in Table A1e.

**SARS-CoV-2.** IgM and IgG strips reported sensitivity and specificity against patient sera: sensitivity 100% and specificity 93.3% (kappa 0.872) for IgM (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.0c01554), sensitivity 90% and specificity 96.6% against real-time PCR on 60 samples (DOI: 10.1089/mab.2021.0027), and others without values in the abstract (DOI: 10.1039/d0an00629g; DOI: 10.1097/cm9.0000000000000922). The colloidal-gold assays in Table A1e add 158/170 positives with an AUC of 0.958 (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.23619) and an IgM sensitivity of 90.67% (DOI: 10.1002/ctm2.90). A nasal secretory IgA strip detected spike-specific IgA purified from people infected with BA.5 (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2024.1386891). For SARS-CoV-2 antigen, a spike strip detected 62.5 ng/mL in a subunit vaccine (DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2021.635677); a nucleocapsid kit detected more than 0.2 ng/mL recombinant protein without cross-reaction (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2021.114183); a gold-nanoparticle receptor-binding-domain strip detected 1 ng/mL with a sensitivity of 94.3% and a specificity of 90.9% against RT-PCR on 100 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.28416); a nanogold test reached a sensitivity of 95% and a specificity of 98% on clinical specimens (DOI: 10.3390/nano12142477); another detected 2 ng/mL with 98% concordance (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07299-3); and the CoV-SCAN strip detected 10 variants at 1.6 to 12.5 TCID50/swab (DOI: 10.1016/j.jcvp.2022.100080). A nucleic-acid sandwich strip for the N3 gene reached 5 pM (DOI: 10.1016/j.jbiosc.2022.11.001).

**Other human viruses.** An HIV-1 p24 strip with in-house monoclonal antibodies reached 25 pg/mL and detected 10/10 positives with a specificity of 98.03% (DOI: 10.1155/2016/6743904); a strip for severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (SFTSV) reached 1 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.8b02366); a dengue competitive strip with the 4G2 antibody reached 5.12 x 10^2 PFU (DOI: 10.3390/bios12070495); a varicella-zoster strip reached 30 ng/mL of gE without cross-reactivity with EV71, HSV-1 or HSV-2 (DOI: 10.1016/j.virol.2023.07.008); and an HTLV-I IgG strip showed a sensitivity of 96.7% and a specificity of 100% (DOI: 10.3390/v15010129).

**Animal and aquatic viruses.** A feline coronavirus antibody strip was tested on plasma, serum, whole blood and ascitic fluid (DOI: 10.1007/978-1-4939-2438-7_3); a nervous necrosis virus strip detected whole virions in fish tissue (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-020-63553-z); porcine circovirus 2 was detected at 10^3.18 TCID50/mL (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.01953-22) and pseudorabies virus at 1.336 x 10^3 copies/uL (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2024.1399123). A canine parvovirus strip on faecal samples reached a sensitivity of 71% and a specificity of 92% against haemagglutination (DOI: 10.1007/s00705-018-3846-2), and two further canine parvovirus strips, one in colloidal gold and fluorescent format on 60 clinical samples and one in nanogold with a sensitivity of 96.4% and a specificity of 88.2% against qPCR, have been described (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-023-12604-2; DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-55548-x).

These records show how widely the format has been adapted, but they say little about signal improvement, because no engineered comparison strip is run beside them. A strip is listed in this group when its abstract describes no deliberate change to the label, binder or readout.

## 3.1.9 What the evidence supports

Read together, the studies of this part tell one consistent story about the colour of a gold strip. It is made stronger in three ways: by making the captured gold more visible after capture (enlargement, layer assembly, an amplifier complex), by a better particle (clusters, dimers, shells, shaped gold) and, in a few designs, by delivering more particles to the line (magnetic enrichment). The best-supported comparisons are those in which the same paper runs its own conventional strip (Table A4). Within those papers the reported gain in the colour signal ranges from about 2-fold to about 1000-fold. The high end belongs to designs with extra hardware or reagents (a magnet, an electrode set, an imager, a multi-particle aptamer system with a two-step comparator), whereas shaped or composite gold labels give gains of about 5- to 15-fold in the colour channel.

A second finding is that the structured gold labels of 3.1.4 owe most of their headline sensitivity to a second signal, so the gain in colour alone is much smaller than the title of such a paper suggests. Among the shaped labels read in full, the colour gain ranged from none to about 100-fold: nanostars about 100-fold, nanocrowns about 65-fold, nanoantennas about 59-fold against a quoted value and nanocages 7-fold gave the largest colour gains; silver-gold or composite labels gave 2- to 5-fold, or none; and the figure quoted in the abstract belonged to SERS, photothermal or fluorescence.

Conjugation chemistry, binder replacement, antigen design and assay format address other limits (the activity of the antibody, supply, variants, interpretation of the line), and their abstracts seldom state a change in the colour signal against a baseline.

Three limitations apply across the part. First, many reported LODs come from scanners, imagers or readers rather than the naked eye, so they describe instrument-assisted performance. Second, clinical evaluation is the exception among engineering papers: several tested only spiked buffer, saliva or recombinant protein, and the reference method varies (ELISA, RT-PCR, gel-read RT-PCR). Third, comparators differ (own conventional strip, same-size particles, commercial strip, a quoted literature value), so fold-changes cannot be ranked. Reporting quality of the papers read in full is given in Table A3. Two animal-virus strips (DOI: 10.1039/d4an00180j; DOI: 10.1039/d2an02043b) are still known from abstracts only, and the supporting information of the papers read in full has not been examined.
