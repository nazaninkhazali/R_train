# Engineering the colorimetric signal of lateral flow assays for virus detection

*Working text assembled 2026-11-21 from Sections 1 to 4. The article body has no introduction, abstract or conclusion yet; Tables A1a to D4 are supplementary and follow the text.*

## 1. Gold nanoparticle labels: engineering the colorimetric signal

### 1.1 Where the colour of a strip comes from, and where it is lost

A colorimetric lateral flow immunoassay (LFIA) gives its answer as a coloured line. That line appears when the labelled immune complex is captured at the test line and the label absorbs enough visible light to stand out from the white nitrocellulose membrane. Its intensity is therefore set by three quantities: how many labelled particles reach the line and are held there, how much light each captured particle removes, and how clearly the result can be distinguished from the background by the eye or by a reader. Gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) became the standard label because they absorb visible light strongly, are simple to synthesise and conjugate readily to antibodies (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Their weakness follows from the same physics: when only a few particles are captured, a red line made of a few particles is too faint to see, and the limit of detection (LOD) of a gold strip is comparatively high (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229).

Each of the three quantities offers a lever, and the literature on gold labels reads as a sequence of attempts to pull them in turn. The first attempts work on the standard particle itself, its size and its surface, so that every particle that reaches the line is an active one (Section 1.2). When that is not enough, the captured gold can be made larger after the assay has run (Section 1.3). Rather than enlarging gold after capture, a third group designs particles that are optically stronger from the outset, by assembling, alloying or combining them with other materials (Section 1.4), and a fourth, much larger group shapes them into stars, cages and crowns, often to carry a second, instrumental signal as well (Section 1.5). The particle is only half of the probe, so the binder and the antigen it carries come next (Section 1.6), followed by the format of the assay (Section 1.7) and, in one study, the optics of the membrane itself (Section 1.8). Conventional colloidal gold strips that apply none of these changes form the baseline (Section 1.9), and Section 1.10 states what the evidence supports. The sections that follow leave gold behind in three steps. Section 2 asks what changes when the carrier is not gold, Section 3 what changes when the label is left alone and a second source of colour is added after capture, and Section 4 what changes when the strip, the sample and the reaction around the label are redesigned.

Three rules hold throughout the article. A change is treated as an improvement only when the same study runs a conventional or unmodified strip beside it, because fold-changes from different studies rest on different baselines, matrices and readouts and cannot be ranked. Where a label also provides a surface-enhanced Raman scattering (SERS), photothermal or fluorescence readout, only the colour channel is considered; the second-channel figures are listed in Tables A1g and A4 for reference. And the target virus is named for every method, with the numerical details of every study in Tables A1a to A1g.

### 1.2 The standard particle: size, surface and what it carries

The first lever is the most obvious one: a bigger particle carries more colour. It also migrates more slowly through the membrane and may not reach the test line in sufficient numbers, so size is a compromise rather than a quantity to maximise (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229; DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). Two studies located that compromise experimentally. For the hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), particles were grown stepwise from a seed to diameters between 34.2 and 137.8 nm; those of 42.7 nm gave the strongest test line at 10, 100 and 500 ng/mL of antigen and, in human whole blood spiked with 500 ng/mL HBsAg, a clearer line than particles made by the Turkevich-Frens method or a commercial HBV kit, although no LOD value is reported (DOI: 10.3390/s16122154). For dengue virus NS1, 40 nm colloidal gold, 80 nm gold nanospheres and 150 nm gold nanoshells were compared with the same antibody: the 80 nm particles gave the most distinct and consistent lines, while the 150 nm shells produced false-positive signals in every buffer tested (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics16030395). Both point to an optimum between 40 and 80 nm, not to the largest particle that can be made.

A particle of the right size is still wasted if the antibody on it cannot bind. Random adsorption leaves part of the antibodies with their binding sites turned towards the gold, and oriented coupling corrects this. On 100 nm AuNPs for HBsAg, a thiolated hydrazide ligand attached the antibody through its Fc region so that the antigen-binding sites faced outward. The same performance then required about seven times less antibody (0.2 against 1.5 mg per pmol of particles), conjugation took 10 minutes instead of 90, and the LOD fell from 5 to 2 ng/mL relative to carbodiimide coupling in the same study; with a portable strip reader, 35 positive and 15 negative sera agreed with a chemiluminescence immunoassay (R2 = 0.9527) without false positives (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2021.338623).

The surface that holds the antibody also meets the sample, and here the gold can work for or against the assay. In a colloidal gold strip for SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG, plasma anticoagulated with EDTA-K2 gave stronger lines and more IgM-positive results than serum from the same 112 patients: 17 weak and 15 clear IgM positives in plasma were negative in serum, and 40 IgG samples that were merely positive in serum read as strongly positive in plasma. The authors attribute this to chelation and aggregation of the colloidal gold by EDTA-K2, an effect that sodium citrate and lithium heparin did not produce; no false positives arose in 200 healthy controls, and EDTA-K2 plasma agreed best with the chemiluminescence reference (DOI: 10.2147/ijn.s281594). The protein layer that adsorbs onto a probe in biological fluids, the protein corona, has an equally strong influence, and three studies traced it in NS1 assays. For Zika virus NS1 on gold nanostars, strips run in buffer or bovine serum albumin showed false-positive lines that disappeared in human serum or on serum-pretreated nitrocellulose, and a preformed corona restored the specificity of antibody-antigen binding (LOD 1.6 nM, effective dissociation constant 12.7 nM in serum) (DOI: 10.1021/acs.bioconjchem.6b00523). For dengue virus serotype 2 NS1, the identity of the corona protein decided the outcome: a preformed apolipoprotein A1 corona gave an LOD of 1.24 nM, whereas an albumin corona lowered the test-line intensity by 33% at a given antigen concentration, so that no meaningful LOD could be determined (DOI: 10.3390/pharmaceutics14112439). A systematic comparison of probe chemistries, with mouse IgG, Zika NS1 and dengue NS1 as model pairs in albumin and serum, then showed why: physisorbed, PEG-backfilled probes aggregated and lost signal as antigen rose, covalently grafted probes (NHS, hydrazide) stayed stable although the NHS probes performed worst in serum, and sonication or a single pre-wash restored the signal (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.0c08628). With an earlier comparison of conjugation strategies for dengue NS1 (DOI: 10.1080/15321819.2016.1269338), these mechanistic studies explain why nominally identical strips behave differently in different matrices, even though they report few diagnostic data (Table A1a).

Once size and surface are controlled, the particle itself can be varied. Hollow gold nanoparticles have a molar extinction coefficient (1.48 x 10^10 M^-1 cm^-1) that the authors report to be much larger than that of solid particles of the same size; as a label for HIV-1 p24 antigen they gave darker blue lines and higher optical density in serum than the commercial Alere HIV-1/2 Ag/Ab Combo test (LOD 0.02 ng/mL), a sensitivity about twice as high, and detected one of six preclinical samples that the commercial strip missed (DOI: 10.1039/d4ra05277c). The method of synthesis, by contrast, did not matter: laser-ablated gold, made in water without chemical reductants, detected 10 ng of SARS-CoV-2 spike antigen on a dot-format dipstick, exactly as commercial gold conjugated with the same antibody did (DOI: 10.5599/admet.2079). Gold magnetic nanoparticles in a TORCH IgM strip (rubella virus, cytomegalovirus and herpes simplex virus) gave 100% sensitivity and specificity in 162 sera and are reported to be more sensitive than commercial colloidal gold strips, without a stated value (DOI: 10.3892/ijmm.2015.2333), and gold-oligonucleotide nanoconstructs designed for conserved enterovirus sequences detected 96% of known enteroviruses (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070238).

The molecule on the gold need not be an antibody at all, and some of the most inventive probes recognise what a virus does rather than what it looks like. An oseltamivir derivative immobilised on gold binds the neuraminidase of oseltamivir-resistant (H275Y) influenza virus much more strongly than that of the wild type; the particles turn from deep red to purple in the presence of the resistant enzyme, and a strip built on them detected the resistant virus at 5 x 10^2 to 10^3 PFU per test (1 x 10^4 PFU/mL), also in mixtures with wild-type virus (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-018-31311-x). For dengue-1 RNA, dextrin-capped gold carried a DNA reporter probe in a sandwich hybridisation strip; the visual cut-off, the lowest concentration giving a visible red band, was 0.01 uM of synthetic target, and viral RNA from pooled human sera, amplified by nucleic acid sequence-based amplification (NASBA), was detected at 1.2 x 10^4 PFU/mL in 20 minutes, a figure that depends on the amplification step (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics9030074). Bacterial protein A and protein G served as flexible detectors for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies in 105 sera (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-022-03939-2), and a recombinant streptavidin fusion protein served as the reporter in a hepatitis C virus antibody strip (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.22701).

All of these changes improve the particle that is captured. The boldest change at this level instead increases how many particles each captured target brings with it. The RPA70A domain of replication protein A, a single-stranded DNA-binding protein, was conjugated to AuNPs so that several particles decorate the single-stranded region of one aptamer. For the influenza A nucleoprotein the line was visible to the naked eye at 0.01 ng/mL in buffer, the LOD in tenfold-diluted human nasal fluid was 0.26 pg/mL, and a conventional two-step design with streptavidin-conjugated AuNPs and a biotinylated aptamer, run in the same study, was visible only at 10 ng/mL, a thousandfold difference (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.9b01827).

The limit addressed at this level is therefore the number of active particles at the line. Where a like-for-like comparison exists, the gains are the thousandfold of the multi-particle aptamer design, the 2.5-fold of oriented conjugation and the twofold of hollow gold against a commercial strip; for most other surface modifications the studies explain behaviour rather than quantify gain. A well-made particle still meets a ceiling, and the next group of studies goes past it by enlarging the gold after it has been captured.

### 1.3 Making more of the captured gold

Once the immune complex has formed, every captured particle is a potential seed. Depositing more metal on it enlarges it or roughens it, and a larger particle absorbs more light per captured event (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229). The most careful comparison of the available chemistries placed silver enhancement, a gold-after-silver step based on galvanic replacement, and direct gold enhancement on one strip format for the SARS-CoV-2 receptor-binding domain, with the same antibodies and the same AuNPs. Gold enhancement won, lowering the LOD by more than two orders of magnitude below the unenhanced strip with stable reagents, but it introduced nonspecific colouring and needed an optical instrument to reach the lowest value (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229).

The rest of this subsection is, in effect, a series of attempts to keep that gain while removing its cost. Growing 20 nm particles into much larger ones after the assay turned most of the false negatives of a conventional strip for potato virus X into positives in potato leaf extracts, but as a separate post-assay step (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-018-3052-7). For the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, the enhancement reagents (Au3+ and hydroxylamine) were dried onto an additional pad between the sample and conjugate pads, so that enlargement happens during the normal run with no extra operation. The naked-eye limit fell from 5.0 to 0.5 ng/mL relative to the same strip without the pad, the calibration-curve LOD was 0.10 ng/mL, and in 207 clinical saliva samples the strip reached a sensitivity of 96.10% and a specificity of 99.23% against RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2023.133898). The same idea appears with gold nanoflowers plus gold staining for the nucleocapsid protein in buffer and artificial saliva (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-024-06502-1), and, for two sugarcane mosaic-associated viruses, with a positively charged cysteamine-gold label followed by nanogold enhancement, which extended detection from a linear range of 10^-6 to 10^-9 g/mL down to 10^-12 g/mL (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-022-07950-6).

Enlarging each particle is one way to add colour at the line; adding more particles is another. Layer-by-layer deposition of AuNPs through biotin-streptavidin binding, driven by electrophoresis, lowered the LOD of an HBsAg strip more than 60-fold. Its designers accepted the extra hardware and limited its use: the conventional strip is read first, and enhancement is applied only to samples that look negative or weakly positive. In 118 clinical samples this raised diagnostic sensitivity against a laboratory chemiluminescence assay without loss of specificity, at the cost of a small power supply, a holder and an additional reagent step (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202215548). In SARS-CoV-2 serology, a complex of antibody-gold and antigen-gold conjugates achieved a similar effect chemically, increasing the test-line signal over antigen-gold alone and bringing the visual limit to the cut-off of the reference ELISA (DOI: 10.1002/gch2.202200008).

The gains at this level are consistently large, from tenfold for the single-step pad to more than two orders of magnitude for gold enhancement, and they are achieved with the same antibodies and particles as the baseline strip. Their price is reagents, time or background and, for the lowest values, an instrument (Table A1b). This is what motivates the alternative: rather than enlarging gold after capture, design a particle that is brighter from the start.

### 1.4 Brighter by design: clusters, dimers, shells and composites

The optical response of gold depends on the size, shape and composition of a particle and on the distance to its neighbours. Each of these parameters has been turned into a design strategy, and the studies are easiest to follow as variations on one question: how close should gold sit to other gold?

Clusters answer it by keeping particles apart. Placing several particles at each binding event multiplies the colour, but particles closer than about 10 nm couple plasmonically, their absorption shifts towards the near-infrared, and the visible colour fades. Clusters built around a streptavidin core, with biotinylated antibodies holding the particles about 15 nm apart, avoided this coupling and kept the red colour; for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein a cluster of 40 nm particles outperformed single particles, while a mixed cluster of 40 and 15 nm particles did not (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Gold dendrimer nanochains, which show a red-to-blue shift, have been tested for HIV-1 p24 (DOI: 10.1039/c7tb01394a). Core-satellite particles, in which a silica core carries many small gold satellites, lowered the LOD by two orders of magnitude relative to 40 nm colloidal gold in the same assay for recombinant SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein and inactivated virus, although the visual limit remained much higher than the calculated LOD (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.2c13303).

Dimers answer the same question the opposite way, by bringing gold as close as possible. Two particles joined with a sub-nanometre gap couple strongly, show two plasmon peaks and give a stronger signal than single spheres. For porcine epidemic diarrhea virus such dimers gave a clearly lower naked-eye LOD than a commercial strip and full agreement with RT-PCR on faecal samples, though the authors note that dimers are hard to prepare at scale and that their long-term stability is unknown (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06013-5).

Hollow alloy shells change composition instead of spacing. Silver-rich Ag-Au nanoshells show two plasmon peaks and a larger integrated absorbance than AuNPs of the same size, and in a bovine viral diarrhea virus strip they gave a lower visual LOD than the authors' own AuNP strip with the same antibodies, with good agreement with a commercial ELISA on field samples (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-026-08241-x).

Composites, finally, add a second material that brings a function gold lacks, most often magnetism. In an avian influenza H7 strip, Au/Fe3O4 core-shell particles gave a naked-eye limit of 10^3.5 EID50, ten times lower than gold particles carrying the same antibody in the same study, while Fe3O4 alone did not produce a valid strip; 200 clinical samples were tested, of which 7 were positive (DOI: 10.1039/c8ra00185e). Magnetic-gold nanostars for the Rift Valley fever virus nucleoprotein combined a spiked plasmonic shell with magnetic pre-concentration and gave a very large gain over the authors' own gold-sphere strip in buffer and spiked serum, at the cost of a magnet and a pre-incubation step and without testing on patient samples (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.5c21544). Composite plasmonic particles imaged at two wavelengths for the HIV capsid protein gave a large gain that depends on an imager rather than on the eye (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114133).

The strongest evidence at this level comes from studies that compare the new particle with a conventional strip made with the same antibodies, as for the clusters, the shells and the Au/Fe3O4 composite. Comparisons with commercial strips, as for the dimers, mix the effect of the label with that of the antibodies, and some particles have been tested only in buffer (Table A1c). The next group of studies takes shape and composition much further, and in doing so adds a second, instrumental signal to the colour.

### 1.5 Shaped gold and the second signal: what the colour gains

A large and recent body of work builds structured gold labels that provide two signals at once: the colour of the line, read by eye, and a SERS, photothermal or fluorescence signal, read with an instrument. The headline sensitivity of these papers almost always belongs to the instrumental channel. For the purpose of this review they are valuable for another reason: most of them report the naked-eye limit of the same strip, and many run a conventional gold strip beside it, so together they form the largest controlled experiment available on which structural changes to gold improve its colour. The second-channel figures are in Table A1g and are not repeated here.

The answer they give is clear: shape matters most. A gold nanocrown, a gold shell decorated with external nanospheres, gave an intense dark-blue line for the SARS-CoV-2 spike S1 protein. Against gold nanospheres in the same study the line was visible by eye at 0.1 ng/mL instead of 10 ng/mL, and the colorimetric LOD was 91.24 pg/mL against 5.9 ng/mL, about 65-fold by calculation (the paper states 100-fold); the antigen was spiked into buffer and saliva (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04361). Gold nanocages showed that geometry can be tuned systematically: the size and core-to-shell gap were varied, the largest cage was the brightest, and its colorimetric LOD for the influenza A (H1N1) nucleoprotein was 1.8 ng/mL against 12.5 ng/mL for gold spheres, a sevenfold gain, with 1.7 ng/mL in spiked saliva (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.4c05384). One-pot Au@Pt nanostars detected antibodies to the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein by eye at 1 ng/mL, about 100 times lower than colloidal gold strips run in the same study, and kept 93% of their signal after eight weeks; in sera from 83 vaccinated and 11 unvaccinated volunteers, however, the positive counts are reported for the photothermal channel (56 positives, against 32 for the colloidal gold strips), not for colour alone (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2024.342241). The sub-nanometre-gap dimers of Section 1.4 reappear here as gold nanoantennas carrying a Raman reporter: for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus spiked into negative faeces (recoveries 94.9% to 102.1%, n = 6) the naked-eye LOD was 1.0 x 10^2 TCID50/mL, about 59-fold below the 5.9 x 10^3 TCID50/mL that the paper quotes for traditional colloidal gold strips, a baseline cited rather than measured, and no clinical samples were tested (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2025.117699). Other shapes extend the series without a stated baseline: gold nanorods with a tunable absorption peak detected the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein by eye at 2 ng/mL, with 87.5% agreement with nucleic acid testing in 40 nasal swabs (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay00347k); Ag-coated gold nanostars detected influenza A antibody at 0.1 ng/mL by colour (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2024.127351); Ag@Au nanoplates gave a very low background for the nucleocapsid protein in 35 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c05787); and gold-silver alloy hollow nanoshells, used in a competitive format in which the blue line fades as SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibody rises, gave a naked-eye limit of 160 ng/mL, with a neutralising effect, among 107 sera, in 79 of 98 vaccinated and none of 9 unvaccinated volunteers, though no neutralisation assay was run on the same sera (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2023.341102).

Alloying is the second structural route, and it gives smaller gains. Silver absorbs more strongly than gold, and a thin gold shell keeps a silver core stable and easy to conjugate. Ag(MBA)@Au particles with a gold shell about 2 nm thick gave a SARS-CoV-2 IgG line visible by eye at 10^-7 mg/mL in buffer and 10^-6 mg/mL in serum, and in 98 vaccinated volunteers the clear colour band identified 26 strong positives, against 11 positives for commercial colloidal gold strips on the same sera and 34 for ELISA (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c01286). The same particles assembled on Fe3O4 reached a visual limit of 10^-8 mg/mL for antibodies to the nucleocapsid protein and found 49 strong positives among 98 vaccinated volunteers, without a gold-strip comparator (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-022-04437-1). Au@Ag particles gave a colorimetric LOD of 300 pg/mL for the nucleocapsid protein, with 100% negative and 85% positive accuracy in 20 patients and 20 healthy subjects (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2025.344149), and Au@MBA@Ag particles gave visual LODs of 6.25 x 10^2 TCID50/mL for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus and 7.42 x 10^2 copies/uL for porcine rotavirus A in faeces, with 93.3% agreement with RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.saa.2025.125863). Where a baseline was run, the gain was modest: integrating polydopamine into Au@Ag doubled the colour sensitivity relative to plain Au@Ag tags and gave visual limits of 3.3 x 10^5 copies/mL for inactivated H1N1 and 1.23 x 10^5 copies/mL for SARS-CoV-2, five- and twofold better than commercial gold strips, with most positives among 32 nasopharyngeal swabs visible as a colour band and four weak positives seen only by SERS (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07587-y).

The third route returns to the composites of Section 1.4, and here the most effective addition turns out to act on the number of particles rather than on the particle. High-density gold nanoshells on Fe3O4 nanoclusters allowed the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein to be enriched magnetically before the strip, and the naked-eye limit fell from 100 ng/mL without enrichment to 1 ng/mL with it, against 40 ng/mL for a self-made colloidal gold strip; spiked artificial saliva and 18 real samples were tested (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2023.115688). A Fe3O4/Au/quantum-dot probe carrying wheat germ agglutinin, a lectin that binds viral membrane glycoproteins broadly, gave a visual sensitivity of 0.1 to 0.5 ng/mL for SARS-CoV-2 and mpox virus in 33 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/smll.202406053). Fe3O4-Au heterodimers gave better coloration than ordinary AuNPs in a three-line nucleic acid strip for influenza H1N1, H3N2 and H9N2 (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay01010h); Janus Au(shell)-Fe3O4 particles gave naked-eye LODs of 50 ng/mL for both H3N2 influenza and SARS-CoV-2 in a multiplex strip (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2024.117030); and Fe3O4-Au(shell) heterodimers for mpox virus gave a 5 ng/mL naked-eye threshold (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2025.128670). Non-magnetic supports work through surface area: 30 nm AuNPs layered on MoS2 nanosheets gave a visual limit of 0.2 ng/mL for the mpox virus A29L antigen against 1 ng/mL for a standard AuNP strip, a fivefold colour gain, in spiked throat swabs and lake water (DOI: 10.1016/j.jhazmat.2023.132136); rhodamine B-loaded UiO-66 with AuNPs on its surface gave a colorimetric LOD of 31.2 pg/mL for potato virus S against 462.4 pg/mL for a conventional AuNP strip, about 15-fold (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2026.1876426); dual-signal nanospheres (RNs@Au) detected Ebola virus glycoprotein by eye at 2 ng/mL in 20 minutes (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.7b02222); a PDA@Au core-shell probe for influenza A (H1N1) gave a visual sensitivity of 93.9% and specificity of 95.8% in 90 throat swabs (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07611-1); and Tris-stabilised AuNPs allowed simultaneous visual detection of porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (1.25 x 10^3 TCID50/mL) and porcine rotavirus (3.13 x 10^2 pg/mL), with 95% and 100% concordance with RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.saa.2024.124670). Silica cores carrying gold and quantum dots show that a second signal need not help the colour at all: for SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG the colorimetric performance was stated to equal that of a traditional gold strip, with bands visible at a 1:10^4 serum dilution and hard to see in 8 early-stage sera (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.0c03484); for the SARS-CoV-2 S and N proteins the colorimetric limit of 50 pg/mL was five times better than a standard AuNP strip (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.2c21042); and for the mpox virus A29L antigen the colorimetric limit of 0.5 ng/mL equalled that of the conventional AuNP strip (DOI: 10.1186/s12951-023-02215-4).

The final group makes the same point more starkly. Eleven studies attach a Raman reporter or a lanthanide to gold without changing its shape, and in all of them the colour channel behaves like an ordinary gold strip. Gold nanostars with a Raman reporter gave a faintly visible line at 1.02 x 10^3 copies/uL of group A rotavirus, with 95% agreement with RT-PCR in 80 faecal samples from pigs, cattle and poultry (DOI: 10.1016/j.saa.2026.127441). Au/DTNB@Ag/DTNB tags gave a naked-eye limit of 80 pg/mL for human rotavirus and, in 95 clinical faecal samples, agreed completely with both RT-qPCR and a commercial colloidal gold strip, so the colour offered nothing the commercial product did not (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-020-04670-4); the same tags reached 10 ng/mL for West Nile virus NS1 protein (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-021-04857-3). For the SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain, a Raman-tagged strip read as a colour strip gave a scanner-quantified limit of 1.2 ng/mL (visible above 1 ng/mL), against 0.4 ng/mL for an ELISA with the same immunoreagents (DOI: 10.3390/bios11120510). For SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG in patient serum, the line on SERS-active gold nanostars was visible at a 1:100 serum dilution, barely at 1:200 and not at 1:1000, and IgG lines in sera taken 3.5 and 4 months after infection stayed below the visual threshold (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.1c02305). Gold-nanobridged nanogap particles for the nucleocapsid protein gave a visible line at 0.1 ng/reaction and, placed into a commercial rapid antigen kit, left its visual limit unchanged at 0.5 ng/reaction (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07179-w). A digital SERS strip for the same antigen is the one case in which the colour of the new strip was worse than commercial strips: only the highest concentration tested (0.1 ug/mL) was visible by eye, whereas three commercial colorimetric tests reached 1 to 10 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1002/smsc.202400259). Magnetic Fe3O4@Au nanotags carrying wheat germ agglutinin captured influenza A virus before the strip, with 100% SERS accuracy in 110 throat swab or sputum samples against 81% for a commercial colloidal gold strip, but the visual limit of the strip itself is not stated (DOI: 10.1016/j.nano.2025.102853). Two nucleic acid strips without amplification gave visible lines at 80 pg/mL of HIV-1 DNA, the same limit as a commercial fluorescence kit run in the same study (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2015.11.099), and at about 20 pM of Kaposi's sarcoma-associated herpesvirus DNA on a two-line strip whose second line detected a bacterial marker (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.6b03536). The lanthanide composite, cysteamine-modified gold-europium nanoparticles for H5N1 avian influenza spiked into swab specimens, gave a naked-eye limit of 10 HAU/mL against 20 HAU/mL for commercial gold nanoparticles in the same study, a twofold gain (DOI: 10.3390/ijms23147957).

Read together, the colour channels of these dual-signal labels rank the structural strategies. Where the same study runs a gold baseline, shaped gold gives the largest gains (nanostars about 100-fold, nanocrowns about 65-fold, nanocages sevenfold); magnetic enrichment adds 100-fold, but through the number of particles delivered rather than through the particle; and silver-gold, polydopamine, MoS2, metal-organic framework and lanthanide composites give between twofold and 15-fold. Several designs give no colour gain, and none of the Raman-tagged strips gives one. In all 22 studies of this group that were read in full, the figure quoted in the abstract belongs to the instrumental channel. The cost is a multi-step synthesis and, for the sensitivity that the abstracts advertise, an instrument. However bright the particle, it captures nothing without a binder, and that is where the next group of studies turns.

### 1.6 The binder and the antigen

A brighter label cannot compensate for a weak binder, and a parallel line of work replaces the conventional antibody with a recognition element that is smaller, more stable, easier to produce or better suited to a changing target. The alternatives form a progression from engineered antibodies to fully synthetic polymers.

Nanobodies, the single-domain fragments of camelid antibodies, stand closest to the antibody and are the best documented. In a SARS-CoV-2 spike S1 strip, a nanobody-gold detector with an ACE2-gold capture probe reached 0.4 x 10^4 copies/mL of gamma-irradiated virus against 1.6 x 10^4 copies/mL for a monoclonal-antibody version of the same strip, a fourfold gain, and in 320 saliva samples with 180 RT-PCR positives it reached a sensitivity of 97.14% and a specificity of 98.57% for samples with Ct of 30 or below, against 90.04% and 97.86% for the antibody strip (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-37347-y). It is the one binder study in this section with a like-for-like comparison. Multivalent and Y-shaped nanobody assemblies have been built for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid and spike S1 proteins and for the chikungunya virus E protein, and a nanobody strip has been applied to human norovirus outbreak specimens (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.4c00509; DOI: 10.1039/d4nr02762k; DOI: 10.1039/d5nr02568k; DOI: 10.3390/bios16020132; DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2025.1707358; DOI: 10.1128/msphere.00219-16). Nanobodies can also be displayed in many copies on a carrier: yeast cell-wall nanofragments presenting anti-spike nanobodies, linked to AuNPs through the SpyTag/SpyCatcher system, gave a red line for SARS-CoV-2 virions that weakened with concentration and failed below 2 x 10^4 virions/mL, although only one positive and one negative clinical sample were run on the strip (DOI: 10.1038/s41565-023-01415-1).

Aptamers move one step further from biology: they are synthesised chemically and can be produced more reproducibly and cheaply than antibodies (DOI: 10.3390/molecules29081776). The same study shows their current limit, however: a competitive aptamer strip for SARS-CoV-2 spike protein on food packaging used an aptamer selected for broad binding to variant spikes, but its sensitivity was below that of other methods and it was tested only with recombinant protein (DOI: 10.3390/molecules29081776). Other aptamer strips for SARS-CoV-2 spike, inactivated virus and nucleocapsid protein were evaluated with inactivated virus or spiked samples (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202107730; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c00554; DOI: 10.1186/s12951-023-02191-9); a pair of sandwich aptamers detected whole avian influenza H5N2 particles with better specificity over other H5Nx viruses (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2019.03.061); an aptamer-based isolation step preceded a strip for grouper nervous necrosis virus (5 ng/mL coat protein) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2020.00886); and a gold-and-aptamer proof of concept was proposed for home detection of SARS-CoV-2 mutants (DOI: 10.1016/j.nano.2022.102590). Two further papers select aptamers for H3N2 and for the Omicron receptor-binding domain without reporting strip data (DOI: 10.1166/jbn.2019.2789; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c01993).

At the end of the progression lie fully synthetic binders. A hydrogel copolymer of aromatic amino-acid monomers, screened for affinity to the SARS-CoV-2 spike trimer and then polymerised in situ on AuNPs, acted as an abiotic antibody mimic: the core-shell probe gave a visual limit of 30 ng/reaction of spike trimer in a direct format and detected pseudovirus at 1 x 10^5 TU/mL in saliva in a sandwich format, without an antibody comparator (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2025.129324). Sialic-acid glyconanoparticles that recognise the spike through its glycan-binding activity, a lectin for severe dengue virus infection and a miniprotein binder aimed at SARS-CoV-2 variants of concern complete the range, without accuracy data (DOI: 10.1021/acscentsci.0c00855; DOI: 10.1080/15321819.2023.2260480; DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06113-2).

The conventional antibody is not standing still either: choosing the right pair and the right antigen can matter as much as replacing it. For the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, two selected antibody pairs reached 8 pg of protein and 625 TCID50/mL of virus in 60 clinical samples (DOI: 10.3390/ijms222212412), a detection antibody directed at a linear epitope reached 10 pg/mL (DOI: 10.3390/ijms25084436), and one study compared the nucleocapsid and the spike as targets (DOI: 10.1016/j.molimm.2021.01.005). In serology strips the antigen is the variable that can be engineered: an acid-treated p72 antigen detected African swine fever virus antibodies at a 1:10,000 serum dilution (DOI: 10.3389/fchem.2021.804981); the protrusion domain of the infectious bursal disease virus VP2 protein served as a highly immunogenic antigen (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-020-10458-6); plant-produced receptor-binding domain gave a sensitivity of 94.1% and a specificity of 98% in 51 sera (DOI: 10.3877/cma.j.issn.2096-112x.2021.01.006); baculovirus-expressed nucleocapsid and receptor-binding domain were tested in 103 convalescent sera (DOI: 10.1007/s10529-022-03316-0); and the shell domain of norovirus was chosen for a broad-range strip tested on 122 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1186/s12866-020-02084-z).

Binder replacement promises independence from animal immunisation and resilience to sequence variation. So far the evidence is largely analytical: apart from the nanobody strip with its fourfold gain, these studies report a detection limit or a specificity statement rather than a comparison with an antibody version of the same strip (Table A1d). With the probe complete, what remains to decide is what its colour on the line actually means.

### 1.7 Assay format: what the line can mean

The format of the immunoassay determines how the colour relates to the amount of target, and the same particle can say different things in different formats. In a sandwich strip the line darkens with the target until excess antigen saturates both antibodies and the line weakens again, the hook effect; a lumpy skin disease virus strip that examined this effect reported a detection limit of 10^3.4 TCID50/mL, a coefficient of variation below 5.3% and no cross-reaction with 12 other viruses (DOI: 10.3390/bios12090739). In a competitive strip the logic is inverted, the line fading as the target rises, which suits functional assays: a competitive gold strip for SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies reached 20.66 IU/L with 100% specificity (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.2c03677), and a biomimetic mock-virus particle displaying trimeric spike measured neutralising antibody at 59 IU/mL against the WHO standard (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04372). A quantitative colloidal gold assay can even turn the strip into a measuring instrument for other tests: one measured the detection limits of nine approved SARS-CoV-2 antigen kits and found nucleic acid testing at least 1835 times more sensitive than antigen testing for a Delta strain (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2023.124462). Reading can also be extended without changing the format: a Japanese encephalitis virus NS1 strip read with a smartphone colorimeter detected 10 pg/mL by eye in spiked serum and was stable for a month at 4 C (DOI: 10.1039/d2na00463a). The limit addressed at this level is interpretation, and the cost is that each format has its own dynamic range, which most reports do not state (Table A1f).

### 1.8 Changing the optics: scattering instead of absorption

Every strategy described so far adds absorbed colour to a white membrane. One study asked whether the membrane itself is the obstacle. When nitrocellulose is made transparent by refractive-index matching and placed over a black backing card, large AuNPs appear as orange lines by scattering light rather than absorbing it, and the background almost disappears. For the influenza A virus nucleoprotein the authors report a very large gain over their own conventional strip and over commercial strips, higher sensitivity in a small set of clinical swabs, and reading by eye or smartphone; the approach needs an additional reagent step and enough light, and the authors themselves place it beyond absorbance-based colorimetry (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). It is therefore treated here as a boundary case, with its figures kept apart from those of absorption-based strips (Table A1c). It also explains, in reverse, why large particles fail on an ordinary membrane: what they add in scattering is invisible against white.

### 1.9 Conventional gold strips: the baseline

All the engineering above is measured against the strip it set out to improve, and many of the included studies describe exactly that strip: a new colloidal gold assay that applies none of the modifications discussed. These studies define what the unmodified format achieves across viruses. Because they rarely run a modified strip beside them, they report no within-study change, and their values are listed in Table A1e.

In SARS-CoV-2 serology, colloidal gold IgM and IgG strips reached a sensitivity of 100% and a specificity of 93.3% for IgM (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.0c01554), a sensitivity of 90% and a specificity of 96.6% against real-time PCR in 60 samples (DOI: 10.1089/mab.2021.0027), 158 of 170 positives with an area under the curve of 0.958 (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.23619) and an IgM sensitivity of 90.67% (DOI: 10.1002/ctm2.90), with further strips reported without values (DOI: 10.1039/d0an00629g; DOI: 10.1097/cm9.0000000000000922); a nasal secretory IgA strip detected spike-specific IgA from people infected with the BA.5 variant (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2024.1386891). For SARS-CoV-2 antigen, a spike strip detected 62.5 ng/mL in a subunit vaccine (DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2021.635677), a nucleocapsid kit detected above 0.2 ng/mL of recombinant protein without cross-reaction (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2021.114183), a receptor-binding-domain strip detected 1 ng/mL with a sensitivity of 94.3% and a specificity of 90.9% against RT-PCR in 100 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.28416), a nanogold antigen test reached a sensitivity of 95% and a specificity of 98% (DOI: 10.3390/nano12142477), another detected 2 ng/mL with 98% concordance (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07299-3), and the CoV-SCAN strip detected ten variants at 1.6 to 12.5 TCID50 per swab (DOI: 10.1016/j.jcvp.2022.100080). A nucleic acid sandwich strip for the N3 gene reached 5 pM (DOI: 10.1016/j.jbiosc.2022.11.001).

Beyond SARS-CoV-2, the same format covers a wide range of human viruses: an HIV-1 p24 strip with in-house monoclonal antibodies reached 25 pg/mL and a specificity of 98.03% (DOI: 10.1155/2016/6743904); a strip for severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus reached 1 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.8b02366); a competitive dengue strip with the 4G2 antibody reached 5.12 x 10^2 PFU (DOI: 10.3390/bios12070495); a varicella-zoster strip reached 30 ng/mL of glycoprotein E without cross-reaction with EV71, HSV-1 or HSV-2 (DOI: 10.1016/j.virol.2023.07.008); a strip for the mpox virus A29 protein detected 50 pg/mL of purified antigen without cross-reaction with vaccinia or cowpox virus (DOI: 10.1039/d2an02043b); and an HTLV-I IgG strip reached a sensitivity of 96.7% and a specificity of 100% (DOI: 10.3390/v15010129). It extends equally to animal and aquatic viruses: feline coronavirus antibodies in plasma, serum, whole blood and ascitic fluid (DOI: 10.1007/978-1-4939-2438-7_3), nervous necrosis virus virions in fish tissue (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-020-63553-z), porcine circovirus 2 at 10^3.18 TCID50/mL (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.01953-22), pseudorabies virus at 1.336 x 10^3 copies/uL (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2024.1399123), avian leukosis virus p27 protein at 1.53 ng/mL in 35 egg samples, against 0.2 ng/mL for ELISA (DOI: 10.1039/d4an00180j), and canine parvovirus in faeces, with a sensitivity of 71% and a specificity of 92% against haemagglutination in one study (DOI: 10.1007/s00705-018-3846-2), a colloidal gold and fluorescent format in 60 clinical samples in another (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-023-12604-2), and a sensitivity of 96.4% and a specificity of 88.2% against qPCR in a third (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-55548-x). The breadth of this list is itself a finding: the unmodified gold strip already serves almost every virus family for which a good antibody exists, and the engineering of the preceding sections is aimed at its sensitivity, not at its reach.

### 1.10 What the evidence supports

Read as a whole, the studies of this section give a consistent account of how the colour of a gold strip is strengthened, and it follows the three quantities set out at the start. The number of particles at the line can be raised by attaching several particles to each target or by enriching the target magnetically before the strip, with gains of 100- to 1000-fold that require an extra step or extra hardware. The colour of each captured particle can be raised after capture, by metal enhancement or layer-by-layer assembly, with gains of tenfold to more than two orders of magnitude achieved with the same antibodies and particles as the baseline, at the price of reagents, time or an instrument; or it can be raised before capture, through clusters, shells and above all shaped gold, with gains of about five- to 100-fold in the colour channel. The best-supported comparisons are those in which the same study runs its own conventional strip (Table A4); across them the reported gain ranges from about twofold to about 1000-fold, and the high end belongs invariably to designs that add a magnet, an electrode set, an imager or a pre-incubation.

The dual-signal labels of Section 1.5 deserve a separate conclusion. Their headline sensitivity comes from SERS, photothermal or fluorescence readout, and the gain in colour alone is far smaller than their titles suggest: none to about 100-fold, and none at all for the Raman-tagged strips. For the question of this review their value lies elsewhere, in showing that shaping gold improves its colour more than alloying it or combining it with other materials.

Conjugation chemistry, binder replacement, antigen design and assay format address different limits: the activity of the recognition element, its supply, sequence variation and the interpretation of the line. Their contribution to the colour signal is rarely quantified against a baseline.

Three limitations run across the section. Many reported LODs come from scanners, imagers or readers rather than from the naked eye and therefore describe instrument-assisted performance. Clinical evaluation is the exception among engineering studies, several of which were tested only in spiked buffer, saliva or recombinant protein, with reference methods ranging from ELISA to RT-PCR. And the comparators differ, from the authors' own conventional strip through particles of the same size to commercial strips and quoted literature values, so fold-changes cannot be ranked across studies. The reporting quality of the studies read in full is summarised in Table A3.

Whatever is done to gold, the label remains gold, and the limits reported in this section are those of a plasmonic particle of a few tens of nanometres read against a white membrane. Whether a different carrier changes those limits is the question of the next section.


## 2. Beyond gold: what a different colour carrier can and cannot change

### 2.1 The case for a different particle

Every strategy in Section 1 accepted gold as the label and worked around its limits. The studies in this section ask a different question: if the colour of a strip is set by how much light each captured particle absorbs, why not start from a particle that absorbs more, is easier to manufacture, or comes in more than one colour? The literature offers three answers, and they map onto three distinct problems with gold. The first problem is optical. A 30 to 40 nm gold sphere absorbs strongly but only within a narrow band, and materials such as polydopamine absorb across the whole visible spectrum, which is the argument made for replacing gold in a SARS-CoV-2 serology strip (DOI: 10.3390/bios13030352); carbon, for its part, is simply black, and a black line on a white membrane gives the highest contrast a membrane can show (DOI: 10.3390/bios13090857). The second problem is manufacturing. Colloidal gold varies from batch to batch in particle size and binds antibodies mostly by adsorption, whereas dyed latex microspheres are uniform, intensely coloured and carry surface groups for covalent coupling, the reasons given for choosing them in porcine epidemic diarrhea virus and porcine circovirus 2 strips (DOI: 10.1186/s44149-021-00029-1; DOI: 10.1007/s00253-022-12074-y). The third problem is information. Gold has one colour, so a strip must separate analytes by position; a carrier that exists in red, blue and black lets one strip report two antibodies and a control by hue alone, as a strip for African swine fever virus and classical swine fever virus antibodies demonstrates (DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419).

These three problems organise what follows. The section begins with the carrier that answers the manufacturing problem, dyed latex (2.2), and then shows how the same large particle, once loaded with metal instead of dye, answers the optical problem as well (2.3). Colour coding answers the information problem (2.4). The remaining carriers each test one further idea: carbon tests contrast (2.5), selenium, silver, a pigment and magnetite test alternative hues and surfaces (2.6), polymer chromophores test broad absorption (2.7), and cellulose nanobeads test whether particle size alone can beat gold on clinical samples (2.8). Section 2.9 then turns the comparison around and asks what every coloured label gives up against a fluorescent one, and 2.10 draws the conclusions. As in Section 1, a new label is credited only where the same study runs a gold strip or another baseline beside it. This section draws on 33 studies, 18 of them read in full; the figures of the others are given as their abstracts state them (Tables B1a to B1g and B4).

### 2.2 Dyed latex: a uniform particle, and what it does not change

A latex microsphere is a polymer bead of 200 to 400 nm, dyed throughout and carrying carboxyl groups to which antibodies are attached covalently (DOI: 10.1186/s44149-021-00029-1; DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419). Compared with a gold sphere of 30 nm it is a very large object, and this cuts both ways. The bead holds far more dye than a gold particle holds metal, but it also aggregates and settles more readily, so the protein load on its surface has to be set at the highest level that keeps the suspension stable (DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419), and even the choice between 300 and 400 nm matters: the larger bead produced a weak false-positive line that the smaller one did not (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-025-13694-w).

What a well-made latex strip delivers in practice is best seen in veterinary virology, where most of the latex work has been done. A red latex strip for porcine circovirus 2 detected the three circulating genotypes, but not equally: the endpoint titres were 10^2.5 TCID50 per 0.1 mL for PCV2a against 10^0.7 for PCV2b and 10^1.5 for PCV2d, a difference the authors trace to the immunogens used to raise the antibodies, not to the label. Against PCR on 100 tissue and swab samples the strip was fully specific (47/47) and found 43 of 53 positives (sensitivity 81.13%, kappa 0.720); some of the missed samples, the authors argue, contained viral DNA without infectious virus (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-022-12074-y). A latex strip for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (LOD 10^3.60 TCID50/mL) ran into a problem that no label can solve: solid faecal matter disturbed the flow. A filter pad inserted between the sample and conjugate pads removed it, and the strip then reached a sensitivity of 88.57% and a specificity of 100% in 108 faecal samples against RT-PCR (kappa 0.845), with recoveries of 91.00% to 114.40% and stability for 56 days at room temperature and 37 C (DOI: 10.1186/s44149-021-00029-1). Latex strips of the same kind exist for North American porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus (5 x 10^2.0 TCID50/mL; sensitivity 90.32% on clinical samples) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2022.882112), for SARS-CoV-2 antigen (25 ng/mL) (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.24091), and, in an earlier and more specialised form, for the receptor specificity of influenza A group 1 viruses, where a sialylglycopolymer-latex probe correlated above 0.95 with an ELISA (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2014.10.036).

The two studies that place plain latex beside a laboratory method with the same binders are sobering. A red 200 nm particle gave a visual LOD of 10 ng/mL for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein and detected irradiated Omicron-infected cell extract at 7.5 x 10^5 TCID50/mL, whereas a sandwich ELISA with the same antibody pair reached 15 pg/mL, almost three orders of magnitude lower (DOI: 10.1111/1751-7915.70117). In a double-antigen sandwich for SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies, latex beads gave an LOD of 7.11 ng/mL against 1.13 ng/mL for europium fluorescent beads in the same design; and yet, against the conventional virus neutralisation test, the two performed alike, latex reaching a sensitivity of 98.29% and a specificity of 97.70% in 554 sera and the fluorescent version 97.44% and 98.28% in 389 (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2022.124200). Dyed latex, then, improves uniformity, coupling and colour choice, and it can be read by eye or by smartphone; it does not by itself move the analytical limit, which stays where the antibody pair and the sample put it (Table B1a). The question of how to make the large particle also a brighter one is answered by the next group.

### 2.3 The same particle, loaded with metal

If the advantage of latex is its size and the advantage of gold is its absorption, the obvious experiment is to put the one on the other. Two influenza studies did exactly this, and both ran gold alone as the baseline, which is why they provide the clearest colour gains of the whole section. In the first, gold or platinum nanoparticles were deposited on poly(2-vinylpyridine) latex. The composite line is dark blue with gold and dark brown with platinum, and the light extinction of one composite particle was calculated at about 200 (gold) and 100 (platinum) times that of a 30 nm gold sphere. The lowest influenza A antigen concentration visible by eye was 6.25 x 10^-3 HAU/mL with the gold composite and 2.5 x 10^-2 HAU/mL with the platinum composite, against 4.0 x 10^-1 HAU/mL with conventional gold, a gain of more than 64- and 16-fold, although only antigen solutions were tested (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.8b11745). In the second, polystyrene latex was coated with 10 nm gold, using the same monoclonal antibody as bridge and as label so that no foreign protein was introduced. For influenza H3N2 in infected allantoic fluid the visual limit was 1/16 HAU (10^1.49 TCID50/mL), against 4 HAU for a strip made with 10 nm gold and 1 HAU for one made with 30 nm gold, again 64- and 16-fold; the strip reader put the LODs at 0.016 HAU against 1.06 and 0.562 HAU, the strip did not react with H1, H5, H7 or H9 subtypes, and it kept its visual limit for three months at room temperature (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-020-04255-1). That two independent groups, with different polymers and different gold sizes, arrive at the same 16- to 64-fold suggests that the gain belongs to the architecture rather than to one recipe.

The carrier need not be loaded with metal to become brighter. Silica nanoparticles doped with the dye Direct Red 224 detected H7 avian influenza (H7N2 and H7N9) at 0.08 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1039/d1ay00204j), and dye-loaded polymersomes carried the colour of a SARS-CoV-2 strip whose reagents were purified against 121 human samples (DOI: 10.1021/acssensors.1c00854). These two studies have no gold baseline, so the size of their gain is unknown. What the metal-latex composites establish is that the limit of Section 1, the optical effect of one captured particle, can be raised by changing the carrier instead of the metal; what they cost is a two-step synthesis and, so far, the absence of clinical samples.

### 2.4 Colour as a code

Gold reports by position: one line per analyte. A carrier that comes in several colours can report by hue, and the strip then carries more information at the same length. The clearest implementation uses red, blue and black latex for a red African swine fever virus line, a blue classical swine fever virus line and a black control. On a standard positive serum the visual endpoint was 1:256 for both antibodies, which equals the commercial ELISA for African swine fever virus (1:256) and is fourfold better than the commercial ELISA for classical swine fever virus (1:64); across 180 sera the agreement with the two ELISAs gave kappa values of 0.986 and 0.918 (DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419). The same study is candid about what colour cannot do: antibodies to the E2 protein of classical swine fever virus do not distinguish vaccinated from infected animals, so the blue line measures vaccine response rather than infection (DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419).

Colour can also serve the reader rather than the assay. A second African swine fever strip used red latex for the p72 test line and blue latex, made with chicken IgY, for the control, so that a valid test shows two different colours. Its visual endpoint of 1:1024 matched the commercial ELISA, it reached a sensitivity of 97.9% and a specificity of 98.2% in 159 sera (kappa 0.955), and it survived 16 months at 4 C, 12 months at room temperature and 10 months at 37 C (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-025-13694-w). The principle extends to human viruses: a bicolour latex strip measured SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies at about 48 ng/mL in 13 sera (DOI: 10.3390/bios12020103), and a red and blue strip read the two products of a duplex RT-LAMP, human norovirus GII at 5.8 copies/uL and human adenovirus F41 at 63 copies/uL (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2026.1787049). The attraction is that the strip stays equipment-free; the risk is that the result now depends on the user telling hues apart, and none of these studies reports blinded or multi-reader interpretation (Table B1b).

### 2.5 Carbon: contrast without gain

Carbon black is the oldest non-metal label in lateral flow, inexpensive, chemically inert and, on a white membrane, as dark as a label can be. The virology studies that use it, however, either do not test it against gold or find it wanting. A carbon black strip for severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus reached 100 pg/mL in spiked serum, with recoveries of 91.58% to 105.4%, and ran no gold comparator (DOI: 10.1039/d3an00515a). A vertical-flow immunoassay, in which the sample passes through the membrane rather than along it, used carbon black coupled to anti-human IgG to detect IgG against the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein in serum within 5 to 7 minutes, with an LOD of 7.81 BAU/mL and an optimum of 150 to 250 ug of antibody per mg of carbon; the lower LOD than colloidal gold claimed in its abstract comes from a comparison with published methods, not from a gold assay run alongside (DOI: 10.3390/bios13090857). The one study that did run both labels, for porcine deltacoronavirus, built competitive strips with the same monoclonal antibody on gold and on carbon. Carbon was one dilution step less sensitive (1:64 against 1:128 of a 10^8 TCID50/mL stock), and in 137 faecal samples against RT-PCR it reached a sensitivity of 73.2% where gold reached 83.9%, at the same specificity of 98.8% (DOI: 10.1016/j.isci.2026.116948). Contrast, it seems, is not the limiting factor of a gold strip; a faint red line and a faint black line are both faint (Table B1c).

### 2.6 Other hues, other surfaces: selenium, silver, ultramarine blue and magnetite

Four inorganic particles have been tried as labels, and each illustrates a different lesson. Selenium is red like gold and has been used for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies (IgM 20 ng/mL, IgG 5 ng/mL; sensitivity 93.33% and specificity 97.34% in 90 patients and 263 controls) (DOI: 10.1039/d0lc00828a; DOI: 10.1109/tnb.2021.3105662). Its most developed application, however, turns on the surface rather than the colour: PEG-stabilised selenium particles hold the antibody in a "stand-up" orientation, which the authors credit for a reading time under one minute, and the resulting SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid kit had an LOD of 10 pg/mL, defined as the lowest concentration at which the line remained visible. In 192 clinical samples (60 negative, 132 positive) it gave 100% negative, 93.94% positive and 95.83% overall coincidence with RT-PCR, in throat swabs, saliva, serum and fingerstick blood alike, without cross-reaction with common respiratory pathogens; the largest clinical dataset in this section thus belongs to a label whose LOD was never measured against gold (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2024.116878).

Silver teaches the opposite lesson. It absorbs more strongly than gold at the same size, but it is unstable in biological fluids, and the study that used it had first to solve that: a thin calix[4]arene coating made the particles stable in plasma and for six months of storage and gave them carboxyl groups to which the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein was coupled covalently. The particles are yellow. In a dipstick for anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG they reached 5 ng/mL in buffer, an order of magnitude below citrate gold with adsorbed spike protein, and gave the same signal with ten times fewer particles. In plasma the LOD rose to 1.5 ug/mL, and in 25 sera the dipstick was fully specific (10/10) but found only 11 of 15 RT-PCR-positive patients, a rate the authors call typical of gold-based strips (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c00870). The gain, in other words, was real and was lost to the matrix. Ultramarine blue, an inexpensive pigment, was carboxylated and coupled to antibodies as a blue label for HBsAg, reaching 0.37 ng/mL (signal-to-noise 3) in spiked fetal calf serum by eye or image analysis; its abstract claims a lower LOD than a gold strip, but the comparisons in the main text are with literature values for iron oxide and red silica, and no patient samples were tested (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2019.11.037). Natural-colour magnetite (Fe3O4) combined a visible line with a magnetic core in a SARS-CoV-2 strip reaching 0.5 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2026.150960). Of the four, only silver was measured against gold in the same study, and its advantage did not survive plasma (Table B1d).

### 2.7 Polymer chromophores: broad absorption, and the surface problem again

Polydopamine absorbs across the whole visible spectrum, and the one study that built a virus strip on it shows that a new label brings its own surface chemistry to solve. Polydopamine adheres strongly to nitrocellulose; probes coupled directly to it would stick at the test line regardless of antigen and read as false negatives. The particles were therefore wrapped in polyelectrolyte layers, silica and a PEG-silane before antigen and antibody were attached, growing to 230 to 320 nm in the process. The finished three-line strip separated SARS-CoV-2 IgG from IgM, was quantified with a camera and image software, and gave LODs of 1.51 ng/mL (IgM) and 2.34 ng/mL (IgG) from calibration curves, while a commercial gold strip run in the same study barely detected 10 to 100 ng/mL; the matrix was serum spiked with standard antibodies (DOI: 10.3390/bios13030352). A related polymer, black polylevodopa, carries abundant carboxyl groups of its own and gave LODs of 5 pg/mL for SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid antigen and 0.1 ng/mL for influenza A H1N1 antigen, without cross-reaction with influenza B, respiratory syncytial virus or other coronavirus nucleoproteins; the higher sensitivity than gold and commercial strips that the authors state is shown only in supplementary figures, without a fold-change or a clinical sample in the main text (DOI: 10.1016/j.bbrc.2024.149821). Polydiacetylene vesicles are a reminder of the rule applied throughout this review: they show a red line and red fluorescence at once, and for HBsAg the line was visible by eye down to 1 ng/mL while 0.1 ng/mL was reached only in the fluorescence image, so only the former counts as a colorimetric result (DOI: 10.1002/asia.201700769). Broad absorption is a sound argument; the evidence that it beats gold rests, for now, on one spiked-serum comparison (Table B1e).

### 2.8 Cellulose nanobeads: size alone, tested on patients

The metal-latex composites of 2.3 showed that a large carrier can out-absorb a gold sphere, but they were tested on antigen solutions. Cellulose nanobeads, dyed cellulose particles of 330 to 365 nm sold for lateral flow in red, blue, black and green, are the one non-metal label for which the same question has been answered on clinical samples. Their first use for a virus paired them with scFv-Fc fusion proteins selected by phage display against the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein: the best pair detected 2 ng of antigen and 2.5 x 10^4 PFU of cultured virus by eye within 20 minutes, with a handheld reader for semi-quantitative results, and did not react with the nucleoproteins of MERS-CoV, SARS-CoV or influenza H1N1; gold was not run (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2020.112868). The direct comparison followed, for the same antigen, on otherwise identical strips carrying cellulose nanobeads or 40 nm gold. The authors reason that a 330 nm bead offers roughly 68 times the surface of a 40 nm sphere for the detection antibody, and the results bear this out at two levels. In buffer the lowest antigen concentration visible by eye was 0.41 ng/mL for cellulose against 3.28 ng/mL for gold, and at the gold limit the reader signal of the cellulose strip was about 20 times higher. In archived nasopharyngeal swabs read by eye, both strips were fully specific in 10 RT-qPCR-negative samples, but the weakest positive that gold still detected had a Ct of 21.31 (1.26 x 10^6 copies/mL), whereas cellulose reached a Ct of 26.10 (3.59 x 10^4 copies/mL): gold met the WHO target product profile of 10^6 copies/mL exactly, cellulose fell two orders of magnitude below it. The two labels were tested on different positive sets (32 and 23 samples), and neither detected samples with Ct above 30 (DOI: 10.1039/d5ra08197a). The carrier has since been used in a dual-colour design, red test and blue control beads, for the Nipah virus nucleocapsid protein, with an analytical LOD of 1 ng/mL and, for gamma-irradiated cultured virus quantified by RT-ddPCR, 4.1 x 10^7 copies/mL by portable reader and 8.3 x 10^7 by eye; it did not react with other respiratory virus nucleoproteins, and clinical specimens of a biosafety level 4 pathogen were not available (DOI: 10.1080/22221751.2026.2716499). Size alone, then, buys about an order of magnitude on patients, and no more: the larger particle still stops well short of the viral loads that nucleic acid tests reach (Table B1f).

### 2.9 The price of reading by eye

Every label in this section was chosen so that the result can be read without an instrument. A handful of studies measured what that choice costs by running a fluorescent label on the same antibodies. Fluorescent microspheres were more sensitive than coloured ones in strips for rotavirus and adenovirus and for influenza A and B, with no cross-reaction in either format (DOI: 10.1186/s12985-018-0951-5; DOI: 10.1186/s12985-019-1190-0). The SARS-CoV-2 neutralising-antibody strip of 2.2 put a number on the gap, about sixfold in LOD, and then showed that the gap disappeared at the diagnostic level, where latex and fluorescence agreed equally well with the neutralisation test (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2022.124200). For antibodies to the human adenovirus 3 hexon protein, quantum dots reached a positive percent agreement of 100% against 96.9% for gold, at a negative agreement of 98.2%, in 90 sera (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics16162670). The pattern is consistent and useful: a coloured label gives up a few-fold to an order of magnitude in analytical LOD against a fluorescent one on the same binders, and that loss matters only when the clinical cut-off falls between the two limits (Table B1g).

### 2.10 What the evidence supports

Replacing gold changes what a strip can do more than how strong its colour is. Dyed latex brings uniformity, covalent coupling and a choice of colours, and it has become the label of veterinary antigen strips for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus, porcine circovirus 2, porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus and African and classical swine fever virus; multicolour latex lets one strip report several analytes by hue. The colour gains that can be measured against gold in the same study come from a single principle, particle size: metal-loaded latex lowered the visual limit for influenza antigen 16- to 64-fold below 10 to 30 nm gold, and cellulose nanobeads lowered it eightfold in buffer and detected SARS-CoV-2 in swabs at a viral load about 35 times lower than gold. The other comparisons are small or negative: calixarene-coated silver gained an order of magnitude in buffer and lost it in plasma, polydopamine quantified low antibody concentrations better than a commercial gold strip in spiked serum, and carbon was less sensitive than gold in the one clinical comparison (Table B4). Against fluorescent labels on the same binders, every coloured label was less sensitive. Selenium, ultramarine blue, magnetite, polylevodopa, red silica and polymersomes rest on single studies without a gold comparator.

Three limitations recur. Reference methods differ between studies (PCR, RT-PCR, commercial ELISA, virus neutralisation), so agreement values cannot be compared across them. Several studies used spiked or standard samples only, and the silver study, whose gain in buffer vanished in plasma, shows what that omission can hide. And genotype or strain coverage depends on the antibody pair rather than on the label, as the porcine circovirus 2 strip makes explicit. The reporting quality of the 18 studies read in full is summarised in Table B3.

The carriers of this section differ from gold in size, colour, cost and surface, and the best of them, metal-loaded latex and cellulose nanobeads, give gains of 8- to 64-fold by carrying more optical effect on each captured particle. None of them, however, adds colour after capture. The next section leaves the label as it is and asks what a second source of colour at the test line can add.


## 3. Catalytic and enzymatic amplification: adding colour after capture

### 3.1 From a better label to a second source of colour

Sections 1 and 2 improved the colour of a strip by changing the label: its size, its shape, its material. Whatever the label, however, the colour of a conventional strip remains limited by two quantities, what each captured particle absorbs and how many particles the antibodies manage to capture (DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.7b06229). Amplification attacks the problem from the other side. It leaves the label as it is and adds a second source of colour at the test line after the sandwich has formed: something captured there either deposits more material or turns over a substrate into a coloured product, so that each captured label produces far more colour than it carries. This is the principle of the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and in the laboratory it is routine; on a strip, however, conventional enzyme amplification demands technical skill and equipment that suit field use poorly (DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.7b06229). The studies of this section ask how far catalytic amplification can be brought into a strip while keeping it simple enough for the point of need.

They can be read as a progression in how much of the amplifier is built into the strip and how stable it is. The simplest amplifier uses the captured gold itself as a seed for further metal deposition, and it continues the enlargement strategies of Section 1.3 (Section 3.2). The next uses a natural enzyme as the label, with a substrate that the enzyme converts into colour (Section 3.3). Natural enzymes, however, lose activity in storage, and the third step replaces them with inorganic particles that mimic enzyme activity, nanozymes, first as noble-metal particles (Section 3.4), then as engineered structures with supports, magnetic cores and several metals (Section 3.5), and finally as non-noble catalysts and catalysts paired with a new binder (Section 3.6). All of these amplifiers cost a step, and the fourth part of the section follows the attempts to build the step into the strip so that the user adds nothing but the sample (Section 3.7). A last group adds a second, instrumental readout to the catalytic colour, and only the colour is counted here (Section 3.8); Section 3.9 states what the evidence supports.

As before, an improvement counts only against an unamplified or conventional strip run in the same study. This section draws on 49 studies, 22 of them read in full, after the exclusion of seven evaluations of marketed amplification kits (rule E10); the figures of the others are given as their abstracts state them (Tables C1a to C1f and C4).

### 3.2 Depositing more metal on captured gold

The simplest amplifier is the particle itself. Gold that has been captured at the test line can be enlarged by depositing silver, gold or copper on it, which darkens the line without any new label, and the idea was introduced for gold in Section 1.3. The silver version lowered the detection limit of an equine influenza virus strip 5-fold against conventional rapid tests (DOI: 10.1292/jvms.17-0105). For foot-and-mouth disease virus (FMDV) it was built into a device in which silver grows on the captured gold from 0.05 to 10 um, about 200 times in diameter, and black lines appear within 5 minutes after the reagents are released. For the O/TUR/2009 strain this gave a detection limit of 10^4 TCID50/0.1 mL against 10^5 for the commercial gold kit, and 10- to 100-fold for most serotypes, although the gain was not seen for serotypes SAT2 and SAT3. The same study shows what real samples can do to an amplifier: in 132 samples from an outbreak in Mongolia, read against a colour chart, sensitivity against rRT-PCR was 85.4% on vesicular epithelium but 46.7% on lesion swabs and 36.8% on saliva, and saliva inhibited the untreated test in 51.3% of samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2019.113736).

Gold and copper reach further. Gold enhancement lowered the limit of an infectious spleen and kidney necrosis virus (ISKNV) strip from 0.25 to 0.050 ug/mL, a fivefold gain (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2026.130562). Copper deposition was the most powerful of the three. For HIV-1 p24, a copper shell grown in situ on the captured gold with the help of polyethylenimine, which fixes the copper ions at the particle and avoids their nonspecific binding to protein and membrane, lowered the visual limit from 2 ng/mL to 50 fg/mL, over four orders of magnitude, and the same step improved a model streptavidin-gold assay from 3 pM to 0.3 fM. It also showed its value on a marketed strip: a commercial gold antibody strip for SARS-CoV-2 missed 7 of 69 PCR-confirmed positive blood samples (specificity 100% in 63 negatives), and all 7 became positive after amplification while the negatives stayed negative (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2020.112753; the bacterial result in the same paper is not counted). For the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, copper deposition for 3 minutes turned the red band black and lowered the visual limit from 0.01 ug/mL to 10 pg/mL, three orders of magnitude, but only with certified reference antigen: the authors state that no cultured virus or real samples were tested (DOI: 10.3390/bios12010013). A three-dimensional device combines polyA gold nanoparticles with gold and silver deposition for SARS-CoV-2 nucleic acid, but its abstract gives no values (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.5c03575). One related design redesigned the gold-nanosphere strip for the SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain and added a thermal-contrast reading step of under one minute: the visual limits were 3.6 aM in buffer and 28.6 aM in nasopharyngeal wash, and the thermal step distinguished 26 to 32% of the visual false negatives of commercial strips, a gain that is thermal, not colorimetric (DOI: 10.1021/acsanm.1c03217).

Metal deposition is therefore a large and cheap gain, but it carries the cost that Section 1.3 described: an added reagent step and a risk of background. The remaining question is whether the step can be made part of the strip, and the answer, discussed in Section 3.7, came first for gold, with a self-enhancing triplex strip.

### 3.3 Natural enzymes as labels

A natural enzyme amplifies in a different way. Horseradish peroxidase (HRP) and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) turn over their substrates repeatedly, so that one captured label produces many coloured product molecules, and on a strip they have been used alone, combined with gold, or on magnetic carriers. The clearest demonstration of what the enzyme adds to gold comes from potato virus X. Adding ALP on a second gold conjugate lowered the LOD to 0.3 ng/mL, 27-fold below the 8 ng/mL of the conventional strip with the same antibodies and the same 20 nm gold, in buffer and in potato leaf extract, and the line changed from red to dark violet, which gives better contrast on white nitrocellulose. By contrast, adding the second gold conjugate without the enzyme gained only 1.5-fold, so the enzyme, not the extra gold, carried the gain (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-017-2595-3). The choice of enzyme matters for the sample: the authors note that the endogenous peroxidase activity of plant extracts limits the use of HRP enhancement, and they pair gold with ALP, the second most common enzyme of commercial ELISA kits, for that reason (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-017-2595-3).

In human virology HRP with the chromogen TMB has been tested at scale. An HRP-labelled strip for influenza A and B, in which TMB is added to a substrate pad after the sample and the whole procedure takes 20 minutes, detected 8 influenza A isolates at 0.001 to 0.00025 HAU and 5 influenza B isolates at 0.016 to 0.004 HAU, more than one order of magnitude below BD gold strips (0.01 to 0.04 HAU and 0.32 to 0.04 HAU). Against rRT-PCR the clinical figures depended strongly on how the sample was taken: sensitivity for influenza A was 90.2% (55/61) on nasopharyngeal swabs but 66.2% (45/68) on oropharyngeal swabs, for influenza B 82.6% (71/86) and 58.4% (45/77), with specificity of 99.7% or above throughout; the assignment of these figures to the two viruses is reconstructed from the counts, because the text of the paper is interleaved (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.25322). Several more strips follow the same pattern. HRP added to a colloidal gold strip for bovine parvovirus gave a tenfold gain over the traditional strip (10^2 TCID50) on faecal samples (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2023.1174737); a magnetic enzyme label detected dengue virus NS1 of serotypes 1 to 4 at 0.1 to 1.0 ng/mL with 100% sensitivity and specificity on clinical samples (DOI: 10.7717/peerj.7779); a capture-layer design with an enzyme label detected dengue NS1 with 5-fold higher sensitivity than a gold-standard ELISA (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.0c00367); a hepatitis B e-antigen strip with an amplification system of unstated mechanism was 27-fold more sensitive than its conventional strip, in 420 blood samples (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.25452); and HRP tracers detected hepatitis B virus DNA in a vertical-flow format with 100% consistency with real-time PCR (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-022-03988-7).

The most inventive use of HRP deposits the product not as a precipitate but as a second colour. In tyramide signal amplification the enzyme converts biotin-tyramide into radicals that bind to nearby proteins, and a second round with streptavidin-HRP microspheres multiplies the label. For the SARS-CoV-2 nucleoprotein the single-colour version gave a naked-eye cut-off of 31.3 pg/mL, which the authors compare favourably with values quoted from the literature for two commercial antigen tests (121 and 204 pg/mL); the dual-colour version reached an LOD of 3.98 pg/mL against 55.46 pg/mL for the traditional strip, about 14-fold, with a naked-eye cut-off of 7.8 pg/mL against 125 pg/mL, 16-fold. Inactivated virus was detected at 0.018 TCID50/mL, seven variants including Omicron BA.1 and BA.2 at 5 TCID50/mL, and no signal arose with influenza A and B, parainfluenza 2 or respiratory syncytial virus; no patient samples were tested (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2024.116262).

The weakness of natural enzymes is stability. In one direct test, freeze-dried antibody-HRP conjugates lost most of their signal within two weeks of dry storage at room temperature, whereas a platinum nanocatalyst kept its signal for weeks, even at 44 C (DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.7b06229). That observation is the main reason the field moved to the particles of the next section. The limit addressed at this level is the number of coloured product molecules per captured label; the cost is a substrate step and a label that loses activity in dry storage.

### 3.4 Noble-metal nanozymes: from a model to a strip

Nanozymes are particles with enzyme-like activity. Supplied with hydrogen peroxide and a chromogenic substrate, a peroxidase-like nanozyme oxidises the substrate and deposits colour at the test line, exactly as HRP does, but with the stability of an inorganic material. Platinum-based particles dominate the evidence, and the most thoroughly characterised example set the pattern for the rest.

A porous platinum core-shell nanocatalyst for the HIV-1 p24 antigen reached 0.8 pg/mL (DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.7b06229). Its activity depends on particle size, on the polymer capping used in synthesis and on the porous platinum shell, which stays accessible to hydrogen peroxide even when serum proteins coat the surface; the best size range was 120 to 200 nm, and larger particles performed worse. The same strip works in two regimes, because at high antigen the intrinsic dark colour of the particle is visible and at low antigen the catalytic step takes over, which widens the dynamic range over several orders of magnitude. In serial plasma from an HIV seroconversion panel it detected antigen during the acute phase, and lyophilised reagents survived weeks of accelerated ageing; but the assay needs a wash step and a 5-minute development, so it is not a one-step strip (DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.7b06229).

Gold-platinum particles carry the principle to SARS-CoV-2. In a nanoenzyme-linked sensor for the nucleocapsid protein, Au@Pt particles gave an LOD of 0.026 ng/mL and a positivity of 76.2% against 47.6% for ELISA in clinical samples; the developed substrate is read by transmitted light in a small 3D-printed holder with a laser, a photometer and a smartphone, so the result is colorimetric but not equipment-free (DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2021.130718). The LOD was similar to a laboratory ELISA and lower than that of a conventional strip with the same antibodies, and the Au@Pt particles kept their activity over days while HRP lost it (DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2021.130718). The first simple strips followed. Au@Pt particles with a 2-minute TMB step lowered the naked-eye limit for the nucleocapsid protein from 50 ng/mL without catalysis to 40 pg/mL, about 1250-fold (the paper says more than 1000-fold), and the result in artificial saliva did not differ from that in water; as the authors note, no clinical samples were tested (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-024-06697-3). Au@Pt@Pd, a trimetallic particle, shows the limits of attributing a gain to the amplifier: the LOD was 0.24 ng/mL before catalysis and 0.06 ng/mL after, so catalysis added only 4-fold, and the 16-fold and 8-fold advantage over a gold strip (0.98 ng/mL) and a gold-flower strip (0.49 ng/mL) therefore comes mostly from the probe itself, with an ELISA at 0.12 ng/mL; in 44 positive and 50 negative nasopharyngeal swabs the sensitivity was 84.09%, the specificity 100% and the accuracy 92.55% (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-024-06819-x). A related Au@Pd@Pt strip reached 0.037 ng/mL for the same antigen, 17-fold below the unenhanced strip (DOI: 10.1039/d2ay01530g).

The same particles have been tried on other targets. Peroxide-tolerant Au@Pt served for potato virus X (4 pg/mL in tuber, 8 pg/mL in leaf) (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2020.121961), oxidase-like Au@Pt nanorods for hepatitis B virus DNA needed no hydrogen peroxide (8.5 pM) (DOI: 10.1039/d1an02084f), an AuPt nanoalloy was used for human papillomavirus 16 (HPV16) DNA (DOI: 10.1039/d3ra00757j), Au@Pt for respiratory syncytial virus F protein reached 0.78 ng/mL, about 4-fold better than colloidal gold (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2026.130820), Pt-Pd particles for foot-and-mouth disease virus serotype O detected 96.7% of positive sera (DOI: 10.1016/j.jchromb.2024.124043), PdRu nanocages gave 0.93 and 0.19 nM for HPV16 and HPV18 (DOI: 10.1016/j.jcis.2024.06.002), and polydopamine-platinum particles for influenza A gave a 2-fold colorimetric and a 200-fold catalytic gain against a gold strip (DOI: 10.1186/s12951-025-03903-z). A platinum-polymer nanoparticle strip for SARS-CoV-2 evaluated on 200 clinical specimens had a sensitivity of 74% and a specificity of 100% (DOI: 10.3390/biomedicines13122993), a reminder that amplification does not remove the limits that antibody pairs and sample type set (Table C1c). The limit addressed at this level is turnover per label; the cost is peroxide and substrate, a development step and, in the best-characterised case, a wash step. The question that the next studies pose is how much more catalytic activity can be placed on each captured label.

### 3.5 Engineering the nanozyme: supports, magnets and several metals

A nanozyme can only turn over substrate at its surface, so the obvious way to make it stronger is to put more catalytic surface on each probe. The studies of this group do it by giving the particle a support with a large surface, a magnetic core that also concentrates the virus, and a combination of metals, and they show that the three work together.

The support comes first. A platinum nanozyme grown on a mesoporous silica shell around an Fe3O4 core, for influenza A, has a larger specific surface than a version with a non-porous shell, and with DAB as substrate the visual limit was 1 ng/mL before catalysis (10-fold below the 10 ng/mL of a commercial gold strip) and 0.01 ng/mL after catalysis, a further 100-fold; the quantitative LOD was 0.0089 ng/mL, 112-fold lower than for the non-porous version, and 33 copies/mL for inactivated H1N1, with the naked-eye limit moving from 10^4 to 50 copies/mL (200-fold). Recovery in spiked throat swabs was 95.20 to 106.00% and no patient samples were tested; the abstract states that the sensitivity is about 100 times that of commercial gold strips, whereas the visual figures of the text would give 1000 times, and both are reported as stated (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2024.343474). The same logic with MoS2 as support: Fe3O4@MoS2@Pt nanotags for SARS-CoV-2 and H1N1 had a visual limit of 1 ng/mL before catalysis, equal to that of a commercial gold strip (1 ng/mL) and 10-fold below a gold strip made with the same antibodies (10 ng/mL), and 0.1 ng/mL after catalysis; the colour LOD was 80 pg/mL (the abstract prints ng/mL) and, for H1N1, 0.1 ug/mL visual before and 0.05 ug/mL after catalysis, against 1 ug/mL for a commercial gold strip (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.3c15851). A three-dimensional film-like GO-Pt-AuPt nanozyme detected two respiratory viruses at 1 pg/mL (DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.4c09513).

The magnetic core adds a second gain that does not come from catalysis at all. In a multi-metallic nanozyme on MoS2 with Fe3O4/Ag satellites, magnetic separation concentrates influenza A virus from the throat swab before the strip, and catalysis then raised the visual limit from 0.05 ng/mL to 5 pg/mL, tenfold, to an LOD of 0.8 pg/mL; the version without the Fe3O4/Ag (MoS2@AP) had an LOD of 10 pg/mL and the nanofibre label alone (AP) 100 pg/mL, ten and one hundred times higher. Three commercial gold strips read 100 pg/mL visually, which is 125 times the calculated LOD but only 20 times the visual limit after catalysis, a distinction to keep in mind, and 20 clinical throat swabs were all classified correctly, with inactivated virus detected at 140 copies/mL within 26 minutes (DOI: 10.1002/exp.20240425). The most elaborate design, a rambutan-like Fe3O4 core with two Au@Ir layers and wheat germ agglutinin, which binds viral and bacterial glycoproteins broadly, separates these gains: for the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, magnetic enrichment alone lowered the visual limit from 0.1 to 0.01 ng/mL, the second Au@Ir layer made the catalysis 5-fold stronger than a single layer, and catalysis took the visual limit from 0.5 to 0.01 ng/mL (50-fold) and the LOD from 0.1099 to 0.0018 ng/mL. Against a gold strip (about 1 ng/mL) the colour mode is 2- to 10-fold better and the catalytic LOD at least 555-fold better, and against an ELISA (0.21 ng/mL) 116-fold; sensitivity and specificity were both 100% in 23 positive and 9 negative throat swabs (the probe also detects two bacteria in the same strip, which are not counted) (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.5c03762). A core-satellite magnetic nanozyme whose Pt and Ir protrusions mimic the spikes of a coronavirus for SARS-CoV-2 and mpox virus antigens confirmed the arithmetic: catalysis lowered the visual limit 100-fold (5 to 0.05 ng/mL, LOD 1.80 and 2.09 ng/mL to 19.4 and 22.5 pg/mL), magnetic enrichment added tenfold, and against gold strips of 5 and 10 ng/mL the overall gain was 1000- to 4000-fold; in 117 throat swabs the direct mode reached a sensitivity of 89.04% (kappa 0.859) and the enrichment mode 100% (73/73) against qPCR (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.6c03394).

Taken together, these studies give the section its most useful quantitative result. For a nanozyme of this kind the catalytic step adds about 10- to 100-fold to the visual limit of the same strip, magnetic enrichment adds about 10-fold, a better support or a second metal layer adds 5-fold or more, and what is advertised against a commercial gold strip is the product of these gains and the brightness of the probe itself, measured sometimes on the visual limit and sometimes on a calculated LOD.

### 3.6 Non-noble catalysts, and catalysts paired with a new binder

Platinum is expensive, and iron oxide was the first alternative. A Fe3O4 nanozyme strip for Ebola virus glycoprotein, developed with DAB and peroxide after the run, reached 1 ng/mL by eye against 100 ng/mL for a standard colloidal gold strip and 0.1 ng/mL for an ELISA (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2015.05.025). Because no Ebola patient samples were available, the authors tested pseudovirus in serum and, as a surrogate, serum from 20 PCR-positive and 31 negative patients with a different bunyavirus infection; the strip detected 12 of 20 positives against 8 of 20 for the ELISA, with no false positives (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2015.05.025). The potato virus study that followed used Fe3O4 very differently. The particles were applied as a plain colorimetric label read by eye within 10 minutes, with no substrate step, to build monovalent and bivalent strips for potato viruses X and Y; the authors note that substrate-based amplification could raise the sensitivity further but did not test it, all 12 tissue-culture plantlets matched RT-PCR, and storage stability and batch consistency were not evaluated (DOI: 10.3390/plants15040656). The two studies show that the term "nanozyme strip" covers both amplified and non-amplified formats.

Other catalysts change the chemistry and, in one case, the binder. A PDA@MnO2 oxidase-like mimic was paired with a peptide, WFLNDSELISML, obtained by phage biopanning and binding the spike protein, in place of an antibody. Against a gold strip with the same peptide the visual limit was 500 pg/mL, against 100 pg/mL for the PDA@MnO2 probe without catalysis and 50 pg/mL with it, 10-fold below gold; the abstract's 18.7-fold lower LOD is not reproduced by the printed LODs (15.0, 12.8 and 8.01 pg/mL), and the visual 10-fold is the reliable figure. Pseudovirus in saliva was visible at 300 TU/mL and at 150 TU/mL after catalysis, and no patient samples were tested (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c02821). The binder and the catalyst, in other words, are independent levers, as Sections 1.6 and 3.2 anticipated. Bimetallic Fe-Co particles were used for porcine rotavirus on faecal samples (100 TCID50/mL, 2-fold better than commercial gold strips; sensitivity 95.45%, specificity 100%) (DOI: 10.1016/j.ab.2026.116259), and a nanozyme strip for avian leukosis virus reached 12.5 TCID50/mL without cross-reaction with six avian viruses (DOI: 10.1016/j.tvjl.2025.106508) (Table C1d). The limit addressed at this level is the cost, stability and reactivity of the catalyst; the cost remains a substrate step.

### 3.7 Building the amplification into the strip

Every amplifier discussed so far costs a step, and the step is why many simple gold strips remain in use. The last group of studies tries to remove it. They do so in two ways: by delivering the reagents through the strip in the right sequence, or by delaying their release until the labels have been captured.

The gold version came first. A self-enhancing triplex strip for influenza A, influenza B and SARS-CoV-2 laminates two enhancement pads carrying Au3+ and hydroxylamine beneath the detection layer, separated by a double-sided adhesive barrier, so that gold grows in situ after the immunoreaction without a user step, and the growth is confined to the captured particles, which limits background. The visual limits were 2 ng/mL for influenza A, 2 ng/mL for influenza B and 0.2 ng/mL for SARS-CoV-2, against 10, 20 and 10 ng/mL for the conventional strip, gains of 5-, 10- and 50-fold (the abstract's 5- to 25-fold refers to the calculated LODs of 1.0, 1.0 and 0.2 ng/mL). In 172 samples, 132 standard-added and 40 paired, sensitivity was 97.0%, 97.2% and 98.3% with 100% specificity, though these are antigen-spiked samples; 24 residual RT-qPCR-positive swabs (8 per virus) were all detected for influenza A and SARS-CoV-2 and mostly for influenza B, and more than 85% of the signal remained after 21 days at 45 C, which the authors estimate as at least three months of storage (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2025.129228).

For nanozymes, the chromogenic substrate itself has to wait. A strip with delayed substrate release keeps the substrate in a glass-fibre membrane at the sample pad, so that it dissolves and reaches the test line only after the nanozyme probes have been captured, which lowers the background and removes a user step; it detected a synthetic influenza A (H1N1) nucleic acid spiked into serum with a 25-fold lower LOD than the same strip before catalysis, and has not been tested on patient samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2025.127557). A hydrogel-integrated strip traps DAB in a sodium alginate hydrogel on a glass-fibre membrane and releases it after about 4 minutes. With the SARS-CoV-2 E gene nucleic acid as a model target, the visual LOD was 0.2 nM, 25-fold lower than the common strip without amplification (5 nM), and the automatic release matched the manual addition of DAB (calculated LOD 0.146 against 0.150 nM); it was tested in artificial saliva spiked at 10, 50 and 100 nM, without cross-signal from RSV, influenza B, adenovirus and H1N1 sequences, and without patient samples (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07731-8). A platinum strip for the influenza A nucleoprotein reaches the same goal with a flow-delay pad and an acidic secondary flow that deliver TMB and peroxide after the sample, so that the user adds nothing but the sample and a smartphone application reads the intensity ratio; the LOD was 11.6 pg/mL, 1000-fold lower than the traditional strip, and against RT-PCR on 42 clinical samples tested in triplicate the sensitivity was 96.8% and the specificity 98.4%, though these figures count the 126 replicate measurements, not 42 independent patients (DOI: 10.1021/acsinfecdis.5c00731). A nanozyme strip for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein with a push-button enhancement in a 3D-printed casing reached 0.1 ng/mL in undiluted serum, with the step reduced to one press (DOI: 10.1039/d1an01350e). An aptamer-based design for the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein changes how the chemical signal is transformed and raised the signal quality more than 20-fold with an LOD of 168 pg/mL in 25 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1039/d4nr04130e). The limit addressed at this level is the operator, and the cost is a more complex strip assembly and, so far, very little patient data (Table C1e).

### 3.8 Catalytic colour with a second readout, and mixed targets

Several nanozyme strips add a second readout, such as photothermal, fluorescent or Raman signals, or combine viral and bacterial targets, and in each only the colour channel and the viral target are counted here. A Fe3O4@MoS2@Pt nanotag read in a catalytic colorimetric mode and in a photothermal mode, discussed in Section 3.5, is the clearest example, and its photothermal limits are not counted (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.3c15851). Others combine nanozymes with fluorescence or SERS: a SQF@ZIF-8/Pt hybrid for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein (colorimetric 0.0302 ng/mL) (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.5c02780), a SERS-nanozyme strip for norovirus (0.001 ng/mL visual) and adenovirus (10^2 PFU/mL visual) in 92 stool samples (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.5c03047), and a trimodal strip for norovirus (visual 0.05 ng/mL) and E. coli O157 in 116 stool samples, consistent with qPCR (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.6c07352). A FeAu@AuIr nanozyme strip for SARS-CoV-2 and two bacteria, tested on 170 positive respiratory samples, completes the group, with no values in its abstract (DOI: 10.1002/advs.202502574). Only the colour channel and the viral target are counted for this review (Table C1f).

### 3.9 What the evidence supports

Catalytic amplification gives the largest changes in the colour signal of this review. Within their own papers, the reported gains on the visual limit range from about twofold to four orders of magnitude, and they fall into three levels. Metal deposition and natural enzymes give mostly 5- to 100-fold gains with the same label (silver 5- to 100-fold, gold 5- to 50-fold, alkaline phosphatase 27-fold, tyramide 14- to 16-fold), and copper reaches over four orders on a model antigen. Nanozyme catalysis adds about 10- to 100-fold on the visual limit of the same strip, and, in engineered probes, magnetic enrichment adds another 10-fold and a better support or a second metal layer 5-fold or more. What is advertised against a commercial gold strip, between 16- and 4000-fold, is the product of these gains and the brightness of the probe, and it is measured sometimes on the visual limit and sometimes on a calculated LOD (Table C4). Nanozymes are consistently described as more stable than natural enzymes, which is the main reason for their use (Table C2).

Three cautions run through the evidence. First, amplification is paid for with steps: substrate, hydrogen peroxide, washing or a reading device; the clearest example is the smartphone nanoenzyme sensor for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, whose result depends on an optical reader, and the built-in designs of Section 3.7 are the answer to it. Second, the comparator differs (an unamplified strip, a commercial gold strip, the same strip before catalysis, an ELISA), so fold-improvements cannot be ranked. Third, the clinical evidence is thin and does not always hold up: where patient samples were tested, sensitivity ranged from 12 of 20 samples for the Ebola-surrogate strip and 36.8% for foot-and-mouth disease on saliva through 74% for a platinum-polymer SARS-CoV-2 strip to 100% with panels of about 30 samples, several designs were tested on antigen or spiked samples only, and replicate measurements were sometimes counted as separate samples. The potato virus Fe3O4 strip shows, finally, that a nanozyme can also serve as a plain label without any amplification step, which makes the term "nanozyme strip" ambiguous. The reporting quality of the 22 studies read in full is summarised in Table C3.

Amplification multiplies the colour of what has been captured, but it cannot raise the number of target molecules that reach the line, give the complex more time to form, or make the sample usable. These limits belong to the strip and the sample, and they are the subject of the next section.


## 4. Assay and flow engineering: when the strip around the label limits the colour

### 4.1 Everything that happens before the label meets the line

Sections 1 to 3 changed what sits on the strip: the particle, its colour and the amplifier. Yet a label can only colour the line if it gets there. A sample has to enter the strip in a usable form, flow past the capture line for long enough, bring enough target molecules, meet binders that recognise the target and be read in a defined format. Any of these steps can limit the signal whatever the label does, and the label of the studies in this section is usually plain colloidal gold. The gains reported here therefore describe the strip, the sample and the reaction around the label.

The studies are organised along the path of the sample. The first group slows the liquid down so that complexes have longer to bind (Section 4.2). The second increases the number of target molecules that reach the line, by concentrating the sample, collecting it on magnetic particles, driving it electrically or arranging the reaction outside the strip (Section 4.3). The third deals with whether the sample reaches the strip in a usable form at all (Section 4.4). The fourth examines what the strip can recognise, through new binders and antigens (Section 4.5), and the fifth how the reaction is arranged and read, in functional, serological and nucleic acid formats (Section 4.6). Strips that combine these elements for one virus form the baseline (Section 4.7), and Section 4.8 states what the evidence supports.

As before, an improvement counts only against a conventional or unmodified strip run in the same study. This section draws on 99 studies, 16 of them read in full, after the exclusion of six papers in which the strip was used only to screen antibodies or peptides (rule E9); the figures of the others are given as their abstracts state them (Tables D1a to D1f and D4).

### 4.2 Giving the complex more time: flow control

The simplest limit outside the label is the time that the sample spends in front of the capture line. In a conventional strip the liquid crosses the line in seconds, and slowing it lets labelled complexes bind more completely. The designs of this group do it with barriers, geometry or a change of direction, and they give consistent, moderate gains.

Barriers that delay the front are the most common. For the SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain, a hydrophobic cellulose barrier placed in the flow path (a delayed lateral flow design) lowered the LOD to 0.11 ng/mL, 9.1-fold below the conventional strip, with a 2.6-fold higher slope sensitivity; in 12 throat swabs, 8 of them RT-PCR-positive, the sensitivity was 87.5% and the specificity 100% (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-022-05467-3). The barrier can also be dissolvable. Sugar barriers, placed between the conjugate pad and the test line and between the test and control lines, delayed the wicking of a SARS-CoV-2 nucleic acid strip by 10 to 280 s depending on the sugar content (10% was optimal) and lowered the LOD from 2.5 nM to 1 nM with one barrier and 0.5 nM with two, 2.5- and 5-fold, with a synthetic ORF1ab target and no clinical samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.carbpol.2021.118259). A water-soluble polymer wall of 15 wt% dextran methacrylate, 3 mm wide, placed behind the test line lengthened the capillary time to about 400 s and gave an LOD of 50 pg/mL for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleoprotein, 5- to 20-fold better than three commercial antigen kits on Omicron nucleoprotein; seven inactivated variants were detected and five other respiratory viruses were not, with a coefficient of variation below 20% at 500 pg/mL and below 10% at 2000 pg/mL, and no patient samples were tested (DOI: 10.3390/bios14060305). A cotton intermembrane, a cheap layer placed between pads, shows the same effect from another angle. The LoD (the lowest concentration with 19 of 20 replicates reactive) fell from 7.5 ng/mL for the first prototype to 3.75 ng/mL when the test-line antibody was doubled, and to 2.0 ng/mL with the cotton layer, which gave the same two-fold gain as raising the gold conjugate concentration, at an estimated 35% lower reagent cost; purified antigen and inactivated virus were the only samples (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-31641-5). The pattern across the four studies is consistent: each lengthening of the contact time buys 2- to 9-fold, and the cheapest designs buy the same as more reagent.

Geometry and direction work on the same limit. A tapered strip for dengue virus NS1 reached 4.9 ng/mL, about 11-fold better than previously reported (DOI: 10.1063/1.5035113); a sponge shunt for hepatitis B virus nucleic acid improved the analytical sensitivity without a stated value (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-017-01558-x); a two-dimensional paper network for influenza haemagglutinin reached 4.45 x 10^2 TCID50/mL in spiked paediatric nasal swabs (DOI: 10.1039/c8lc00691a); a paper-hydrogel hybrid detected dengue virus RNA in clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/adhm.201600920); and femtosecond-laser micromachined channels in nitrocellulose were applied to respiratory viruses and SARS-CoV-2 without values in the abstract (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-74407-3). Sending the sample through the membrane, rather than along it, changes the contact geometry altogether. Vertical-flow cellulose assays were built for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies and antigen (DOI: 10.1021/acssensors.1c00235; DOI: 10.1039/d2lc00073c) and for influenza A (H1N1), where a vertical-flow paper immunosensor with a different pore sample pad reached 1.34 PFU/mL in buffer and 2.27 PFU/mL in saliva (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2018.10.008). The most careful comparison, for Rift Valley fever virus nucleoprotein, used 40 nm gold with the same antibody pair in both formats. In buffer the vertical-flow LOD was 0.039 ng/mL against 4 ng/mL for the lateral-flow prototype, about 100-fold, but in 10-fold diluted serum it was 0.078 ng/mL, equal to 0.78 ng/mL in undiluted serum against 2 ng/mL for the prototype, which is the 2.5-fold of the abstract; the serum background had absorbed most of the gain, and cross-reactivity with other biothreat antigens was minimal (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.00341-25). A thermochromic strip for dengue virus serotype 2 NS1 converted the photothermal heat of 12 nm gold nanospheres into a colour change of a thermochromic sheet and reached a 4-fold lower LOD than a typical visual readout; the final reading is a colour but its source is heat, so it is flagged (DOI: 10.1039/d3nr01858j). Low-cost fabrication addresses manufacturing rather than sensitivity: manually developed SARS-CoV-2 IgM/IgG strips (sensitivity 93.3% and 96.6%) (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.2c07014), 3D-printed and foil-based nanoimprinted chips for SARS-CoV-2 serology (DOI: 10.18063/ijb.v7i4.399; DOI: 10.3390/bios15040229), a centrifugal microfluidic chip for Ebola virus-like particles (DOI: 10.1039/d3ay00073g) and a cellulose-based strip for SARS-CoV-2 anti-S antibodies, run on 500-fold diluted serum over 10 ng/mL to 1 ug/mL (DOI: 10.1007/s10570-022-04808-y). The limit addressed at this level is contact time; the cost is the one-step simplicity of a conventional strip, and the gains are measured against the authors' own strips (Table D1a). Slowing the liquid, however, cannot add molecules that are not in the sample, and the next group of studies does.

### 4.3 Bringing more of the target to the line

When the target is scarce the limit is not time but number: too few molecules reach the line. The most effective answer in this section is to concentrate the target before the strip, and the best of these studies leave the strip and the label untouched, so that the whole gain can be attributed to the front end. Their gains are also the clearest example in this review of what hardware buys.

The first family is electrical. A nanoelectrokinetic preconcentrator coupled to a commercial SARS-CoV-2 IgG/IgM strip concentrated antibodies more than 30-fold on rolled paper discs under an electric field of about 70 V/cm, with 95.7% extraction efficiency on the right paper, and gave a 32-fold lower LOD: the signal appeared at 1/6,400 instead of 1/200 dilution of a vaccinated serum, and four samples that the commercial strip missed became detectable (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114385). The principle was extended to a nanoelectrokinetic strip with a Nafion-cellulose gel for the nucleocapsid protein, with a 32.5-fold enhancement and 100% sensitivity and specificity against 66.7% sensitivity for the conventional strip in a clinical evaluation (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2025.117357), and to electrokinetic focusing by ion concentration polarization for the spike protein (DOI: 10.1039/d3ay00990d). Electricity can also drive the assembly of the immune complex itself. For antibodies to hepatitis B surface antigen and to hepatitis C virus, an electrophoretic step driven by a 9 V battery for 3 minutes lowered the LOD from 625 ng/mL for a conventional strip in serum to 0.6 ng/mL, about 1000-fold. The decomposition is instructive: the conventional strip reached 4.9 ng/mL in buffer, so the electrophoresis itself accounted for 8-fold, and the other roughly 130-fold came from removing the background IgG that competes with the specific antibody in serum. In 181 sera the diagnostic sensitivity rose from 75% to 98% for anti-HBs and from 65% to 96% for anti-HCV, against instrumental tests, with 3 uL of finger-prick blood and a result in under 10 minutes (DOI: 10.1021/acssensors.3c00130).

The second family is physical. A red-blood-cell-membrane-coated nanoporous aluminium oxide membrane enriched SARS-CoV-2 virus, nucleocapsid protein and IgG under 3 bar in 3 minutes, again in front of a commercial strip; the LOD improved up to 20-fold, and in 62 individuals the sensitivity of the strip rose from 14.29% to 88.1% with 100% specificity, although some low-viral-load samples were made by diluting positive swabs and the device needs a pressure source and 3 mL of sample (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-37018-6). A non-powered preconcentrator, with no electric field, enriched the nucleocapsid protein in front of a commercial antigen strip in 10 minutes and improved the LOD more than 10-fold: 0.1 ng/mL gave no visible line without it and a visible line with it, and RT-PCR confirmed about 10-fold enrichment (change in Ct above 3); the only clinical test was one patient nasal sample at two dilutions, read by five trained engineers against a colour chart (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114965). For large volumes, osmosis and agglutination do the same. An osmotic processor with a polymer concentrated a 10 mL urine sample about 100-fold in 0.5 hours, matched a reference at 100-fold concentration in front of a commercial SARS-CoV-2 strip, and was estimated by mass spectrometry to raise the nucleocapsid protein about 200-fold, but only spiked urine was used and the text gives no LOD value (DOI: 10.3389/fbioe.2022.884271). An agglutination system with biotinylated antibody and streptavidin forms aggregates that are filtered and read on paper. Going from 50 uL to 2 mL lowered the LoD 10-fold, from 100 to 10 pg/mL, where the 40-fold increase in volume predicted 40-fold, and the LoD in spiked saliva equalled that in buffer; gamma-irradiated virus was detected at 250 genome copies/uL, with no patient samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2023.341674). For HIV nucleic acid, a dialysis-based concentrator gave a 10-fold signal gain (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2016.02.017).

The third family uses magnetic particles, which concentrate the target and can also colour the line. The plant virus studies are the cleanest, because they separate the contributions. For potato virus X, magnetic concentration alone lowered the visual limit 2-fold, and so did the aggregation of magnetic particles with gold particles, which increases the visibility of the label; together they gave 0.25 ng/mL against 8 ng/mL for the strip with gold alone, 32-fold, in buffer and in spiked leaf extract, with an instrumental LOD of 0.26 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2017.12.023). Two 2-fold gains that make a 32-fold gain are the clearest evidence in this review that enrichment strategies are not additive. A barcode strip with a magnetic label and three test zones set three cut-offs for the same virus, and magnetic concentration (x10) lowered them from 3, 30 and 150 ng/mL to 0.5, 9 and 30 ng/mL, 6-, 3.3- and 5-fold, with complete agreement with a commercial test on plant samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2016.11.025). For SARS-CoV-2, a magnetic-focus-enhanced strip detected 400 PFU/mL in buffer and 1200 PFU/mL in saliva, with sensitivity 66.7% and specificity 100% on nasal swabs (DOI: 10.3390/bios11120488), and for the hepatitis B virus preS2 antigen a secondary-antibody magnetic nanoprobe gave sensitivity 93.3% and specificity 90% on 25 clinical sera (DOI: 10.3390/bios10110161).

The last family needs no hardware at all: it rearranges the order of the reaction. For potato virus Y, mixing the gold conjugate with the sample for only 30 s before the strip, instead of drying the conjugate on the pad, lowered the calculated LOD from 330 ng/mL to 5.4 ng/mL, 60-fold as stated by the authors, but the figures compare a visual limit with a calculated one: visual to visual the limit fell from 330 to 12 ng/mL, about 27-fold; an ELISA gave 4 ng/mL and the quantitative results correlated with it at 0.891 (DOI: 10.3390/s18113975). Performing the antigen capture outside the strip had a larger effect still. A pipetting-based immunoassay, in which antibody-coated magnetic beads capture the antigen in a pipette tip, used before a commercial lateral flow kit for influenza A nucleoprotein, lowered the LOD from 10^4 to 10^2 EID50 per reaction, 100-fold; on 11 RT-PCR-positive bird faecal samples the strip alone found 6, and with capture it found five more, at the price of one false negative and one false positive (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-019-53083-8).

Concentration shows the largest gains in this section, from 3-fold to 1000-fold, without changing the label. Its cost is hardware (electrodes, pressure, magnets), larger sample volumes and, in most cases, little patient evidence: where the gain was measured, it was often on spiked or reference samples, and its decomposition (as in the electrophoretic and magnetic studies) shows that part of what is called enrichment is the removal of interference or the arrangement of the reaction (Table D1b).

### 4.4 Getting the sample into the strip

Before any concentration, the sample must reach the strip in a usable form, and several studies treat the sample itself as a design variable. A pretreatment with TCEP and bovine serum albumin made sputum usable for lateral flow detection of porcine epidemic diarrhea virus and MERS coronavirus (DOI: 10.4110/in.2021.21.e11). A gargle with a bean extract (Beanguard) replaced swab collection for SARS-CoV-2 antigen tests, with a sensitivity of 97.8% and a specificity of 100% in 45 patients tested within six days of onset, in a study of 102 patients (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.01614-21). A porous microneedle patch collected sample for an IgM/IgG test for SARS-CoV-2 (IgM 3 ng/mL, IgG 7 ng/mL) (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-022-14725-6), and a new diluent improved a SARS-CoV-2 IgG test (sensitivity 91%, specificity 100% on 25 positive and 25 negative samples) (DOI: 10.1007/s13337-021-00669-4). The saliva results of the foot-and-mouth studies in Section 3 show why this matters: a sample matrix can inhibit a test that works in buffer. Together these studies show that sample handling is itself a design variable, at the level of one study each and at the cost of extra reagents or devices (Table D1c).

### 4.5 What the strip can recognise: binders and antigens made for the strip

Slower flow, concentration and a better sample can all be wasted if the binder does not recognise the target, and the most numerous group of studies in this section develops binders and antigens for a new virus. They continue the line of Section 1.6, and as there, the abstracts rarely give a comparison with a conventional antibody.

Antibody pairs and fragments come first. Monoclonal pairs or fragments were developed for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.27520; DOI: 10.1371/journal.pntd.0010311; DOI: 10.3390/microorganisms11102422; DOI: 10.1093/jaoacint/qsaf105), for the hepatitis C virus core antigen (10 ng/strip and 0.3125 ng/mL, with a sensitivity of 92.0% and a specificity of 100% in the second study) (DOI: 10.3390/ph17081022; DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-54470-8), for the feline leukaemia virus p27 antigen (DOI: 10.1016/j.ab.2025.116013), for the Lassa virus nucleoprotein, with lineage-dependent detection and specificity 100% on 60 human samples (DOI: 10.1128/jcm.00071-26), for the orthoebolavirus glycoprotein, which detected Ebola virus in non-human primates at day 4 (DOI: 10.1038/s44298-026-00209-1), for the Nipah and Hendra viruses (diagnostic specificity 94.4%) (DOI: 10.3390/v17071021), and for mpox and other orthopoxviruses with four monoclonal antibodies to the A27 protein (3 x 10^5 pfu/mL) (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.02456-24). Affimers were used for the Crimean-Congo haemorrhagic fever virus nucleoprotein (DOI: 10.1371/journal.pntd.0008364).

Non-antibody binders follow. Six aptamer-aptamer pairs for the SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain were compared, the best reaching sensitivity 100% and specificity 93.3% on swabs (DOI: 10.1093/jaoacint/qsae004); a competitive aptamer device for the spike protein reached 51.81 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2024.126825), an aptamer lateral flow biosensor for the nucleocapsid protein 500 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1016/j.virol.2025.110688), and aptamer cocktails in three models sensitivity of 96.6% to 100% with specificity 100% (DOI: 10.1186/s12879-025-11751-3). For dengue virus serotype 2 NS1 an aptamer-antibody strip reached 5.2 ng/mL in buffer and 6.1 ng/mL in spiked serum (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-29869-4). Nanobodies were used in a spike S1 strip improved by streptavidin-biotin capture (sensitivity 95.21% and specificity 99.29% against 91.66% and 98.57% for the earlier strip) (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-57230-8), for SARS-CoV-2 and MERS-CoV (DOI: 10.1021/acssynbio.4c00592) and for Zika virus NS1 (25 ng/mL in buffer, 1 ng/mL in urine) (DOI: 10.1021/acssynbio.4c00819). A competitive strip with an affinity-engineered receptor-binding domain/ACE2 pair detected betacoronavirus S1 at 1.66 ug/mL (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2026.118909); helical affibodies replaced the antibody in a spike strip (DOI: 10.1021/jacs.4c17452); the human ACE2 receptor served as capture for the spike S1 antigen (1.86 x 10^5 copies/mL) on clinical specimens (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2020.112715); glycan recognition was used for SARS-CoV-2 in a flow-through device and in a glycopolymer-capture system for SARS-CoV-2 and other betacoronaviruses (DOI: 10.1021/acssensors.1c01470; DOI: 10.1021/acscentsci.1c01080); and high-affinity haemagglutinin-binding proteins detected intact influenza virus particles (3.54 x 10^7 and 1.34 x 10^7 CEID50/mL) (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.7b00769).

How the binder is presented to the target matters as much as which binder it is. Orienting the antibody on the particle improved a SARS-CoV-2 strip, which detected 5 x 10^4 copies/mL and gave 100% accuracy on 19 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2022.133245); engineered nucleocapsid antigens were designed to improve the stability of SARS-CoV-2 strips (DOI: 10.3390/biom13101524); an antibody against a drug-resistance-associated neuraminidase mutant allowed detection of oseltamivir-resistant influenza virus (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-020-17246-w); genotype-specific antibodies were developed for human bocavirus 1 and 2 (DOI: 10.1007/s12519-023-00697-8); and an extracellular-vesicle capture design links HIV to vesicle markers in blood (DOI: 10.1016/j.vesic.2025.100084). Antigen detection kits with recombinant or monoclonal nucleocapsid antibodies detected SARS-CoV-2 Omicron (62.5 TCID50/mL on simulated positive samples) and its sublineages, Wuhan and Delta (DOI: 10.3390/vaccines11020303; DOI: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e20913). These studies establish what a strip can recognise; whether it then works depends on the format and the sample, which many do not report in the abstract (Table D1d). The cost of a new binder is its development, and the abstracts rarely give a within-study comparison with a conventional antibody.

### 4.6 How the reaction is arranged and read: formats, functional assays and DNA structures

The last group asks how the immunoreaction itself is organised and what the colour is made to mean. The clearest evidence that format alone can change the limit by two orders of magnitude comes from a comparison of three SARS-CoV-2 antibody-detection formats run with the same receptor-binding domain antigen and 30 nm gold. The format with protein A immobilised in the line and labelled antigen in the conjugate gave a 15- and 255-fold lower detection limit (10 ng/mL) than the other two on pooled positive serum, while still detecting 22 of 22 patient sera correctly (DOI: 10.3390/bios13070750). A two-stage label incorporation into immune complexes (a serodiagnosis model) raised sensitivity from 62.5% to 100% at specificity 100% (DOI: 10.3390/bios12070434). A half-strip format that uses open reagents served for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein (0.65 ng/mL) and for Rift Valley fever virus antibodies (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.0c01975; DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics12112664).

Functional and serological formats read a different question. Neutralizing antibodies against SARS-CoV-2 were measured with a competitive strip from a finger prick (DOI: 10.1016/j.jcv.2021.105024), a strip capturing neutralized receptor-binding domain (DOI: 10.1016/j.crmeth.2022.100273) and a strip with an LOD of 2 ug/mL, sensitivity 97.80% and specificity 100% on 684 uninfected and clinical samples (DOI: 10.2147/ijn.s408921). Serological and multiplex tests include a four-serotype dengue panel with serotype-specific NS1 detection (333 to 22,500 TCID50/mL in plasma and serum) (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.02796-22), a species-independent double-antigen test for Rift Valley fever virus antibodies (DOI: 10.3390/v18030316), a semi-quantitative multi-line SARS-CoV-2 antibody test that classifies high and low titres (DOI: 10.1039/d3tb02895j), a strip with HPV16 virus-like-particle capture to determine HPV16 vaccination status (DOI: 10.1016/j.vaccine.2016.09.021) and a dengue NS1 capture assay with serotype specificity of 100% (DOI: 10.3390/bios15120802). A caution applies: the reference standards of these formats differ (neutralization tests, ELISA), so their agreement figures cannot be compared with antigen strips.

Nucleic acid targets are read on strips by hybridisation, and DNA structures can add signal without amplifying the target. For SARS-CoV-2, a triplex enhanced assay reached 0.01 nM without amplification (DOI: 10.3390/ijms232315258), a hybridisation chain reaction strip 200 copies/uL against 500 to 2000 for commercial unamplified strips (DOI: 10.1021/acsinfecdis.2c00472), a net-shaped DNA nanostructure strip covered 10^3 to 10^8 copies/mL (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c03698) and protein-based probe anchoring reached about 0.2 nM (DOI: 10.3390/mi14101936). For influenza A (H1N1), an M13 nanoscaffold gave an amplification-free strip at 1 pM (DOI: 10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2024.136945), and for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus a DNA nanostructure with multivalent binding gave 97.06% accuracy on 102 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/adhm.202504037). A probe-gated silica nanoparticle strip detected SARS-CoV-2 at 0.73 pg/mL in unprocessed swabs (sensitivity 100%, specificity 83% against RT-qPCR) (DOI: 10.1002/open.202300120) (Table D1e).

### 4.7 Strips for one virus: the baseline of this section

A large share of the records are strips developed for one virus without a deliberate change to the label, many of them evaluated on patient samples. They matter as the baseline: they show what the combination of binder, format and sample achieves without signal engineering. Their abstracts rarely give a within-study change in the colour signal, and their values are listed in Table D1f.

For SARS-CoV-2, antigen prototypes include a saliva strip tested on 111 hospitalised patients (DOI: 10.48101/ujms.v127.8207), a prototype compared with BinaxNOW (DOI: 10.3390/v14122609), a spike strip with a specificity of 98% (25 ng/test) (DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2023.1111644), a nucleocapsid antigen test that meets the WHO target product profile at 4 TCID50/swab (DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0258819), a visual platform whose reading correlated with a reader (R2 0.9571; sensitivity 98%, specificity 100% on 170 swabs) (DOI: 10.1039/d3ay02075d) and an in-house antibody kit tested on 300 samples (DOI: 10.1186/s13568-023-01620-0). For other viruses: an influenza A and B strip with new monoclonal antibodies, 4- to 8-fold more sensitive than commercial tests, with a sensitivity of 89% (A) and 83% (B) and specificity 100% on 101 swabs (DOI: 10.1039/d1ra01361k); a paper-based dengue NS1 device on 219 sera (sensitivity 88.89%, specificity 86.67%, kappa 0.7522 against nested PCR) (DOI: 10.1016/j.ijid.2021.05.007); and standard and rapid antigen assays for human metapneumovirus (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.70374).

### 4.8 What the evidence supports

The gains in the colour signal in this section come from the strip and the sample, not from the label. Within-study changes range from about 2-fold to 1000-fold (Table D4), and they separate into three levels. Flow control gains 2- to 9-fold, and up to 20-fold against commercial kits, by lengthening the contact time, and the cheapest designs buy the same as more reagent. Concentrating the target gains 3- to 1000-fold, with the largest gains from electrical and physical front ends that leave the strip and label untouched, but with hardware, volume and in most cases thin patient evidence. And changing the order or the format of the reaction gains 15- to 255-fold, from pre-incubation, capture outside the strip and the arrangement of the line and the conjugate, without any extra device. The decompositions that some studies allow qualify these figures: the 1000-fold of the electrophoretic assay is largely the removal of background IgG in serum, the 100-fold of the vertical-flow strip falls to 2.5-fold in serum, the two 2-fold gains of magnetic concentration and aggregation make 32-fold only in combination, and the 60-fold of pre-incubation is 27-fold on matched visual limits. Section 4 therefore shows that the gains reported for the strip cannot be compared across studies without knowing how the limit was read and in which matrix.

Three limitations recur. Many studies use reference or spiked samples, and clinical samples are the exception among the engineering papers; the clinical test of the non-powered preconcentrator was one diluted patient sample, that of the delayed-flow strip 12 swabs and that of the nanotrap 62 individuals. Several studies give a fold-improvement and not the accuracy of the strip on patients. And the antibody and binder papers rarely report the matrix, the number of samples or the reference method in the abstract.
