# 3.1 Gold nanoparticle labels: engineering the colorimetric signal

## 3.1.1 Where the colour of a strip comes from

In a colorimetric lateral flow immunoassay (LFIA) the result is a coloured line. It appears when the labelled immune complex is captured at the test line and the label absorbs enough visible light to be distinguished from the white nitrocellulose membrane. The intensity of that line is set by three quantities: the number of labelled particles that reach and are retained at the line, the optical effect of each captured particle, and the contrast between the line and its background as perceived by the eye or by a reader. Gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) became the standard label because they absorb visible light strongly, are simple to synthesise and conjugate readily to antibodies (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Their limitation is a comparatively high limit of detection (LOD): when only a few particles are captured, the line is too faint to see (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229).

Each of the three quantities can be addressed separately, and the literature on gold labels can be read as a sequence of attempts to do so. The first group of studies optimises the standard particle and its surface so that more active particles reach the line (Section 3.1.2). The second enlarges or builds up the gold after it has been captured (Section 3.1.3). The third designs particles with a larger optical effect each, by assembling, shaping or alloying the gold (Sections 3.1.4 and 3.1.5). A further group changes what the particle carries, the binder and the antigen (Section 3.1.6), or the format and optics of the strip itself (Sections 3.1.7 and 3.1.8). Conventional colloidal gold strips that apply none of these changes serve as the baseline against which all of them are measured (Section 3.1.9), and Section 3.1.10 states what the evidence supports. Throughout, a change is treated as an improvement only when the same study runs a conventional or unmodified strip beside it; fold-changes from different studies rest on different baselines, matrices and readouts and are not ranked against each other. Where a label also provides a surface-enhanced Raman scattering (SERS), photothermal or fluorescence readout, only the colour channel is considered here; the second-channel figures are listed in Tables A1g and A4 for reference. The target virus is named for every method, and the numerical details of every study are given in Tables A1a to A1g.

## 3.1.2 The standard particle: size, surface and conjugation

The first design decision is the particle itself. Larger AuNPs carry more colour per particle, but they migrate more slowly through the membrane and may not reach the test line in sufficient numbers, so size is a compromise (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229; DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). Two studies tested this compromise directly. For the hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), particles were grown stepwise from a seed to diameters between 34.2 and 137.8 nm; those of 42.7 nm gave the strongest test-line signal at 10, 100 and 500 ng/mL of antigen and, in human whole blood spiked with 500 ng/mL HBsAg, a clearer line than particles made by the Turkevich-Frens method or a commercial HBV kit, although no LOD value is reported (DOI: 10.3390/s16122154). For dengue virus NS1, 40 nm colloidal gold, 80 nm gold nanospheres and 150 nm gold nanoshells were compared with the same antibody: the 80 nm particles gave the most distinct and consistent lines, whereas the 150 nm shells produced false-positive signals in every buffer tested (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics16030395). Both studies point to an optimum in the 40 to 80 nm range rather than to the largest particle that can be made.

How the antibody is attached determines how many of the captured particles are active. Random adsorption leaves part of the antibodies with their binding sites turned towards the gold. On 100 nm AuNPs for HBsAg, a thiolated hydrazide ligand attached the antibody through its Fc region, so that the antigen-binding sites face outward. This oriented coupling required about seven times less antibody (0.2 against 1.5 mg per pmol of particles), shortened conjugation from 90 to 10 minutes and lowered the LOD from 5 to 2 ng/mL relative to carbodiimide coupling in the same study; with a portable strip reader, 35 positive and 15 negative sera agreed with a chemiluminescence immunoassay (R2 = 0.9527) without false positives (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2021.338623).

The surface of colloidal gold also interacts with the sample, and this interaction can be exploited or must be controlled. In a colloidal gold strip for SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG, plasma anticoagulated with EDTA-K2 gave stronger lines and more IgM-positive results than serum from the same 112 patients (for instance 17 weak and 15 clear IgM positives in plasma that were negative in serum), and 40 IgG samples that were merely positive in serum read as strongly positive in plasma. The authors attribute the effect to chelation and aggregation of the colloidal gold by EDTA-K2, which sodium citrate and lithium heparin did not cause; no false positives arose in 200 healthy controls, and EDTA-K2 plasma agreed best with the chemiluminescence reference (DOI: 10.2147/ijn.s281594). The protein layer that adsorbs onto the probe in biological fluids, the protein corona, has a similar influence. For Zika virus NS1 on gold nanostars, strips run in buffer or in bovine serum albumin showed false-positive lines, whereas strips run in human serum or on serum-pretreated nitrocellulose did not, and a preformed corona restored the specificity of antibody-antigen binding (LOD 1.6 nM, effective dissociation constant 12.7 nM in serum) (DOI: 10.1021/acs.bioconjchem.6b00523). For dengue virus serotype 2 NS1, a preformed apolipoprotein A1 corona gave an LOD of 1.24 nM, whereas a preformed albumin corona reduced the test-line intensity by 33% at a given antigen concentration so that no meaningful LOD could be determined (DOI: 10.3390/pharmaceutics14112439). A systematic comparison of probe chemistries in albumin and serum, with mouse IgG, Zika NS1 and dengue NS1 as model pairs, showed that physisorbed, PEG-backfilled probes aggregated and lost signal as antigen rose, that covalently grafted probes (NHS, hydrazide) remained stable although the NHS probes performed worst in serum, and that sonication or a single pre-wash restored the signal (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.0c08628). Together with an earlier comparison of conjugation strategies for dengue NS1 (DOI: 10.1080/15321819.2016.1269338), these studies explain why nominally identical strips perform differently in different matrices, even though they report few diagnostic data (Table A1a).

Several groups have replaced the citrate-reduced gold sphere with a related particle or placed a different ligand on it. Hollow gold nanoparticles have a molar extinction coefficient (1.48 x 10^10 M^-1 cm^-1) that the authors report to be much larger than that of solid particles of the same size; as a label for HIV-1 p24 antigen, with the antibody adsorbed at one main binding site, they gave darker blue lines and higher optical density in serum than the commercial Alere HIV-1/2 Ag/Ab Combo test (LOD 0.02 ng/mL), a sensitivity about twice as high, and detected one of six preclinical samples that the commercial strip missed (DOI: 10.1039/d4ra05277c). Laser-ablated gold, synthesised in water without chemical reductants, detected 10 ng of SARS-CoV-2 spike antigen on a dot-format dipstick, as did commercial gold conjugated with the same antibody, so the method of synthesis did not change the colour result (DOI: 10.5599/admet.2079). Gold magnetic nanoparticles in a TORCH IgM strip (rubella virus, cytomegalovirus and herpes simplex virus) gave 100% sensitivity and specificity in 162 sera and are reported to be more sensitive than commercial colloidal gold strips, without a stated value (DOI: 10.3892/ijmm.2015.2333). Gold-oligonucleotide nanoconstructs designed for conserved enterovirus sequences detected 96% of known enteroviruses (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070238).

The ligand on the gold can also be chosen to recognise a functional property of the virus rather than an epitope. An oseltamivir derivative immobilised on gold binds the neuraminidase of oseltamivir-resistant (H275Y) influenza virus much more strongly than that of wild-type virus; the particles turn from deep red to purple in the presence of the resistant enzyme, and a lateral flow strip built on them detected the resistant virus at 5 x 10^2 to 10^3 PFU per test (1 x 10^4 PFU/mL), also in mixtures with wild-type virus (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-018-31311-x). For dengue-1 RNA, dextrin-capped gold carried a DNA reporter probe in a sandwich hybridisation strip; the visual cut-off, defined as the lowest concentration giving a visible red band, was 0.01 uM of synthetic target, and viral RNA extracted from pooled human sera and amplified by nucleic acid sequence-based amplification (NASBA) was detected at 1.2 x 10^4 PFU/mL in 20 minutes, a figure that depends on the amplification step (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics9030074). Bacterial protein A and protein G served as flexible detectors for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies on 105 sera (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-022-03939-2), and a recombinant streptavidin fusion protein served as the reporter in a hepatitis C virus antibody strip (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.22701).

The most direct way to strengthen the line at this level is to attach several particles to each captured target. A single-stranded DNA-binding protein, the RPA70A domain of replication protein A, conjugated to AuNPs binds the single-stranded region of an aptamer, so that several particles decorate one aptamer. For the influenza A nucleoprotein the line was visible to the naked eye at 0.01 ng/mL in buffer, the LOD in tenfold-diluted human nasal fluid was 0.26 pg/mL, and a conventional two-step design with streptavidin-conjugated AuNPs and a biotinylated aptamer, run in the same study, was visible only at 10 ng/mL, a thousandfold difference (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.9b01827).

The limit addressed at this level is therefore the number of active particles at the line. Where a like-for-like comparison exists, the gains are the thousandfold of the multi-particle aptamer design, the 2.5-fold of oriented conjugation and the twofold of hollow gold against a commercial strip; for most other surface modifications the studies are mechanistic and give no value against a baseline strip.

## 3.1.3 Making more of the captured gold: enlargement and assembly at the line

Once the immune complex has formed, the captured particles can serve as seeds for further metal deposition. Enlarging them, or making them more irregular, increases the absorption of each captured event and darkens the line (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229). The clearest comparison of the available chemistries placed silver enhancement, a gold-after-silver step based on galvanic replacement, and direct gold enhancement on a single strip format for the SARS-CoV-2 receptor-binding domain, with the same antibodies and the same AuNPs. Gold enhancement gave the lowest LOD, more than two orders of magnitude below the unenhanced strip, and used stable reagents, but it introduced nonspecific colouring and required an optical instrument to reach the lowest value (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229).

The same principle has been implemented in several practical forms. Growing 20 nm particles into much larger ones after the assay turned most of the false negatives of a conventional strip for potato virus X into positives in potato leaf extracts (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-018-3052-7). For the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, the enhancement reagents (Au3+ and hydroxylamine) were dried onto an additional pad placed between the sample and conjugate pads, so that the enhancement occurs during the normal run without any extra operation. This pad lowered the naked-eye limit from 5.0 to 0.5 ng/mL relative to the same strip without it, gave a calibration-curve LOD of 0.10 ng/mL, and in 207 clinical saliva samples the strip reached a sensitivity of 96.10% and a specificity of 99.23% against RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2023.133898). Gold nanoflowers combined with gold staining have been applied to the same antigen in buffer and artificial saliva (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-024-06502-1), and for two sugarcane mosaic-associated viruses a positively charged cysteamine-gold label followed by nanogold signal enhancement extended detection from a linear range of 10^-6 to 10^-9 g/mL down to 10^-12 g/mL (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-022-07950-6).

An alternative to growing the particles is to assemble more of them at the line. Layer-by-layer deposition of AuNPs through biotin-streptavidin binding, driven by electrophoresis, lowered the LOD of an HBsAg strip more than 60-fold. The design is a two-stage workflow in which the conventional strip is read first and enhancement is applied only to samples that appear negative or weakly positive; in 118 clinical samples this increased diagnostic sensitivity against a laboratory chemiluminescence assay without loss of specificity, at the cost of a small power supply, a holder and an additional reagent step (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202215548). In SARS-CoV-2 serology, a complex of antibody-gold and antigen-gold conjugates increased the test-line signal over antigen-gold alone and brought the visual limit to the cut-off of the reference ELISA (DOI: 10.1002/gch2.202200008).

The gains at this level are consistently large, from tenfold for the single-step pad to more than two orders of magnitude for gold enhancement, and they are achieved with the same antibodies and the same particles as the baseline strip. Their price is additional reagents, time or background, and, for the lowest values, dependence on an instrument (Table A1b). This limits their use where the strip must work without equipment and motivates the alternative strategy: designing a particle that is brighter from the outset.

## 3.1.4 Particles with a larger optical effect: clusters, dimers, shells and composites

The optical response of a gold particle is governed by its size, its shape, its composition and its distance from neighbouring particles. Each of these parameters has been used to increase the effect of a single captured particle.

Clusters place several particles at each binding event. The obstacle is plasmon coupling: particles closer than about 10 nm shift their absorption towards the near-infrared and the visible colour fades. Clusters built around a streptavidin core, in which biotinylated antibodies hold the particles about 15 nm apart, avoid this coupling and retain the red colour; for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, a cluster of 40 nm particles outperformed single particles, whereas a cluster of 40 and 15 nm particles did not (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Gold dendrimer nanochains, which show a red-to-blue shift, have been tested for HIV-1 p24 (DOI: 10.1039/c7tb01394a). Core-satellite particles, in which a silica core carries many small gold satellites (SiO2@Au), lowered the LOD by two orders of magnitude relative to 40 nm colloidal gold in the same assay for recombinant SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein and inactivated virus, although the visual limit remained much higher than the calculated LOD (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.2c13303).

Dimers exploit the opposite regime. Two particles joined with a sub-nanometre gap couple strongly and display two plasmon peaks, which gives a stronger signal than single spheres. For porcine epidemic diarrhea virus, such dimers gave a clearly lower naked-eye LOD than a commercial strip and full agreement with RT-PCR on faecal samples; the authors note that dimers are difficult to prepare at scale and that their long-term stability has not been established (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06013-5).

Hollow alloy shells broaden the absorption band. Silver-rich Ag-Au nanoshells have two plasmon peaks and a larger integrated absorbance than AuNPs of the same size; in a bovine viral diarrhea virus strip they gave a lower visual LOD than the authors' own AuNP strip with the same antibodies and agreed well with a commercial ELISA on field samples (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-026-08241-x).

Composite particles combine gold with a second material that adds a function. In an avian influenza H7 strip, Au/Fe3O4 core-shell particles gave a naked-eye limit of 10^3.5 EID50, ten times lower than gold particles carrying the same antibody in the same study, while Fe3O4 alone did not produce a valid strip; 200 clinical samples were tested, of which 7 were positive (DOI: 10.1039/c8ra00185e). Magnetic-gold nanostars for the Rift Valley fever virus nucleoprotein combine a spiked plasmonic shell with magnetic pre-concentration and gave a very large gain over the authors' own gold-sphere strip in buffer and spiked serum, at the cost of a magnet and a pre-incubation step and without testing on patient samples (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.5c21544). Composite plasmonic particles imaged at two wavelengths for the HIV capsid protein give a large gain that depends on an imager rather than on the eye (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114133).

The strongest evidence at this level comes from studies that compare the new particle with a conventional strip made with the same antibodies (clusters, shells, the Au/Fe3O4 composite). Comparisons with commercial strips, as for the dimers, mix the effect of the label with that of the antibodies, and some particles have only been tested in buffer (Table A1c). All of these designs still rely on absorbed colour seen against a white membrane.

## 3.1.5 Gold labels with a second signal: what the colour channel gains

A large and recent group of studies builds structured gold labels that provide two signals at once: the colour of the line, read by eye, and a SERS, photothermal or fluorescence signal, read with an instrument. The headline sensitivity of these papers belongs almost always to the instrumental channel. Their value for the present question lies elsewhere: because most of them report the naked-eye limit of the same strip, and many run a conventional gold strip beside it, they show which structural changes to the gold improve the colour itself. The second-channel figures are given in Table A1g and are not repeated here.

Shaping the gold is the most effective of these changes. A gold nanocrown, a gold shell decorated with external nanospheres, gave an intense dark-blue line for the SARS-CoV-2 spike S1 protein: against gold nanospheres in the same study, the line was visible by eye down to 0.1 ng/mL instead of 10 ng/mL, and the colorimetric LOD was 91.24 pg/mL against 5.9 ng/mL, about 65-fold by calculation (the paper states 100-fold); the antigen was spiked into buffer and saliva (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04361). Gold nanocages, whose size and core-to-shell gap were tuned so that the largest cage was the brightest, gave a colorimetric LOD of 1.8 ng/mL for influenza A (H1N1) nucleoprotein against 12.5 ng/mL for gold spheres, a sevenfold gain, with 1.7 ng/mL in spiked saliva (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.4c05384). One-pot Au@Pt nanostars detected antibodies to the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein by eye at 1 ng/mL, about 100 times lower than colloidal gold strips run in the same study, and the signal retained 93% of its intensity after eight weeks; in sera from 83 vaccinated and 11 unvaccinated volunteers, however, the positive counts are reported for the photothermal channel (56 positives, against 32 for colloidal gold strips), not for colour alone (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2024.342241). Gold nanoantennas, the sub-nanometre-gap dimers of Section 3.1.4 fitted with a Raman reporter, gave a naked-eye LOD of 1.0 x 10^2 TCID50/mL for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus spiked into negative faeces (recoveries 94.9% to 102.1%, n = 6), about 59-fold below the 5.9 x 10^3 TCID50/mL that the paper quotes for traditional colloidal gold strips; this baseline is cited, not measured, and no clinical samples were tested (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2025.117699). Gold nanorods with a tunable absorption peak detected the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein by eye at 2 ng/mL, with 87.5% agreement with nucleic acid testing in 40 nasal swabs (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay00347k); Ag-coated gold nanostars detected influenza A antibody at 0.1 ng/mL by colour (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2024.127351); and Ag@Au nanoplates gave a very low background for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein in 35 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c05787). In a competitive format for SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies, gold-silver alloy hollow nanoshells gave a blue line that fades as antibody rises, with a naked-eye limit of 160 ng/mL; of 107 sera, 79 of 98 vaccinated volunteers showed a neutralising effect and none of 9 unvaccinated did, although no neutralisation assay was run on the same sera (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2023.341102).

Silver-gold core-shell particles combine the stronger absorption of silver with a gold surface that remains stable and easy to conjugate. Ag(MBA)@Au particles with a gold shell about 2 nm thick gave a SARS-CoV-2 IgG line visible by eye at 10^-7 mg/mL in buffer and 10^-6 mg/mL in serum; in 98 vaccinated volunteers the clear colour band identified 26 strong positives, against 11 positives for commercial colloidal gold strips on the same sera and 34 for ELISA (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c01286). The same particles assembled on Fe3O4 gave a visual limit of 10^-8 mg/mL for antibodies to the nucleocapsid protein and identified 49 strong positives among 98 vaccinated volunteers, without a gold-strip comparator (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-022-04437-1). Au@Ag particles gave a colorimetric LOD of 300 pg/mL for the nucleocapsid protein, with 100% negative and 85% positive accuracy in 20 patients and 20 healthy subjects (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2025.344149), and Au@MBA@Ag particles gave visual LODs of 6.25 x 10^2 TCID50/mL for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus and 7.42 x 10^2 copies/uL for porcine rotavirus A in faeces, with 93.3% agreement with RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.saa.2025.125863). Integrating polydopamine into the Au@Ag structure doubled the colour sensitivity relative to plain Au@Ag tags and gave visual limits of 3.3 x 10^5 copies/mL for inactivated H1N1 and 1.23 x 10^5 copies/mL for SARS-CoV-2, five- and twofold better than commercial gold strips; in 32 positive and 4 negative nasopharyngeal swabs the colour band was visible in most positives, and four weak positives were seen only by SERS (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07587-y).

Combining gold with a non-plasmonic material adds a second function while gold provides the colour. Magnetic cores allow the target to be enriched before the strip. High-density gold nanoshells on Fe3O4 nanoclusters lowered the naked-eye limit for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein from 100 ng/mL without enrichment to 1 ng/mL with it, against 40 ng/mL for a self-made colloidal gold strip, and the strip was tested on spiked artificial saliva and 18 real samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2023.115688). Fe3O4-Au heterodimers gave better coloration than ordinary AuNPs in a three-line nucleic acid strip for influenza H1N1, H3N2 and H9N2 (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay01010h); Janus Au(shell)-Fe3O4 particles gave naked-eye LODs of 50 ng/mL for both H3N2 influenza and SARS-CoV-2 in a multiplex strip (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2024.117030); Fe3O4-Au(shell) heterodimers for mpox virus gave a 5 ng/mL naked-eye threshold (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2025.128670); and a Fe3O4/Au/quantum-dot probe carrying wheat germ agglutinin, which binds viral membrane glycoproteins broadly, gave a visual sensitivity of 0.1 to 0.5 ng/mL for SARS-CoV-2 and mpox virus in 33 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/smll.202406053). Layered materials and porous carriers have also been used: 30 nm AuNPs layered on MoS2 nanosheets gave a visual limit of 0.2 ng/mL for the mpox virus A29L antigen against 1 ng/mL for a standard AuNP strip, a fivefold colour gain, tested on spiked throat swabs and lake water (DOI: 10.1016/j.jhazmat.2023.132136); rhodamine B-loaded UiO-66 with AuNPs on its surface gave a colorimetric LOD of 31.2 pg/mL for potato virus S against 462.4 pg/mL for a conventional AuNP strip, about 15-fold (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2026.1876426); dual-signal nanospheres (RNs@Au) detected Ebola virus glycoprotein by eye at 2 ng/mL in 20 minutes (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.7b02222); and a PDA@Au core-shell probe for influenza A (H1N1) gave a visual sensitivity of 93.9% and specificity of 95.8% in 90 throat swabs (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07611-1). Silica cores carrying a layer of gold and quantum dots illustrate that a second signal need not improve the colour at all: for SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG the colorimetric performance was stated to equal that of a traditional gold strip, with bands visible at a 1:10^4 serum dilution, and the colour bands were hard to see in 8 early-stage sera (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.0c03484); for the SARS-CoV-2 S and N proteins the colorimetric limit of 50 pg/mL was five times better than a standard AuNP strip (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.2c21042); and for the mpox virus A29L antigen the colorimetric limit of 0.5 ng/mL equalled that of the conventional AuNP strip (DOI: 10.1186/s12951-023-02215-4). Tris-stabilised AuNPs allowed simultaneous visual detection of porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (1.25 x 10^3 TCID50/mL) and porcine rotavirus (3.13 x 10^2 pg/mL), with 95% and 100% concordance with RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.saa.2024.124670).

Eleven further studies attach a Raman reporter or a lanthanide to the gold without changing its shape, and in these the colour channel behaves as an ordinary gold strip. Gold nanostars with a Raman reporter gave a faintly visible line at 1.02 x 10^3 copies/uL of group A rotavirus, with 95% agreement with RT-PCR in 80 faecal samples from pigs, cattle and poultry (DOI: 10.1016/j.saa.2026.127441). Au/DTNB@Ag/DTNB tags gave a naked-eye limit of 80 pg/mL for human rotavirus, and in 95 clinical faecal samples the strip agreed completely with both RT-qPCR and a commercial colloidal gold strip, so the colour channel offered no gain over the commercial product (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-020-04670-4); the same tags gave a visual limit of 10 ng/mL for West Nile virus NS1 protein (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-021-04857-3). For the SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain, a Raman-tagged gold strip read as a colour strip gave a scanner-quantified limit of 1.2 ng/mL (visible above 1 ng/mL), against 0.4 ng/mL for an ELISA with the same immunoreagents (DOI: 10.3390/bios11120510). For SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG in patient serum, the line on SERS-active gold nanostars was visible by eye at a 1:100 serum dilution, barely at 1:200 and not at 1:1000, and IgG lines in sera taken 3.5 and 4 months after infection were below the visual threshold (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.1c02305). Gold-nanobridged nanogap particles for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein gave a visible line at 0.1 ng/reaction and, when introduced into a commercial rapid antigen kit, left its visual limit unchanged at 0.5 ng/reaction (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07179-w). A digital SERS strip for the same antigen is the one case in which the colour channel of the new strip was less sensitive than commercial strips: only the highest concentration tested (0.1 ug/mL) was visible by eye, whereas three commercial colorimetric tests reached 1 to 10 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1002/smsc.202400259). Magnetic Fe3O4@Au nanotags carrying wheat germ agglutinin captured influenza A virus before the strip; the SERS accuracy in 110 throat swab or sputum samples was 100%, against 81% for a commercial colloidal gold strip, but the visual limit of the strip itself is not stated (DOI: 10.1016/j.nano.2025.102853). Two nucleic acid strips without amplification gave visible lines at 80 pg/mL of HIV-1 DNA, the same limit as a commercial fluorescence kit run in the same study (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2015.11.099), and at about 20 pM of Kaposi's sarcoma-associated herpesvirus DNA on a two-line strip whose second line detected a bacterial marker (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.6b03536). The lanthanide composite, cysteamine-modified gold-europium nanoparticles for H5N1 avian influenza spiked into swab specimens, gave a naked-eye limit of 10 HAU/mL against 20 HAU/mL for commercial gold nanoparticles in the same study, a twofold gain (DOI: 10.3390/ijms23147957).

Taken together, the colour channels of these dual-signal labels give a consistent and modest picture. Where the same study runs a gold baseline, shaped gold gives the largest colour gains (nanostars about 100-fold, nanocrowns about 65-fold, nanocages sevenfold), magnetic enrichment adds 100-fold through the number of particles delivered rather than through the particle itself, and silver-gold, polydopamine, MoS2, metal-organic framework and lanthanide composites give between twofold and 15-fold. Several designs give no colour gain at all, and none of the Raman-tagged strips reports one. In every one of the 22 studies of this group that were read in full, the figure quoted in the abstract belongs to the instrumental channel. The cost of these labels is a multi-step synthesis and, for the sensitivity that the abstracts advertise, an instrument.

## 3.1.6 The binder and the antigen

A brighter label cannot compensate for a weak binder. A parallel line of work therefore replaces the conventional antibody with a recognition element that is smaller, more stable, easier to produce or better suited to a variable target.

Nanobodies, the single-domain fragments of camelid antibodies, are the best documented. In a SARS-CoV-2 spike S1 strip, a nanobody-gold detector with an ACE2-gold capture probe gave an LOD of 0.4 x 10^4 copies/mL for gamma-irradiated virus against 1.6 x 10^4 copies/mL for a monoclonal-antibody version of the same strip, a fourfold gain; in 320 saliva samples with 180 RT-PCR positives, the nanobody strip reached a sensitivity of 97.14% and a specificity of 98.57% for samples with Ct of 30 or below, against 90.04% and 97.86% for the antibody strip (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-37347-y). Multivalent and Y-shaped nanobody assemblies have been reported for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid and spike S1 proteins and for the chikungunya virus E protein, and a nanobody strip has been applied to human norovirus outbreak specimens (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.4c00509; DOI: 10.1039/d4nr02762k; DOI: 10.1039/d5nr02568k; DOI: 10.3390/bios16020132; DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2025.1707358; DOI: 10.1128/msphere.00219-16). Nanobodies can also be displayed on a carrier: yeast cell-wall nanofragments presenting anti-spike nanobodies, linked to AuNPs through the SpyTag/SpyCatcher system, gave a red line for SARS-CoV-2 virions with a concentration-dependent signal that failed below 2 x 10^4 virions/mL, although only one positive and one negative clinical sample were run on the strip (DOI: 10.1038/s41565-023-01415-1).

Aptamers are synthesised chemically and can be produced more reproducibly and cheaply than antibodies (DOI: 10.3390/molecules29081776). A competitive aptamer strip for SARS-CoV-2 spike protein on food packaging used an aptamer selected for broad binding to variant spike proteins, but its sensitivity was below that of other methods and it was tested only with recombinant protein (DOI: 10.3390/molecules29081776). Other aptamer strips for SARS-CoV-2 spike, inactivated virus and nucleocapsid protein were evaluated with inactivated virus or spiked samples (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202107730; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c00554; DOI: 10.1186/s12951-023-02191-9); a pair of sandwich aptamers detected whole avian influenza H5N2 particles with enhanced specificity over other H5Nx viruses (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2019.03.061); an aptamer-based isolation step preceded a strip for grouper nervous necrosis virus (5 ng/mL coat protein) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2020.00886); and a gold-and-aptamer proof of concept was proposed for home detection of SARS-CoV-2 mutants (DOI: 10.1016/j.nano.2022.102590). Two further papers select aptamers for H3N2 and for the Omicron receptor-binding domain without reporting strip data (DOI: 10.1166/jbn.2019.2789; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c01993).

Fully synthetic binders go one step further. A hydrogel copolymer of aromatic amino-acid monomers, screened for affinity to the SARS-CoV-2 spike trimer and then polymerised in situ on AuNPs, acted as an abiotic antibody mimic: the core-shell probe gave a visual limit of 30 ng/reaction of spike trimer in a direct format and detected pseudovirus at 1 x 10^5 TU/mL in saliva in a sandwich format, without an antibody comparator (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2025.129324). Sialic-acid glyconanoparticles that recognise the spike protein through its glycan-binding activity, a lectin for severe dengue virus infection and a miniprotein binder aimed at SARS-CoV-2 variants of concern have been described without accuracy data (DOI: 10.1021/acscentsci.0c00855; DOI: 10.1080/15321819.2023.2260480; DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06113-2).

Within the conventional antibody format, the choice of antibody pair and of antigen also decides what the particle can capture. For the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, two selected antibody pairs reached 8 pg of protein and 625 TCID50/mL of virus in 60 clinical samples (DOI: 10.3390/ijms222212412), a detection antibody directed at a linear epitope reached 10 pg/mL (DOI: 10.3390/ijms25084436), and one study compared the nucleocapsid and the spike as targets (DOI: 10.1016/j.molimm.2021.01.005). In serology strips the antigen is the variable: an acid-treated p72 antigen detected African swine fever virus antibodies at a 1:10,000 serum dilution (DOI: 10.3389/fchem.2021.804981); the protrusion domain of the infectious bursal disease virus VP2 protein served as a highly immunogenic antigen (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-020-10458-6); plant-produced receptor-binding domain gave a sensitivity of 94.1% and a specificity of 98% in 51 sera (DOI: 10.3877/cma.j.issn.2096-112x.2021.01.006); baculovirus-expressed nucleocapsid and receptor-binding domain were tested in 103 convalescent sera (DOI: 10.1007/s10529-022-03316-0); and the shell domain of norovirus was chosen for a broad-range strip tested on 122 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1186/s12866-020-02084-z).

Binder replacement promises independence from animal immunisation and resilience to sequence variation. The evidence so far is largely analytical: apart from the nanobody strip, with its fourfold gain, these studies report a detection limit or a specificity statement rather than a comparison with an antibody version of the same strip (Table A1d).

## 3.1.7 Assay format: what the line can mean

Even with a good label and a good binder, the format of the immunoassay determines how the colour relates to the amount of target. In a sandwich strip, excess antigen can suppress the line (the hook effect); a lumpy skin disease virus strip that examined this effect reported a detection limit of 10^3.4 TCID50/mL, a coefficient of variation below 5.3% and no cross-reaction with 12 other viruses (DOI: 10.3390/bios12090739). In competitive formats the line weakens as the target increases, which suits functional assays: a competitive gold strip for SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies reached 20.66 IU/L with 100% specificity (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.2c03677), and a biomimetic mock-virus particle displaying trimeric spike measured neutralising antibody at 59 IU/mL against the WHO standard (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04372). Quantitative colloidal gold assays can also benchmark other tests: one measured the detection limit of nine approved SARS-CoV-2 antigen kits and found nucleic acid testing at least 1835 times more sensitive than antigen testing for a Delta strain (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2023.124462). A Japanese encephalitis virus NS1 strip read with a smartphone colorimeter detected 10 pg/mL by eye in spiked serum and was stable for a month at 4 C (DOI: 10.1039/d2na00463a). The limit addressed here is interpretation, and the cost is that each format has its own dynamic range, which most reports do not state (Table A1f).

## 3.1.8 Changing the optics: scattering instead of absorption

All the strategies above increase absorbed colour. One study instead removes the white background that hides a different optical signal. When the nitrocellulose membrane is rendered transparent by refractive-index matching and placed over a black backing card, large AuNPs appear as orange lines by scattering rather than by absorption, and the background almost disappears. For the influenza A virus nucleoprotein the authors report a very large gain over their own conventional strip and over commercial strips, higher sensitivity in a small set of clinical swabs, and reading by eye or smartphone; the approach needs an additional reagent step and sufficient light, and the authors themselves place it beyond absorbance-based colorimetry (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). It is therefore treated as a boundary case and its figures are kept separate from those of absorption-based strips (Table A1c).

## 3.1.9 Conventional gold strips: the baseline

Many of the included studies describe new colloidal gold strips that apply none of the modifications above. They define what the unmodified format achieves and are the strips that the engineering studies set out to improve; because they seldom run a modified strip beside them, they report no within-study change, and their values are listed in Table A1e.

For SARS-CoV-2 serology, colloidal gold IgM and IgG strips reached a sensitivity of 100% and a specificity of 93.3% for IgM (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.0c01554), a sensitivity of 90% and a specificity of 96.6% against real-time PCR in 60 samples (DOI: 10.1089/mab.2021.0027), 158 of 170 positives with an area under the curve of 0.958 (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.23619) and an IgM sensitivity of 90.67% (DOI: 10.1002/ctm2.90), with further strips reported without values (DOI: 10.1039/d0an00629g; DOI: 10.1097/cm9.0000000000000922); a nasal secretory IgA strip detected spike-specific IgA from people infected with the BA.5 variant (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2024.1386891). For SARS-CoV-2 antigen, a spike strip detected 62.5 ng/mL in a subunit vaccine (DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2021.635677), a nucleocapsid kit detected above 0.2 ng/mL of recombinant protein without cross-reaction (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2021.114183), a receptor-binding-domain strip detected 1 ng/mL with a sensitivity of 94.3% and a specificity of 90.9% against RT-PCR in 100 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.28416), a nanogold antigen test reached a sensitivity of 95% and a specificity of 98% (DOI: 10.3390/nano12142477), another detected 2 ng/mL with 98% concordance (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07299-3), and the CoV-SCAN strip detected ten variants at 1.6 to 12.5 TCID50 per swab (DOI: 10.1016/j.jcvp.2022.100080). A nucleic acid sandwich strip for the N3 gene reached 5 pM (DOI: 10.1016/j.jbiosc.2022.11.001).

For other human viruses, an HIV-1 p24 strip with in-house monoclonal antibodies reached 25 pg/mL and a specificity of 98.03% (DOI: 10.1155/2016/6743904); a strip for severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus reached 1 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.8b02366); a competitive dengue strip with the 4G2 antibody reached 5.12 x 10^2 PFU (DOI: 10.3390/bios12070495); a varicella-zoster strip reached 30 ng/mL of glycoprotein E without cross-reaction with EV71, HSV-1 or HSV-2 (DOI: 10.1016/j.virol.2023.07.008); a colloidal gold strip for the mpox virus A29 protein detected 50 pg/mL of purified antigen without cross-reaction with vaccinia or cowpox virus (DOI: 10.1039/d2an02043b); and an HTLV-I IgG strip reached a sensitivity of 96.7% and a specificity of 100% (DOI: 10.3390/v15010129). For animal and aquatic viruses, strips have been described for feline coronavirus antibodies in plasma, serum, whole blood and ascitic fluid (DOI: 10.1007/978-1-4939-2438-7_3), nervous necrosis virus virions in fish tissue (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-020-63553-z), porcine circovirus 2 at 10^3.18 TCID50/mL (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.01953-22), pseudorabies virus at 1.336 x 10^3 copies/uL (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2024.1399123), avian leukosis virus p27 protein at 1.53 ng/mL in 35 egg samples, against 0.2 ng/mL for ELISA (DOI: 10.1039/d4an00180j), and canine parvovirus in faeces, with a sensitivity of 71% and a specificity of 92% against haemagglutination in one study (DOI: 10.1007/s00705-018-3846-2), a colloidal gold and fluorescent format in 60 clinical samples in another (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-023-12604-2), and a sensitivity of 96.4% and a specificity of 88.2% against qPCR in a third (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-55548-x).

## 3.1.10 What the evidence supports

Read as a whole, the studies of this section give a consistent account of how the colour of a gold strip is strengthened. Three routes work. The first makes the captured gold more visible after capture, by metal enhancement, layer-by-layer assembly or an amplifier complex, and gives the largest gains with the same antibodies and particles as the baseline, at the price of reagents, time or an instrument. The second gives each captured particle a larger optical effect, through clusters, dimers, shells and above all shaped gold, and gives gains of about five- to 100-fold in the colour channel. The third delivers more particles to the line, by magnetic enrichment or by attaching several particles to each captured target, and gives gains of 100- to 1000-fold with extra hardware or an extra step. The best-supported comparisons are those in which the same study runs its own conventional strip (Table A4); across them the reported gain in colour ranges from about twofold to about 1000-fold, and the high end belongs invariably to designs that add a magnet, an electrode set, an imager or a pre-incubation.

A second conclusion concerns the dual-signal labels of Section 3.1.5. Their headline sensitivity comes from SERS, photothermal or fluorescence readout, and the gain in colour alone is far smaller than their titles suggest: none to about 100-fold, with none at all for the Raman-tagged strips. For the question of this review, these studies are informative mainly for showing that shaping the gold improves its colour more than alloying or compositing it.

Conjugation chemistry, binder replacement, antigen design and assay format address other limits, namely the activity of the recognition element, its supply, sequence variation and the interpretation of the line; their contribution to the colour signal is rarely quantified against a baseline.

Three limitations apply across the section. Many reported LODs come from scanners, imagers or readers rather than from the naked eye and therefore describe instrument-assisted performance. Clinical evaluation is the exception among the engineering studies, several of which were tested only in spiked buffer, saliva or recombinant protein, with reference methods that vary from ELISA to RT-PCR. And the comparators differ, from the authors' own conventional strip, through particles of the same size, to commercial strips and quoted literature values, so that fold-changes cannot be ranked across studies. ### Table A2. Strategy overview for gold-nanoparticle engineering

| Strategy | Bottleneck addressed | Approaches in the included studies | Reported within-paper gains (see tables for values) | Costs and limits seen in the evidence | Table |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Size and surface tuning | antibody activity, particle mobility | size series, oriented conjugation, additives, corona and aggregation studies | less antibody needed with oriented conjugation; serotype-specific LODs with an 80 nm particle | mostly mechanistic; few diagnostic data | A1a |
| Enlargement and amplification | size of the captured signal | silver, gold and galvanic enhancement, self-enhancement, layer assembly, amplifier complex | LOD lowered within the same strip format, by up to more than two orders of magnitude | extra steps, background, lowest values often need calibration or a reader | A1b |
| Structured particles | optical effect per particle | clusters, dimers, nanoshells, composites, nanostars | about 4- to 24-fold against the authors' own strips; 50-fold against a commercial strip (dimers) | scale-up (dimers), buffer-only tests (nanostars), instrument imaging (two-wavelength) | A1c |
| Binder replacement and antigen design | binder quality, supply, variants | nanobodies, aptamers, glycans, lectins, miniproteins, antibody pairs, recombinant and plant-made antigens | nanobody: lower LOD than mAb in the same strip | aptamer strips less sensitive; mostly spiked or inactivated-virus samples | A1d |
| Optical readout change | background of the white membrane | plasmonic scattering on a black backing | one study: 361-fold against its own strip, sensitivity 90% on 30 positive swabs | extra reagent step, light source, boundary to colorimetric | A1c |
