# 3.4 Assay and flow engineering: when the strip around the label limits the colour

## 3.4.1 Everything that happens before the label meets the line

Sections 3.1 to 3.3 changed what sits on the strip: the particle, its colour and the amplifier. Yet a label can only colour the line if it gets there. A sample has to enter the strip in a usable form, flow past the capture line for long enough, bring enough target molecules, meet binders that recognise the target and be read in a defined format. Any of these steps can limit the signal whatever the label does, and the label of the studies in this section is usually plain colloidal gold. The gains reported here therefore describe the strip, the sample and the reaction around the label.

The studies are organised along the path of the sample. The first group slows the liquid down so that complexes have longer to bind (Section 3.4.2). The second increases the number of target molecules that reach the line, by concentrating the sample, collecting it on magnetic particles, driving it electrically or arranging the reaction outside the strip (Section 3.4.3). The third deals with whether the sample reaches the strip in a usable form at all (Section 3.4.4). The fourth examines what the strip can recognise, through new binders and antigens (Section 3.4.5), and the fifth how the reaction is arranged and read, in functional, serological and nucleic acid formats (Section 3.4.6). Strips that combine these elements for one virus form the baseline (Section 3.4.7), and Section 3.4.8 states what the evidence supports.

The rules of the earlier sections apply: a change is an improvement only when the same study runs a conventional or unmodified strip beside it, the target virus is named for every method, and only the colour channel is evaluated. The section covers 99 records from 2015 to 2026, after the exclusion of six papers in which the strip was used only to screen antibodies or peptides (rule E9); 16 were read in full, and the figures of the others are given as their abstracts state them (Tables D1a to D1f; Table D4 collects the within-study comparisons).

## 3.4.2 Giving the complex more time: flow control

The simplest limit outside the label is the time that the sample spends in front of the capture line. In a conventional strip the liquid crosses the line in seconds, and slowing it lets labelled complexes bind more completely. The designs of this group do it with barriers, geometry or a change of direction, and they give consistent, moderate gains.

Barriers that delay the front are the most common. For the SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain, a hydrophobic cellulose barrier placed in the flow path (a delayed lateral flow design) lowered the LOD to 0.11 ng/mL, 9.1-fold below the conventional strip, with a 2.6-fold higher slope sensitivity; in 12 throat swabs, 8 of them RT-PCR-positive, the sensitivity was 87.5% and the specificity 100% (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-022-05467-3). The barrier can also be dissolvable. Sugar barriers, placed between the conjugate pad and the test line and between the test and control lines, delayed the wicking of a SARS-CoV-2 nucleic acid strip by 10 to 280 s depending on the sugar content (10% was optimal) and lowered the LOD from 2.5 nM to 1 nM with one barrier and 0.5 nM with two, 2.5- and 5-fold, with a synthetic ORF1ab target and no clinical samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.carbpol.2021.118259). A water-soluble polymer wall of 15 wt% dextran methacrylate, 3 mm wide, placed behind the test line lengthened the capillary time to about 400 s and gave an LOD of 50 pg/mL for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleoprotein, 5- to 20-fold better than three commercial antigen kits on Omicron nucleoprotein; seven inactivated variants were detected and five other respiratory viruses were not, with a coefficient of variation below 20% at 500 pg/mL and below 10% at 2000 pg/mL, and no patient samples were tested (DOI: 10.3390/bios14060305). A cotton intermembrane, a cheap layer placed between pads, shows the same effect from another angle. The LoD (the lowest concentration with 19 of 20 replicates reactive) fell from 7.5 ng/mL for the first prototype to 3.75 ng/mL when the test-line antibody was doubled, and to 2.0 ng/mL with the cotton layer, which gave the same two-fold gain as raising the gold conjugate concentration, at an estimated 35% lower reagent cost; purified antigen and inactivated virus were the only samples (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-31641-5). The pattern across the four studies is consistent: each lengthening of the contact time buys 2- to 9-fold, and the cheapest designs buy the same as more reagent.

Geometry and direction work on the same limit. A tapered strip for dengue virus NS1 reached 4.9 ng/mL, about 11-fold better than previously reported (DOI: 10.1063/1.5035113); a sponge shunt for hepatitis B virus nucleic acid improved the analytical sensitivity without a stated value (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-017-01558-x); a two-dimensional paper network for influenza haemagglutinin reached 4.45 x 10^2 TCID50/mL in spiked paediatric nasal swabs (DOI: 10.1039/c8lc00691a); a paper-hydrogel hybrid detected dengue virus RNA in clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/adhm.201600920); and femtosecond-laser micromachined channels in nitrocellulose were applied to respiratory viruses and SARS-CoV-2 without values in the abstract (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-74407-3). Sending the sample through the membrane, rather than along it, changes the contact geometry altogether. Vertical-flow cellulose assays were built for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies and antigen (DOI: 10.1021/acssensors.1c00235; DOI: 10.1039/d2lc00073c) and for influenza A (H1N1), where a vertical-flow paper immunosensor with a different pore sample pad reached 1.34 PFU/mL in buffer and 2.27 PFU/mL in saliva (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2018.10.008). The most careful comparison, for Rift Valley fever virus nucleoprotein, used 40 nm gold with the same antibody pair in both formats. In buffer the vertical-flow LOD was 0.039 ng/mL against 4 ng/mL for the lateral-flow prototype, about 100-fold, but in 10-fold diluted serum it was 0.078 ng/mL, equal to 0.78 ng/mL in undiluted serum against 2 ng/mL for the prototype, which is the 2.5-fold of the abstract; the serum background had absorbed most of the gain, and cross-reactivity with other biothreat antigens was minimal (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.00341-25). A thermochromic strip for dengue virus serotype 2 NS1 converted the photothermal heat of 12 nm gold nanospheres into a colour change of a thermochromic sheet and reached a 4-fold lower LOD than a typical visual readout; the final reading is a colour but its source is heat, so it is flagged (DOI: 10.1039/d3nr01858j). Low-cost fabrication addresses manufacturing rather than sensitivity: manually developed SARS-CoV-2 IgM/IgG strips (sensitivity 93.3% and 96.6%) (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.2c07014), 3D-printed and foil-based nanoimprinted chips for SARS-CoV-2 serology (DOI: 10.18063/ijb.v7i4.399; DOI: 10.3390/bios15040229), a centrifugal microfluidic chip for Ebola virus-like particles (DOI: 10.1039/d3ay00073g) and a cellulose-based strip for SARS-CoV-2 anti-S antibodies, run on 500-fold diluted serum over 10 ng/mL to 1 ug/mL (DOI: 10.1007/s10570-022-04808-y). The limit addressed at this level is contact time; the cost is the one-step simplicity of a conventional strip, and the gains are measured against the authors' own strips (Table D1a). Slowing the liquid, however, cannot add molecules that are not in the sample, and the next group of studies does.

## 3.4.3 Bringing more of the target to the line

When the target is scarce the limit is not time but number: too few molecules reach the line. The most effective answer in this section is to concentrate the target before the strip, and the best of these studies leave the strip and the label untouched, so that the whole gain can be attributed to the front end. Their gains are also the clearest example in this review of what hardware buys.

The first family is electrical. A nanoelectrokinetic preconcentrator coupled to a commercial SARS-CoV-2 IgG/IgM strip concentrated antibodies more than 30-fold on rolled paper discs under an electric field of about 70 V/cm, with 95.7% extraction efficiency on the right paper, and gave a 32-fold lower LOD: the signal appeared at 1/6,400 instead of 1/200 dilution of a vaccinated serum, and four samples that the commercial strip missed became detectable (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114385). The principle was extended to a nanoelectrokinetic strip with a Nafion-cellulose gel for the nucleocapsid protein, with a 32.5-fold enhancement and 100% sensitivity and specificity against 66.7% sensitivity for the conventional strip in a clinical evaluation (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2025.117357), and to electrokinetic focusing by ion concentration polarization for the spike protein (DOI: 10.1039/d3ay00990d). Electricity can also drive the assembly of the immune complex itself. For antibodies to hepatitis B surface antigen and to hepatitis C virus, an electrophoretic step driven by a 9 V battery for 3 minutes lowered the LOD from 625 ng/mL for a conventional strip in serum to 0.6 ng/mL, about 1000-fold. The decomposition is instructive: the conventional strip reached 4.9 ng/mL in buffer, so the electrophoresis itself accounted for 8-fold, and the other roughly 130-fold came from removing the background IgG that competes with the specific antibody in serum. In 181 sera the diagnostic sensitivity rose from 75% to 98% for anti-HBs and from 65% to 96% for anti-HCV, against instrumental tests, with 3 uL of finger-prick blood and a result in under 10 minutes (DOI: 10.1021/acssensors.3c00130).

The second family is physical. A red-blood-cell-membrane-coated nanoporous aluminium oxide membrane enriched SARS-CoV-2 virus, nucleocapsid protein and IgG under 3 bar in 3 minutes, again in front of a commercial strip; the LOD improved up to 20-fold, and in 62 individuals the sensitivity of the strip rose from 14.29% to 88.1% with 100% specificity, although some low-viral-load samples were made by diluting positive swabs and the device needs a pressure source and 3 mL of sample (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-37018-6). A non-powered preconcentrator, with no electric field, enriched the nucleocapsid protein in front of a commercial antigen strip in 10 minutes and improved the LOD more than 10-fold: 0.1 ng/mL gave no visible line without it and a visible line with it, and RT-PCR confirmed about 10-fold enrichment (change in Ct above 3); the only clinical test was one patient nasal sample at two dilutions, read by five trained engineers against a colour chart (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114965). For large volumes, osmosis and agglutination do the same. An osmotic processor with a polymer concentrated a 10 mL urine sample about 100-fold in 0.5 hours, matched a reference at 100-fold concentration in front of a commercial SARS-CoV-2 strip, and was estimated by mass spectrometry to raise the nucleocapsid protein about 200-fold, but only spiked urine was used and the text gives no LOD value (DOI: 10.3389/fbioe.2022.884271). An agglutination system with biotinylated antibody and streptavidin forms aggregates that are filtered and read on paper. Going from 50 uL to 2 mL lowered the LoD 10-fold, from 100 to 10 pg/mL, where the 40-fold increase in volume predicted 40-fold, and the LoD in spiked saliva equalled that in buffer; gamma-irradiated virus was detected at 250 genome copies/uL, with no patient samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2023.341674). For HIV nucleic acid, a dialysis-based concentrator gave a 10-fold signal gain (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2016.02.017).

The third family uses magnetic particles, which concentrate the target and can also colour the line. The plant virus studies are the cleanest, because they separate the contributions. For potato virus X, magnetic concentration alone lowered the visual limit 2-fold, and so did the aggregation of magnetic particles with gold particles, which increases the visibility of the label; together they gave 0.25 ng/mL against 8 ng/mL for the strip with gold alone, 32-fold, in buffer and in spiked leaf extract, with an instrumental LOD of 0.26 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2017.12.023). Two 2-fold gains that make a 32-fold gain are the clearest evidence in this review that enrichment strategies are not additive. A barcode strip with a magnetic label and three test zones set three cut-offs for the same virus, and magnetic concentration (x10) lowered them from 3, 30 and 150 ng/mL to 0.5, 9 and 30 ng/mL, 6-, 3.3- and 5-fold, with complete agreement with a commercial test on plant samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2016.11.025). For SARS-CoV-2, a magnetic-focus-enhanced strip detected 400 PFU/mL in buffer and 1200 PFU/mL in saliva, with sensitivity 66.7% and specificity 100% on nasal swabs (DOI: 10.3390/bios11120488), and for the hepatitis B virus preS2 antigen a secondary-antibody magnetic nanoprobe gave sensitivity 93.3% and specificity 90% on 25 clinical sera (DOI: 10.3390/bios10110161).

The last family needs no hardware at all: it rearranges the order of the reaction. For potato virus Y, mixing the gold conjugate with the sample for only 30 s before the strip, instead of drying the conjugate on the pad, lowered the calculated LOD from 330 ng/mL to 5.4 ng/mL, 60-fold as stated by the authors, but the figures compare a visual limit with a calculated one: visual to visual the limit fell from 330 to 12 ng/mL, about 27-fold; an ELISA gave 4 ng/mL and the quantitative results correlated with it at 0.891 (DOI: 10.3390/s18113975). Performing the antigen capture outside the strip had a larger effect still. A pipetting-based immunoassay, in which antibody-coated magnetic beads capture the antigen in a pipette tip, used before a commercial lateral flow kit for influenza A nucleoprotein, lowered the LOD from 10^4 to 10^2 EID50 per reaction, 100-fold; on 11 RT-PCR-positive bird faecal samples the strip alone found 6, and with capture it found five more, at the price of one false negative and one false positive (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-019-53083-8).

Concentration shows the largest gains in this section, from 3-fold to 1000-fold, without changing the label. Its cost is hardware (electrodes, pressure, magnets), larger sample volumes and, in most cases, little patient evidence: where the gain was measured, it was often on spiked or reference samples, and its decomposition (as in the electrophoretic and magnetic studies) shows that part of what is called enrichment is the removal of interference or the arrangement of the reaction (Table D1b).

## 3.4.4 Getting the sample into the strip

Before any concentration, the sample must reach the strip in a usable form, and several studies treat the sample itself as a design variable. A pretreatment with TCEP and bovine serum albumin made sputum usable for lateral flow detection of porcine epidemic diarrhea virus and MERS coronavirus (DOI: 10.4110/in.2021.21.e11). A gargle with a bean extract (Beanguard) replaced swab collection for SARS-CoV-2 antigen tests, with a sensitivity of 97.8% and a specificity of 100% in 45 patients tested within six days of onset, in a study of 102 patients (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.01614-21). A porous microneedle patch collected sample for an IgM/IgG test for SARS-CoV-2 (IgM 3 ng/mL, IgG 7 ng/mL) (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-022-14725-6), and a new diluent improved a SARS-CoV-2 IgG test (sensitivity 91%, specificity 100% on 25 positive and 25 negative samples) (DOI: 10.1007/s13337-021-00669-4). The saliva results of the foot-and-mouth studies in Section 3.3 show why this matters: a sample matrix can inhibit a test that works in buffer. Together these studies show that sample handling is itself a design variable, at the level of one study each and at the cost of extra reagents or devices (Table D1c).

## 3.4.5 What the strip can recognise: binders and antigens made for the strip

Slower flow, concentration and a better sample can all be wasted if the binder does not recognise the target, and the most numerous group of studies in this section develops binders and antigens for a new virus. They continue the line of Section 3.1.6, and as there, the abstracts rarely give a comparison with a conventional antibody.

Antibody pairs and fragments come first. Monoclonal pairs or fragments were developed for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.27520; DOI: 10.1371/journal.pntd.0010311; DOI: 10.3390/microorganisms11102422; DOI: 10.1093/jaoacint/qsaf105), for the hepatitis C virus core antigen (10 ng/strip and 0.3125 ng/mL, with a sensitivity of 92.0% and a specificity of 100% in the second study) (DOI: 10.3390/ph17081022; DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-54470-8), for the feline leukaemia virus p27 antigen (DOI: 10.1016/j.ab.2025.116013), for the Lassa virus nucleoprotein, with lineage-dependent detection and specificity 100% on 60 human samples (DOI: 10.1128/jcm.00071-26), for the orthoebolavirus glycoprotein, which detected Ebola virus in non-human primates at day 4 (DOI: 10.1038/s44298-026-00209-1), for the Nipah and Hendra viruses (diagnostic specificity 94.4%) (DOI: 10.3390/v17071021), and for mpox and other orthopoxviruses with four monoclonal antibodies to the A27 protein (3 x 10^5 pfu/mL) (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.02456-24). Affimers were used for the Crimean-Congo haemorrhagic fever virus nucleoprotein (DOI: 10.1371/journal.pntd.0008364).

Non-antibody binders follow. Six aptamer-aptamer pairs for the SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain were compared, the best reaching sensitivity 100% and specificity 93.3% on swabs (DOI: 10.1093/jaoacint/qsae004); a competitive aptamer device for the spike protein reached 51.81 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2024.126825), an aptamer lateral flow biosensor for the nucleocapsid protein 500 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1016/j.virol.2025.110688), and aptamer cocktails in three models sensitivity of 96.6% to 100% with specificity 100% (DOI: 10.1186/s12879-025-11751-3). For dengue virus serotype 2 NS1 an aptamer-antibody strip reached 5.2 ng/mL in buffer and 6.1 ng/mL in spiked serum (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-29869-4). Nanobodies were used in a spike S1 strip improved by streptavidin-biotin capture (sensitivity 95.21% and specificity 99.29% against 91.66% and 98.57% for the earlier strip) (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-57230-8), for SARS-CoV-2 and MERS-CoV (DOI: 10.1021/acssynbio.4c00592) and for Zika virus NS1 (25 ng/mL in buffer, 1 ng/mL in urine) (DOI: 10.1021/acssynbio.4c00819). A competitive strip with an affinity-engineered receptor-binding domain/ACE2 pair detected betacoronavirus S1 at 1.66 ug/mL (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2026.118909); helical affibodies replaced the antibody in a spike strip (DOI: 10.1021/jacs.4c17452); the human ACE2 receptor served as capture for the spike S1 antigen (1.86 x 10^5 copies/mL) on clinical specimens (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2020.112715); glycan recognition was used for SARS-CoV-2 in a flow-through device and in a glycopolymer-capture system for SARS-CoV-2 and other betacoronaviruses (DOI: 10.1021/acssensors.1c01470; DOI: 10.1021/acscentsci.1c01080); and high-affinity haemagglutinin-binding proteins detected intact influenza virus particles (3.54 x 10^7 and 1.34 x 10^7 CEID50/mL) (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.7b00769).

How the binder is presented to the target matters as much as which binder it is. Orienting the antibody on the particle improved a SARS-CoV-2 strip, which detected 5 x 10^4 copies/mL and gave 100% accuracy on 19 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2022.133245); engineered nucleocapsid antigens were designed to improve the stability of SARS-CoV-2 strips (DOI: 10.3390/biom13101524); an antibody against a drug-resistance-associated neuraminidase mutant allowed detection of oseltamivir-resistant influenza virus (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-020-17246-w); genotype-specific antibodies were developed for human bocavirus 1 and 2 (DOI: 10.1007/s12519-023-00697-8); and an extracellular-vesicle capture design links HIV to vesicle markers in blood (DOI: 10.1016/j.vesic.2025.100084). Antigen detection kits with recombinant or monoclonal nucleocapsid antibodies detected SARS-CoV-2 Omicron (62.5 TCID50/mL on simulated positive samples) and its sublineages, Wuhan and Delta (DOI: 10.3390/vaccines11020303; DOI: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e20913). These studies establish what a strip can recognise; whether it then works depends on the format and the sample, which many do not report in the abstract (Table D1d). The cost of a new binder is its development, and the abstracts rarely give a within-study comparison with a conventional antibody.

## 3.4.6 How the reaction is arranged and read: formats, functional assays and DNA structures

The last group asks how the immunoreaction itself is organised and what the colour is made to mean. The clearest evidence that format alone can change the limit by two orders of magnitude comes from a comparison of three SARS-CoV-2 antibody-detection formats run with the same receptor-binding domain antigen and 30 nm gold. The format with protein A immobilised in the line and labelled antigen in the conjugate gave a 15- and 255-fold lower detection limit (10 ng/mL) than the other two on pooled positive serum, while still detecting 22 of 22 patient sera correctly (DOI: 10.3390/bios13070750). A two-stage label incorporation into immune complexes (a serodiagnosis model) raised sensitivity from 62.5% to 100% at specificity 100% (DOI: 10.3390/bios12070434). A half-strip format that uses open reagents served for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein (0.65 ng/mL) and for Rift Valley fever virus antibodies (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.0c01975; DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics12112664).

Functional and serological formats read a different question. Neutralizing antibodies against SARS-CoV-2 were measured with a competitive strip from a finger prick (DOI: 10.1016/j.jcv.2021.105024), a strip capturing neutralized receptor-binding domain (DOI: 10.1016/j.crmeth.2022.100273) and a strip with an LOD of 2 ug/mL, sensitivity 97.80% and specificity 100% on 684 uninfected and clinical samples (DOI: 10.2147/ijn.s408921). Serological and multiplex tests include a four-serotype dengue panel with serotype-specific NS1 detection (333 to 22,500 TCID50/mL in plasma and serum) (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.02796-22), a species-independent double-antigen test for Rift Valley fever virus antibodies (DOI: 10.3390/v18030316), a semi-quantitative multi-line SARS-CoV-2 antibody test that classifies high and low titres (DOI: 10.1039/d3tb02895j), a strip with HPV16 virus-like-particle capture to determine HPV16 vaccination status (DOI: 10.1016/j.vaccine.2016.09.021) and a dengue NS1 capture assay with serotype specificity of 100% (DOI: 10.3390/bios15120802). A caution applies: the reference standards of these formats differ (neutralization tests, ELISA), so their agreement figures cannot be compared with antigen strips.

Nucleic acid targets are read on strips by hybridisation, and DNA structures can add signal without amplifying the target. For SARS-CoV-2, a triplex enhanced assay reached 0.01 nM without amplification (DOI: 10.3390/ijms232315258), a hybridisation chain reaction strip 200 copies/uL against 500 to 2000 for commercial unamplified strips (DOI: 10.1021/acsinfecdis.2c00472), a net-shaped DNA nanostructure strip covered 10^3 to 10^8 copies/mL (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c03698) and protein-based probe anchoring reached about 0.2 nM (DOI: 10.3390/mi14101936). For influenza A (H1N1), an M13 nanoscaffold gave an amplification-free strip at 1 pM (DOI: 10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2024.136945), and for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus a DNA nanostructure with multivalent binding gave 97.06% accuracy on 102 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/adhm.202504037). A probe-gated silica nanoparticle strip detected SARS-CoV-2 at 0.73 pg/mL in unprocessed swabs (sensitivity 100%, specificity 83% against RT-qPCR) (DOI: 10.1002/open.202300120) (Table D1e).

## 3.4.7 Strips for one virus: the baseline of this section

A large share of the records are strips developed for one virus without a deliberate change to the label, many of them evaluated on patient samples. They matter as the baseline: they show what the combination of binder, format and sample achieves without signal engineering. Their abstracts rarely give a within-study change in the colour signal, and their values are listed in Table D1f.

For SARS-CoV-2, antigen prototypes include a saliva strip tested on 111 hospitalised patients (DOI: 10.48101/ujms.v127.8207), a prototype compared with BinaxNOW (DOI: 10.3390/v14122609), a spike strip with a specificity of 98% (25 ng/test) (DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2023.1111644), a nucleocapsid antigen test that meets the WHO target product profile at 4 TCID50/swab (DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0258819), a visual platform whose reading correlated with a reader (R2 0.9571; sensitivity 98%, specificity 100% on 170 swabs) (DOI: 10.1039/d3ay02075d) and an in-house antibody kit tested on 300 samples (DOI: 10.1186/s13568-023-01620-0). For other viruses: an influenza A and B strip with new monoclonal antibodies, 4- to 8-fold more sensitive than commercial tests, with a sensitivity of 89% (A) and 83% (B) and specificity 100% on 101 swabs (DOI: 10.1039/d1ra01361k); a paper-based dengue NS1 device on 219 sera (sensitivity 88.89%, specificity 86.67%, kappa 0.7522 against nested PCR) (DOI: 10.1016/j.ijid.2021.05.007); and standard and rapid antigen assays for human metapneumovirus (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.70374).

## 3.4.8 What the evidence supports

The gains in the colour signal in this section come from the strip and the sample, not from the label. Within-study changes range from about 2-fold to 1000-fold (Table D4), and they separate into three levels. Flow control gains 2- to 9-fold, and up to 20-fold against commercial kits, by lengthening the contact time, and the cheapest designs buy the same as more reagent. Concentrating the target gains 3- to 1000-fold, with the largest gains from electrical and physical front ends that leave the strip and label untouched, but with hardware, volume and in most cases thin patient evidence. And changing the order or the format of the reaction gains 15- to 255-fold, from pre-incubation, capture outside the strip and the arrangement of the line and the conjugate, without any extra device. The decompositions that some studies allow qualify these figures: the 1000-fold of the electrophoretic assay is largely the removal of background IgG in serum, the 100-fold of the vertical-flow strip falls to 2.5-fold in serum, the two 2-fold gains of magnetic concentration and aggregation make 32-fold only in combination, and the 60-fold of pre-incubation is 27-fold on matched visual limits. Section 3.4 therefore shows that the gains reported for the strip cannot be compared across studies without knowing how the limit was read and in which matrix.

Three limitations recur. Many studies use reference or spiked samples, and clinical samples are the exception among the engineering papers; the clinical test of the non-powered preconcentrator was one diluted patient sample, that of the delayed-flow strip 12 swabs and that of the nanotrap 62 individuals. Several studies give a fold-improvement and not the accuracy of the strip on patients. And the antibody and binder papers rarely report the matrix, the number of samples or the reference method in the abstract. The reporting quality of the 16 studies read in full is summarised in Table D3.
