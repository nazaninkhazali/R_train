# 3.2 Coloured labels other than gold: what a different colour carrier changes

## 3.2.1 Why replace gold

Section 3.1 showed that the colour of a gold strip can be strengthened by changing the particle, enlarging it after capture or delivering more particles to the line, and that the largest gains are paid for with reagents, time or an instrument. A second line of work keeps the colorimetric principle but replaces the gold with another coloured carrier. The studies in this section give three reasons for doing so. The first is optical: some materials absorb visible light over a broader or stronger band than a 30 to 40 nm gold sphere, as argued for polydopamine in a SARS-CoV-2 serology strip (DOI: 10.3390/bios13030352) and for carbon, whose black colour gives a high signal-to-noise ratio against the white membrane (DOI: 10.3390/bios13090857). The second is manufacturing: colloidal gold is described as limited in analytical sensitivity and subject to batch-to-batch variation in particle size, whereas dyed latex microspheres are bright, uniform in size and carry surface groups for controlled covalent coupling, as argued for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus and porcine circovirus 2 strips (DOI: 10.1186/s44149-021-00029-1; DOI: 10.1007/s00253-022-12074-y). The third is information: carriers are available in several colours, so that one strip can report more than one analyte and the result is read by hue, as in a strip for African swine fever virus and classical swine fever virus antibodies (DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419).

The section follows the carriers from the most to the least developed. Dyed latex microspheres come first (3.2.2), then latex and silica carriers loaded with metal or dye (3.2.3), colour coding for multiplexing (3.2.4), carbon (3.2.5), selenium, silver, pigment and magnetite particles (3.2.6), organic and polymer chromophores (3.2.7) and cellulose nanobeads (3.2.8). Section 3.2.9 collects the studies in which a coloured label was run beside a fluorescent one, because they show what equipment-free reading gives up, and 3.2.10 states what the evidence supports. As in Section 3.1, a change is treated as an improvement only when the same study runs a gold strip or another baseline beside the new label, the target virus is named for every method, and only the colour channel is evaluated. The section covers 33 included records from 2015 to 2026; 18 were read in full, and the figures of the others are given as their abstracts state them (Tables B1a to B1g; Table B4 gathers the within-study comparisons).

## 3.2.2 Dyed latex microspheres: brightness and uniformity

Latex microspheres are polymer particles, typically 200 to 400 nm in diameter, dyed in a chosen colour and carrying carboxyl groups for covalent coupling of antibodies (DOI: 10.1186/s44149-021-00029-1; DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419). Their size is an advantage and a constraint at once: a large particle carries far more dye than a gold sphere carries metal, but it aggregates and sediments more easily, so the amount of protein coupled to the surface must be set at the highest level that keeps the suspension stable (DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419), and particles of 400 nm were found to give a weak false-positive line where 300 nm particles did not (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-025-13694-w).

Most of the latex work concerns animal viruses detected directly as antigen, and two studies show what such strips achieve and what sets their limits. A red latex strip for porcine circovirus 2, built on monoclonal antibodies raised against the dominant genotypes, detected the three main genotypes with endpoint titres of 10^2.5 (PCV2a), 10^0.7 (PCV2b) and 10^1.5 (PCV2d) TCID50 per 0.1 mL, and was therefore clearly less sensitive for the older PCV2a genotype, which the authors attribute to the immunogens rather than to the label. Against PCR on 100 tissue and swab samples it was fully specific (47/47) and detected 43 of 53 positives (sensitivity 81.13%, kappa 0.720), the missed samples being explained in part by PCR detecting viral DNA whether or not infectious virus is present (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-022-12074-y). A latex strip for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (LOD 10^3.60 TCID50/mL) solved a problem of the sample rather than of the label: solid faecal particles disturbed the flow, and a filter pad inserted between the sample and conjugate pads improved the agreement with RT-PCR; in 108 faecal samples the strip reached a sensitivity of 88.57% and a specificity of 100% (kappa 0.845), with recoveries of 91.00% to 114.40% and stability for 56 days at room temperature and 37 C (DOI: 10.1186/s44149-021-00029-1). Both studies show that a coloured carrier does not by itself set performance: antibody choice and sample handling matter as much as the colour. Latex strips have also been reported for North American porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus (5 x 10^2.0 TCID50/mL; sensitivity 90.32% on clinical samples) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2022.882112), for SARS-CoV-2 antigen (25 ng/mL) (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.24091), and for the receptor specificity of influenza A group 1 viruses, where a sialylglycopolymer-latex probe correlated above 0.95 with an ELISA (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2014.10.036).

Two studies place the sensitivity of a plain latex strip against laboratory formats with the same reagents. For the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, a red-dyed 200 nm particle gave a visual LOD of 10 ng/mL and detected irradiated Omicron-infected cell extract at 7.5 x 10^5 TCID50/mL, whereas a sandwich ELISA built on the same antibody pair reached 15 pg/mL, nearly three orders of magnitude lower (DOI: 10.1111/1751-7915.70117). For SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies in a double-antigen sandwich, latex beads gave an LOD of 7.11 ng/mL against 1.13 ng/mL for europium fluorescent beads in the same design; the diagnostic performance against the conventional virus neutralisation test was nevertheless equivalent, with a sensitivity of 98.29% and a specificity of 97.70% for latex in 554 sera and 97.44% and 98.28% for the fluorescent version in 389 sera, and the latex strip was read by eye and by smartphone (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2022.124200). The limit addressed by dyed latex is therefore the uniformity and brightness of the carrier, not the analytical sensitivity of the assay, which remains well above that of ELISA or fluorescence with the same binders (Table B1a).

## 3.2.3 Latex and silica as carriers for metal or dye

If a large polymer particle is loaded with metal rather than with dye, it combines the surface area of latex with the plasmonic absorption of gold, and the gain in colour becomes measurable. Two studies, both for influenza, show this with a gold baseline in the same experiment. Gold or platinum nanoparticles deposited on poly(2-vinylpyridine) latex gave a dark-blue (gold) or dark-brown (platinum) line; the light extinction of a single composite particle was calculated to be about 200 (gold) and 100 (platinum) times that of a 30 nm gold sphere, and the lowest influenza A antigen concentration visible by eye was 6.25 x 10^-3 HAU/mL for the gold composite and 2.5 x 10^-2 HAU/mL for the platinum composite, against 4.0 x 10^-1 HAU/mL for conventional 30 nm gold, gains of more than 64- and 16-fold; only antigen solution was tested (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.8b11745). Polystyrene latex coated with 10 nm gold, bridged by the same monoclonal antibody that labels the particle, gave a visual limit of 1/16 HAU (10^1.49 TCID50/mL) for influenza H3N2 in infected allantoic fluid, against 4 HAU for a strip with 10 nm gold and 1 HAU for a strip with 30 nm gold, again 64- and 16-fold; the quantitative LOD by strip reader was 0.016 HAU against 1.06 and 0.562 HAU, the strip was specific against H1, H5, H7 and H9 subtypes and kept its visual limit for three months at room temperature (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-020-04255-1). Dye can be loaded in the same way: highly chromatic red silica nanoparticles doped with Direct Red 224 detected H7 avian influenza (H7N2 and H7N9) at 0.08 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1039/d1ay00204j), and dye-loaded polymersomes served as the carrier of a SARS-CoV-2 strip whose reagents were purified against 121 human samples (DOI: 10.1021/acssensors.1c00854). The limit addressed is the optical effect per captured particle, the same as for shaped gold in Section 3.1, and the two metal-latex studies give the largest colour gains of this section; their cost is a two-step synthesis and, so far, the absence of clinical samples.

## 3.2.4 Colour as a code: multicolour strips for multiplexing

Because dyed carriers come in distinct hues, one strip can report more than one analyte, and the result is read by colour rather than by position alone. A strip with red, blue and black latex gives a red line for African swine fever virus antibodies, a blue line for classical swine fever virus antibodies and a black control line. On a standard positive serum its visual endpoint was 1:256 for both antibodies, equal to the commercial ELISA for African swine fever virus (1:256) and fourfold better than the commercial ELISA for classical swine fever virus (1:64); in 180 sera the agreement with the two ELISAs gave kappa values of 0.986 and 0.918 (DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419). The authors note a limit that no label can remove: antibodies to the E2 protein of classical swine fever virus do not separate vaccinated from infected animals, so that line mainly reports vaccine response (DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419). Colour can also serve only to make the result easier to read: a red latex test line for African swine fever virus antibodies with a blue latex control line made with chicken IgY gave a visual endpoint of 1:1024, equal to the commercial ELISA, a sensitivity of 97.9% and a specificity of 98.2% in 159 sera (kappa 0.955), and remained stable for 16 months at 4 C, 12 months at room temperature and 10 months at 37 C (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-025-13694-w). Other multicolour designs include a bicolour latex strip for SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies (about 48 ng/mL; 13 sera) (DOI: 10.3390/bios12020103) and a red and blue strip that reads a duplex RT-LAMP for human norovirus GII (5.8 copies/uL) and human adenovirus F41 (63 copies/uL) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2026.1787049). Colour coding keeps the strip equipment-free, but it relies on the user distinguishing hues, and none of these studies reports blinded or multi-reader interpretation (Table B1b).

## 3.2.5 Carbon

Carbon black gives a dark line from a material that is inexpensive and chemically inert, and it is the non-metal label with the longest record outside virology. In the studies included here its performance relative to gold is either untested or unfavourable. A carbon black strip for severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus reached 100 pg/mL in spiked serum, with recoveries of 91.58% to 105.4% and no gold comparator (DOI: 10.1039/d3an00515a). A vertical-flow immunoassay with carbon black conjugated to anti-human IgG detected IgG against the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein in human serum within 5 to 7 minutes, with an LOD of 7.81 BAU/mL; the conjugate was optimised between 150 and 250 ug of antibody per mg of carbon, and the lower LOD than colloidal gold claimed in the abstract rests on a comparison with published methods, not on a gold assay run in the same study (DOI: 10.3390/bios13090857). The one head-to-head comparison with clinical samples is unfavourable to carbon. For porcine deltacoronavirus, competitive strips with the same monoclonal antibody on gold and on carbon gave visual limits of 1:128 and 1:64 of a 10^8 TCID50/mL stock; in 137 faecal samples against RT-PCR the gold strip reached a sensitivity of 83.9% and a specificity of 98.8% and the carbon strip 73.2% and 98.8%, with 92.7% and over 88% agreement respectively (DOI: 10.1016/j.isci.2026.116948). The limit that carbon addresses is cost and contrast; in the only direct test, it did not improve sensitivity (Table B1c).

## 3.2.6 Selenium, silver, ultramarine blue and magnetite

Several inorganic particles have been proposed as red, yellow or blue labels, each with a particular argument. Selenium nanoparticles are red, like gold, and have been used for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies (IgM 20 ng/mL, IgG 5 ng/mL; sensitivity 93.33% and specificity 97.34% in 90 patients and 263 controls) (DOI: 10.1039/d0lc00828a; DOI: 10.1109/tnb.2021.3105662). Their most developed use exploits the surface rather than the colour: PEG-stabilised selenium particles immobilise the antibody in a "stand-up" orientation, which the authors use to explain a reading time below one minute, and the resulting SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid kit had an LOD of 10 pg/mL, defined as the lowest concentration at which the test line was still visible, and in 192 clinical samples (60 negative, 132 positive) gave 100% negative, 93.94% positive and 95.83% overall coincidence with RT-PCR, in throat swabs, saliva, serum and fingerstick blood, without cross-reaction with common respiratory pathogens; no gold strip was run for the LOD (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2024.116878).

Silver absorbs more strongly than gold at the same size but is unstable in biological fluids. Coating silver particles with a thin calix[4]arene layer made them stable in plasma and for six months of storage and provided carboxyl groups for covalent coupling of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein; the particles give a yellow line. In a dipstick for anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG they gave an LOD of 5 ng/mL in buffer, one order of magnitude below citrate gold particles with adsorbed spike protein, and a similar signal with ten times fewer particles. The gain did not survive the matrix: in human plasma the LOD was 1.5 ug/mL, and in 25 sera the dipstick was fully specific (10/10) but detected only 11 of 15 RT-PCR-positive patients, which the authors describe as typical of gold-based strips (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c00870). Ultramarine blue, an inexpensive inorganic pigment, was carboxylated and coupled to antibodies as a blue label for HBsAg, with an LOD of 0.37 ng/mL (signal-to-noise 3) in spiked fetal calf serum and reading by eye or image analysis; its abstract claims a lower LOD than a gold strip, but the comparisons found in the main text are with literature values for iron oxide and red silica labels, and no patient samples were tested (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2019.11.037). Natural-colour magnetite (Fe3O4) combined colour with a magnetic core in a SARS-CoV-2 strip reaching 0.5 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2026.150960). Among these labels only silver has a gold comparator in the same study, and its advantage was confined to buffer (Table B1d).

## 3.2.7 Organic and polymer chromophores

Polydopamine nanoparticles absorb broadly across the visible spectrum, and the study that used them for SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG shows that a new label needs as much surface engineering as gold. Polydopamine adheres strongly to nitrocellulose, so probes coupled directly to it would be trapped at the test line and read as false negatives; the particles were therefore coated with polyelectrolyte layers, silica and a PEG-silane before antigen and antibody were attached, which brought them to 230 to 320 nm. The resulting three-line strip separated IgG from IgM, was quantified with a camera and image software, and gave LODs of 1.51 ng/mL (IgM) and 2.34 ng/mL (IgG) from calibration curves, whereas a commercial gold strip run in the same study barely detected 10 to 100 ng/mL; serum spiked with standard antibodies, not patient serum, was used (DOI: 10.3390/bios13030352). Black polylevodopa nanoparticles, which carry abundant carboxyl groups for covalent coupling, gave LODs of 5 pg/mL for SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid antigen and 0.1 ng/mL for influenza A H1N1 antigen, without cross-reaction with influenza B, respiratory syncytial virus or other coronavirus nucleoproteins; the higher sensitivity than gold and commercial strips stated by the authors is documented only in supplementary figures, and no fold-change or clinical sample is given in the main text (DOI: 10.1016/j.bbrc.2024.149821). Polydiacetylene vesicles show a red line and red fluorescence at once; for HBsAg the red line was confirmed by eye down to 1 ng/mL, whereas 0.1 ng/mL was reached only in the fluorescence image, so only the former is a colorimetric result (DOI: 10.1002/asia.201700769). The limit addressed by these chromophores is the breadth and strength of absorption; the evidence for a colour gain over gold rests on one spiked-serum comparison (Table B1e).

## 3.2.8 Cellulose nanobeads

Cellulose nanobeads are dyed cellulose particles of 330 to 365 nm that became commercially available for lateral flow in red, blue, black and green, and they are the one non-metal label in this section for which a colour gain over gold has been measured on clinical samples. The first use for a virus combined them with scFv-Fc fusion proteins selected by phage display for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein: the best pair detected 2 ng of antigen and 2.5 x 10^4 PFU of cultured virus by eye within 20 minutes, with a handheld reader for semi-quantitative reading, and did not cross-react with the nucleoproteins of MERS-CoV, SARS-CoV or influenza H1N1; no gold strip was run (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2020.112868). The direct comparison came later, for the same antigen, with cellulose nanobeads and 40 nm gold on otherwise identical strips. The authors attribute the difference to the roughly 68-fold larger surface available for the detection antibody on a 330 nm bead. In buffer the lowest antigen concentration visible by eye was 0.41 ng/mL for cellulose against 3.28 ng/mL for gold, and the reader signal of the cellulose strip at the gold visual limit was about 20 times higher. In archived nasopharyngeal swabs read by eye, both strips were fully specific in 10 RT-qPCR-negative samples; the highest-Ct positive detected by the gold strip had Ct 21.31 (1.26 x 10^6 copies/mL), that detected by the cellulose strip Ct 26.10 (3.59 x 10^4 copies/mL), so that the cellulose cut-off lay two orders of magnitude below the WHO target product profile of 10^6 copies/mL while gold met the profile exactly; the two labels were tested on different positive sets (32 and 23 samples), and neither detected samples with Ct above 30 (DOI: 10.1039/d5ra08197a). A dual-colour design with red test and blue control beads was developed for the Nipah virus nucleocapsid protein, with an analytical LOD of 1 ng/mL for recombinant protein and, for gamma-irradiated cultured virus quantified by RT-ddPCR, 4.1 x 10^7 copies/mL by portable reader and 8.3 x 10^7 copies/mL by eye; no cross-reaction with other respiratory virus nucleoproteins was seen, and clinical specimens of a biosafety level 4 pathogen were not available (DOI: 10.1080/22221751.2026.2716499). The limit addressed is the amount of colour per captured particle, through size rather than through optical physics, and the cost is that the larger particle still fails at the low viral loads that nucleic acid tests detect (Table B1f).

## 3.2.9 What coloured labels give up: comparisons with fluorescence

Several studies run a coloured and a fluorescent label on the same antibodies, and they define the price of reading by eye. Fluorescent microspheres were more sensitive than coloured microspheres in strips for rotavirus and adenovirus and for influenza A and B, without cross-reaction in either format (DOI: 10.1186/s12985-018-0951-5; DOI: 10.1186/s12985-019-1190-0). In the SARS-CoV-2 neutralising-antibody strip of Section 3.2.2 the fluorescent version reached an LOD about six times lower than the latex version, although the diagnostic sensitivity and specificity against the neutralisation test were the same (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2022.124200). For antibodies to the human adenovirus 3 hexon protein, quantum dots gave a positive percent agreement of 100% against 96.9% for gold, with a negative agreement of 98.2%, in 90 sera (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics16162670). The pattern is consistent: a coloured label gives up a few-fold to an order of magnitude in analytical LOD against a fluorescent label on the same binders, and this gap narrows or disappears at the diagnostic level when the clinical cut-off lies above both limits (Table B1g).

## 3.2.10 What the evidence supports

Non-gold coloured labels mainly change what a strip can do, not how strong its colour is. Dyed latex is the best-documented alternative to gold, almost entirely in veterinary virology (porcine epidemic diarrhea virus, porcine circovirus 2, porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus, African and classical swine fever virus), where its advantages are uniformity, covalent coupling and the availability of several colours; multicolour latex lets several analytes share one strip and be told apart by hue. Where the same study runs a gold strip, the gains in colour are concentrated in two designs that exploit particle size: metal-loaded latex, which gave 16- to 64-fold lower visual limits than 10 to 30 nm gold for influenza antigen, and cellulose nanobeads, which gave an eightfold lower visual limit in buffer and detected SARS-CoV-2 in swabs at a viral load about 35 times lower than gold. The other comparisons are small or negative: calixarene-coated silver gave an order of magnitude in buffer that vanished in plasma; polydopamine quantified low antibody concentrations better than a commercial gold strip in spiked serum; and carbon was less sensitive than gold for porcine deltacoronavirus in the one clinical comparison (Table B4). Against fluorescent labels on the same binders, coloured labels were consistently less sensitive. The remaining labels (selenium, ultramarine blue, magnetite, polylevodopa, red silica, polymersomes) rest on single studies without a gold comparator in the same experiment.

Three limitations recur across the section. Reference methods differ between studies (PCR, RT-PCR, commercial ELISA, virus neutralisation), so agreement values cannot be compared across them. Several studies used spiked or standard samples only, and the one study with a gain in buffer that was lost in plasma shows why this matters. And genotype or strain coverage depends on the antibody pair, not on the label, as the porcine circovirus 2 strip makes explicit.
