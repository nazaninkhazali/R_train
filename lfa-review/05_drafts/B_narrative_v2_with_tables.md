# 3.2 Beyond gold: what a different colour carrier can and cannot change

## 3.2.1 The case for a different particle

Every strategy in Section 3.1 accepted gold as the label and worked around its limits. The studies in this section ask a different question: if the colour of a strip is set by how much light each captured particle absorbs, why not start from a particle that absorbs more, is easier to manufacture, or comes in more than one colour? The literature offers three answers, and they map onto three distinct problems with gold. The first problem is optical. A 30 to 40 nm gold sphere absorbs strongly but only within a narrow band, and materials such as polydopamine absorb across the whole visible spectrum, which is the argument made for replacing gold in a SARS-CoV-2 serology strip (DOI: 10.3390/bios13030352); carbon, for its part, is simply black, and a black line on a white membrane gives the highest contrast a membrane can show (DOI: 10.3390/bios13090857). The second problem is manufacturing. Colloidal gold varies from batch to batch in particle size and binds antibodies mostly by adsorption, whereas dyed latex microspheres are uniform, intensely coloured and carry surface groups for covalent coupling, the reasons given for choosing them in porcine epidemic diarrhea virus and porcine circovirus 2 strips (DOI: 10.1186/s44149-021-00029-1; DOI: 10.1007/s00253-022-12074-y). The third problem is information. Gold has one colour, so a strip must separate analytes by position; a carrier that exists in red, blue and black lets one strip report two antibodies and a control by hue alone, as a strip for African swine fever virus and classical swine fever virus antibodies demonstrates (DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419).

These three problems organise what follows. The section begins with the carrier that answers the manufacturing problem, dyed latex (3.2.2), and then shows how the same large particle, once loaded with metal instead of dye, answers the optical problem as well (3.2.3). Colour coding answers the information problem (3.2.4). The remaining carriers each test one further idea: carbon tests contrast (3.2.5), selenium, silver, a pigment and magnetite test alternative hues and surfaces (3.2.6), polymer chromophores test broad absorption (3.2.7), and cellulose nanobeads test whether particle size alone can beat gold on clinical samples (3.2.8). Section 3.2.9 then turns the comparison around and asks what every coloured label gives up against a fluorescent one, and 3.2.10 draws the conclusions. Throughout, the rules of Section 3.1 apply: a change counts as an improvement only when the same study runs a gold strip or another baseline beside the new label, the target virus is named for every method, and only the colour channel is evaluated. The section covers 33 records from 2015 to 2026, of which 18 were read in full; the figures of the others are given as their abstracts state them (Tables B1a to B1g; Table B4 collects the within-study comparisons).

## 3.2.2 Dyed latex: a uniform particle, and what it does not change

A latex microsphere is a polymer bead of 200 to 400 nm, dyed throughout and carrying carboxyl groups to which antibodies are attached covalently (DOI: 10.1186/s44149-021-00029-1; DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419). Compared with a gold sphere of 30 nm it is a very large object, and this cuts both ways. The bead holds far more dye than a gold particle holds metal, but it also aggregates and settles more readily, so the protein load on its surface has to be set at the highest level that keeps the suspension stable (DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419), and even the choice between 300 and 400 nm matters: the larger bead produced a weak false-positive line that the smaller one did not (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-025-13694-w).

What a well-made latex strip delivers in practice is best seen in veterinary virology, where most of the latex work has been done. A red latex strip for porcine circovirus 2 detected the three circulating genotypes, but not equally: the endpoint titres were 10^2.5 TCID50 per 0.1 mL for PCV2a against 10^0.7 for PCV2b and 10^1.5 for PCV2d, a difference the authors trace to the immunogens used to raise the antibodies, not to the label. Against PCR on 100 tissue and swab samples the strip was fully specific (47/47) and found 43 of 53 positives (sensitivity 81.13%, kappa 0.720); some of the missed samples, the authors argue, contained viral DNA without infectious virus (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-022-12074-y). A latex strip for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (LOD 10^3.60 TCID50/mL) ran into a problem that no label can solve: solid faecal matter disturbed the flow. A filter pad inserted between the sample and conjugate pads removed it, and the strip then reached a sensitivity of 88.57% and a specificity of 100% in 108 faecal samples against RT-PCR (kappa 0.845), with recoveries of 91.00% to 114.40% and stability for 56 days at room temperature and 37 C (DOI: 10.1186/s44149-021-00029-1). Latex strips of the same kind exist for North American porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus (5 x 10^2.0 TCID50/mL; sensitivity 90.32% on clinical samples) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2022.882112), for SARS-CoV-2 antigen (25 ng/mL) (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.24091), and, in an earlier and more specialised form, for the receptor specificity of influenza A group 1 viruses, where a sialylglycopolymer-latex probe correlated above 0.95 with an ELISA (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2014.10.036).

The two studies that place plain latex beside a laboratory method with the same binders are sobering. A red 200 nm particle gave a visual LOD of 10 ng/mL for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein and detected irradiated Omicron-infected cell extract at 7.5 x 10^5 TCID50/mL, whereas a sandwich ELISA with the same antibody pair reached 15 pg/mL, almost three orders of magnitude lower (DOI: 10.1111/1751-7915.70117). In a double-antigen sandwich for SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies, latex beads gave an LOD of 7.11 ng/mL against 1.13 ng/mL for europium fluorescent beads in the same design; and yet, against the conventional virus neutralisation test, the two performed alike, latex reaching a sensitivity of 98.29% and a specificity of 97.70% in 554 sera and the fluorescent version 97.44% and 98.28% in 389 (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2022.124200). Dyed latex, then, improves uniformity, coupling and colour choice, and it can be read by eye or by smartphone; it does not by itself move the analytical limit, which stays where the antibody pair and the sample put it (Table B1a). The question of how to make the large particle also a brighter one is answered by the next group.

## 3.2.3 The same particle, loaded with metal

If the advantage of latex is its size and the advantage of gold is its absorption, the obvious experiment is to put the one on the other. Two influenza studies did exactly this, and both ran gold alone as the baseline, which is why they provide the clearest colour gains of the whole section. In the first, gold or platinum nanoparticles were deposited on poly(2-vinylpyridine) latex. The composite line is dark blue with gold and dark brown with platinum, and the light extinction of one composite particle was calculated at about 200 (gold) and 100 (platinum) times that of a 30 nm gold sphere. The lowest influenza A antigen concentration visible by eye was 6.25 x 10^-3 HAU/mL with the gold composite and 2.5 x 10^-2 HAU/mL with the platinum composite, against 4.0 x 10^-1 HAU/mL with conventional gold, a gain of more than 64- and 16-fold, although only antigen solutions were tested (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.8b11745). In the second, polystyrene latex was coated with 10 nm gold, using the same monoclonal antibody as bridge and as label so that no foreign protein was introduced. For influenza H3N2 in infected allantoic fluid the visual limit was 1/16 HAU (10^1.49 TCID50/mL), against 4 HAU for a strip made with 10 nm gold and 1 HAU for one made with 30 nm gold, again 64- and 16-fold; the strip reader put the LODs at 0.016 HAU against 1.06 and 0.562 HAU, the strip did not react with H1, H5, H7 or H9 subtypes, and it kept its visual limit for three months at room temperature (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-020-04255-1). That two independent groups, with different polymers and different gold sizes, arrive at the same 16- to 64-fold suggests that the gain belongs to the architecture rather than to one recipe.

The carrier need not be loaded with metal to become brighter. Silica nanoparticles doped with the dye Direct Red 224 detected H7 avian influenza (H7N2 and H7N9) at 0.08 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1039/d1ay00204j), and dye-loaded polymersomes carried the colour of a SARS-CoV-2 strip whose reagents were purified against 121 human samples (DOI: 10.1021/acssensors.1c00854). These two studies have no gold baseline, so the size of their gain is unknown. What the metal-latex composites establish is that the limit of Section 3.1, the optical effect of one captured particle, can be raised by changing the carrier instead of the metal; what they cost is a two-step synthesis and, so far, the absence of clinical samples.

## 3.2.4 Colour as a code

Gold reports by position: one line per analyte. A carrier that comes in several colours can report by hue, and the strip then carries more information at the same length. The clearest implementation uses red, blue and black latex for a red African swine fever virus line, a blue classical swine fever virus line and a black control. On a standard positive serum the visual endpoint was 1:256 for both antibodies, which equals the commercial ELISA for African swine fever virus (1:256) and is fourfold better than the commercial ELISA for classical swine fever virus (1:64); across 180 sera the agreement with the two ELISAs gave kappa values of 0.986 and 0.918 (DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419). The same study is candid about what colour cannot do: antibodies to the E2 protein of classical swine fever virus do not distinguish vaccinated from infected animals, so the blue line measures vaccine response rather than infection (DOI: 10.1155/tbed/5512419).

Colour can also serve the reader rather than the assay. A second African swine fever strip used red latex for the p72 test line and blue latex, made with chicken IgY, for the control, so that a valid test shows two different colours. Its visual endpoint of 1:1024 matched the commercial ELISA, it reached a sensitivity of 97.9% and a specificity of 98.2% in 159 sera (kappa 0.955), and it survived 16 months at 4 C, 12 months at room temperature and 10 months at 37 C (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-025-13694-w). The principle extends to human viruses: a bicolour latex strip measured SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies at about 48 ng/mL in 13 sera (DOI: 10.3390/bios12020103), and a red and blue strip read the two products of a duplex RT-LAMP, human norovirus GII at 5.8 copies/uL and human adenovirus F41 at 63 copies/uL (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2026.1787049). The attraction is that the strip stays equipment-free; the risk is that the result now depends on the user telling hues apart, and none of these studies reports blinded or multi-reader interpretation (Table B1b).

## 3.2.5 Carbon: contrast without gain

Carbon black is the oldest non-metal label in lateral flow, inexpensive, chemically inert and, on a white membrane, as dark as a label can be. The virology studies that use it, however, either do not test it against gold or find it wanting. A carbon black strip for severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus reached 100 pg/mL in spiked serum, with recoveries of 91.58% to 105.4%, and ran no gold comparator (DOI: 10.1039/d3an00515a). A vertical-flow immunoassay, in which the sample passes through the membrane rather than along it, used carbon black coupled to anti-human IgG to detect IgG against the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein in serum within 5 to 7 minutes, with an LOD of 7.81 BAU/mL and an optimum of 150 to 250 ug of antibody per mg of carbon; the lower LOD than colloidal gold claimed in its abstract comes from a comparison with published methods, not from a gold assay run alongside (DOI: 10.3390/bios13090857). The one study that did run both labels, for porcine deltacoronavirus, built competitive strips with the same monoclonal antibody on gold and on carbon. Carbon was one dilution step less sensitive (1:64 against 1:128 of a 10^8 TCID50/mL stock), and in 137 faecal samples against RT-PCR it reached a sensitivity of 73.2% where gold reached 83.9%, at the same specificity of 98.8% (DOI: 10.1016/j.isci.2026.116948). Contrast, it seems, is not the limiting factor of a gold strip; a faint red line and a faint black line are both faint (Table B1c).

## 3.2.6 Other hues, other surfaces: selenium, silver, ultramarine blue and magnetite

Four inorganic particles have been tried as labels, and each illustrates a different lesson. Selenium is red like gold and has been used for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies (IgM 20 ng/mL, IgG 5 ng/mL; sensitivity 93.33% and specificity 97.34% in 90 patients and 263 controls) (DOI: 10.1039/d0lc00828a; DOI: 10.1109/tnb.2021.3105662). Its most developed application, however, turns on the surface rather than the colour: PEG-stabilised selenium particles hold the antibody in a "stand-up" orientation, which the authors credit for a reading time under one minute, and the resulting SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid kit had an LOD of 10 pg/mL, defined as the lowest concentration at which the line remained visible. In 192 clinical samples (60 negative, 132 positive) it gave 100% negative, 93.94% positive and 95.83% overall coincidence with RT-PCR, in throat swabs, saliva, serum and fingerstick blood alike, without cross-reaction with common respiratory pathogens; the largest clinical dataset in this section thus belongs to a label whose LOD was never measured against gold (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2024.116878).

Silver teaches the opposite lesson. It absorbs more strongly than gold at the same size, but it is unstable in biological fluids, and the study that used it had first to solve that: a thin calix[4]arene coating made the particles stable in plasma and for six months of storage and gave them carboxyl groups to which the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein was coupled covalently. The particles are yellow. In a dipstick for anti-SARS-CoV-2 IgG they reached 5 ng/mL in buffer, an order of magnitude below citrate gold with adsorbed spike protein, and gave the same signal with ten times fewer particles. In plasma the LOD rose to 1.5 ug/mL, and in 25 sera the dipstick was fully specific (10/10) but found only 11 of 15 RT-PCR-positive patients, a rate the authors call typical of gold-based strips (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c00870). The gain, in other words, was real and was lost to the matrix. Ultramarine blue, an inexpensive pigment, was carboxylated and coupled to antibodies as a blue label for HBsAg, reaching 0.37 ng/mL (signal-to-noise 3) in spiked fetal calf serum by eye or image analysis; its abstract claims a lower LOD than a gold strip, but the comparisons in the main text are with literature values for iron oxide and red silica, and no patient samples were tested (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2019.11.037). Natural-colour magnetite (Fe3O4) combined a visible line with a magnetic core in a SARS-CoV-2 strip reaching 0.5 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2026.150960). Of the four, only silver was measured against gold in the same study, and its advantage did not survive plasma (Table B1d).

## 3.2.7 Polymer chromophores: broad absorption, and the surface problem again

Polydopamine absorbs across the whole visible spectrum, and the one study that built a virus strip on it shows that a new label brings its own surface chemistry to solve. Polydopamine adheres strongly to nitrocellulose; probes coupled directly to it would stick at the test line regardless of antigen and read as false negatives. The particles were therefore wrapped in polyelectrolyte layers, silica and a PEG-silane before antigen and antibody were attached, growing to 230 to 320 nm in the process. The finished three-line strip separated SARS-CoV-2 IgG from IgM, was quantified with a camera and image software, and gave LODs of 1.51 ng/mL (IgM) and 2.34 ng/mL (IgG) from calibration curves, while a commercial gold strip run in the same study barely detected 10 to 100 ng/mL; the matrix was serum spiked with standard antibodies (DOI: 10.3390/bios13030352). A related polymer, black polylevodopa, carries abundant carboxyl groups of its own and gave LODs of 5 pg/mL for SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid antigen and 0.1 ng/mL for influenza A H1N1 antigen, without cross-reaction with influenza B, respiratory syncytial virus or other coronavirus nucleoproteins; the higher sensitivity than gold and commercial strips that the authors state is shown only in supplementary figures, without a fold-change or a clinical sample in the main text (DOI: 10.1016/j.bbrc.2024.149821). Polydiacetylene vesicles are a reminder of the rule applied throughout this review: they show a red line and red fluorescence at once, and for HBsAg the line was visible by eye down to 1 ng/mL while 0.1 ng/mL was reached only in the fluorescence image, so only the former counts as a colorimetric result (DOI: 10.1002/asia.201700769). Broad absorption is a sound argument; the evidence that it beats gold rests, for now, on one spiked-serum comparison (Table B1e).

## 3.2.8 Cellulose nanobeads: size alone, tested on patients

The metal-latex composites of 3.2.3 showed that a large carrier can out-absorb a gold sphere, but they were tested on antigen solutions. Cellulose nanobeads, dyed cellulose particles of 330 to 365 nm sold for lateral flow in red, blue, black and green, are the one non-metal label for which the same question has been answered on clinical samples. Their first use for a virus paired them with scFv-Fc fusion proteins selected by phage display against the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein: the best pair detected 2 ng of antigen and 2.5 x 10^4 PFU of cultured virus by eye within 20 minutes, with a handheld reader for semi-quantitative results, and did not react with the nucleoproteins of MERS-CoV, SARS-CoV or influenza H1N1; gold was not run (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2020.112868). The direct comparison followed, for the same antigen, on otherwise identical strips carrying cellulose nanobeads or 40 nm gold. The authors reason that a 330 nm bead offers roughly 68 times the surface of a 40 nm sphere for the detection antibody, and the results bear this out at two levels. In buffer the lowest antigen concentration visible by eye was 0.41 ng/mL for cellulose against 3.28 ng/mL for gold, and at the gold limit the reader signal of the cellulose strip was about 20 times higher. In archived nasopharyngeal swabs read by eye, both strips were fully specific in 10 RT-qPCR-negative samples, but the weakest positive that gold still detected had a Ct of 21.31 (1.26 x 10^6 copies/mL), whereas cellulose reached a Ct of 26.10 (3.59 x 10^4 copies/mL): gold met the WHO target product profile of 10^6 copies/mL exactly, cellulose fell two orders of magnitude below it. The two labels were tested on different positive sets (32 and 23 samples), and neither detected samples with Ct above 30 (DOI: 10.1039/d5ra08197a). The carrier has since been used in a dual-colour design, red test and blue control beads, for the Nipah virus nucleocapsid protein, with an analytical LOD of 1 ng/mL and, for gamma-irradiated cultured virus quantified by RT-ddPCR, 4.1 x 10^7 copies/mL by portable reader and 8.3 x 10^7 by eye; it did not react with other respiratory virus nucleoproteins, and clinical specimens of a biosafety level 4 pathogen were not available (DOI: 10.1080/22221751.2026.2716499). Size alone, then, buys about an order of magnitude on patients, and no more: the larger particle still stops well short of the viral loads that nucleic acid tests reach (Table B1f).

## 3.2.9 The price of reading by eye

Every label in this section was chosen so that the result can be read without an instrument. A handful of studies measured what that choice costs by running a fluorescent label on the same antibodies. Fluorescent microspheres were more sensitive than coloured ones in strips for rotavirus and adenovirus and for influenza A and B, with no cross-reaction in either format (DOI: 10.1186/s12985-018-0951-5; DOI: 10.1186/s12985-019-1190-0). The SARS-CoV-2 neutralising-antibody strip of 3.2.2 put a number on the gap, about sixfold in LOD, and then showed that the gap disappeared at the diagnostic level, where latex and fluorescence agreed equally well with the neutralisation test (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2022.124200). For antibodies to the human adenovirus 3 hexon protein, quantum dots reached a positive percent agreement of 100% against 96.9% for gold, at a negative agreement of 98.2%, in 90 sera (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics16162670). The pattern is consistent and useful: a coloured label gives up a few-fold to an order of magnitude in analytical LOD against a fluorescent one on the same binders, and that loss matters only when the clinical cut-off falls between the two limits (Table B1g).

## 3.2.10 What the evidence supports

Replacing gold changes what a strip can do more than how strong its colour is. Dyed latex brings uniformity, covalent coupling and a choice of colours, and it has become the label of veterinary antigen strips for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus, porcine circovirus 2, porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus and African and classical swine fever virus; multicolour latex lets one strip report several analytes by hue. The colour gains that can be measured against gold in the same study come from a single principle, particle size: metal-loaded latex lowered the visual limit for influenza antigen 16- to 64-fold below 10 to 30 nm gold, and cellulose nanobeads lowered it eightfold in buffer and detected SARS-CoV-2 in swabs at a viral load about 35 times lower than gold. The other comparisons are small or negative: calixarene-coated silver gained an order of magnitude in buffer and lost it in plasma, polydopamine quantified low antibody concentrations better than a commercial gold strip in spiked serum, and carbon was less sensitive than gold in the one clinical comparison (Table B4). Against fluorescent labels on the same binders, every coloured label was less sensitive. Selenium, ultramarine blue, magnetite, polylevodopa, red silica and polymersomes rest on single studies without a gold comparator.

Three limitations recur. Reference methods differ between studies (PCR, RT-PCR, commercial ELISA, virus neutralisation), so agreement values cannot be compared across them. Several studies used spiked or standard samples only, and the silver study, whose gain in buffer vanished in plasma, shows what that omission can hide. And genotype or strain coverage depends on the antibody pair rather than on the label, as the porcine circovirus 2 strip makes explicit.
