# Part A (final working text): gold-nanoparticle engineering

Working text, 2026-11-05. All DOIs below were checked against the evidence table; statements marked as abstract-only are not to be used for detail that needs the full paper. Wording and length will be adjusted when the whole review is assembled.

# 3.1 Gold nanoparticle engineering

## 3.1.0 Scope, evidence base and reporting conventions

Gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) are the most common colorimetric label in lateral flow immunoassays (LFIA). One group notes that many lateral flow platforms use 20-60 nm AuNPs because of their strong visible-light absorption (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). The limitation that most engineering work tries to address is a high limit of detection (LOD) (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229). Panferov and colleagues describe two broad routes: changing the physicochemical properties of the label, and increasing the amount of label or its derivative products at the test line (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229). Another group points out that enlarging AuNPs or clustering them has produced only limited gains, because labels larger than about 200 nm diffuse poorly through the membrane (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z).

This section covers 97 included records on AuNP-based colorimetric strips, 93 of them published in 2015-2026 and four before 2015. The records that report a distinct engineering step or quantitative data are discussed individually. Strips that use conventional colloidal gold without a methodological contribution are grouped in section 3.1.5 and listed in the supplementary table. Eight papers were read in full; they are marked with a dagger in Table A1. All other statements rest on abstracts alone and carry no information that an abstract does not give.

Reporting conventions. LODs are given as stated, with units, matrix and readout (naked eye, flatbed scanner or imager, smartphone, or dedicated reader). Fold-improvements are reported only where a paper compares its own modification with its own comparator, and the comparator is named. Analytical LOD is kept separate from diagnostic sensitivity and specificity. Values that a paper does not report are recorded as "not reported". No ranking across studies is attempted, because comparators, matrices, targets and readouts differ.

## 3.1.1 Particle size and conjugation chemistry

Particle diameter was varied for potato virus X in a study published in 2012, before the 2015 focus window and included as a foundation. The LOD in leaf extract fell from 80 to 3 ng/mL as particle diameter increased from 6.4 to 33.4 nm, and was 9 ng/mL at 52 nm (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-012-5985-8). For hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), AuNPs of 34.2-137.8 nm were used to build a size-controlled strip; the abstract does not give the resulting LOD (DOI: 10.3390/s16122154). A dengue NS1 test built on 80 nm AuNPs gave serotype-specific LODs of 2.9 (DENV-1), 0.5 (DENV-2), 25.2 (DENV-3) and 4.5 ng/mL (DENV-4), and was evaluated on 185 archived plasma samples against an NS1 ELISA; the abstract does not give the accuracy values (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics16030395).

Conjugation chemistry has also been examined. Hydrazide-assisted oriented conjugation of an HBsAg antibody to AuNPs reduced the amount of labelled antibody needed (0.2 mg per pmol of AuNPs); the LOD is not reported in the abstract (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2021.338623). Adding EDTA-K2 to chelate the colloidal gold surface improved the sensitivity of a SARS-CoV-2 IgM/IgG test without false positives, but no values are given in the abstract (DOI: 10.2147/ijn.s281594). Two studies of the protein corona on AuNP probes report that corona composition affects binding in dengue and Zika NS1 sandwich immunoassays (DOI: 10.1021/acs.bioconjchem.6b00523; DOI: 10.3390/pharmaceutics14112439), and a further study found that the aggregation state of the immunoprobe strongly influences dipstick performance (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.0c08628). These studies are mechanistic; none of the abstracts reports a clinical evaluation.

## 3.1.2 On-strip enlargement and signal amplification

Enlargement of the AuNPs on the membrane after the immunoreaction keeps the colorimetric readout and adds a step. Panferov and colleagues compared three post-assay methods on the same strip format, using the same antibodies and the same 22.8 nm AuNPs, for SARS-CoV-2 receptor-binding domain (RBD) spiked into saliva from one healthy volunteer (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229). The LOD was 488 pg/mL for the conventional strip, 61 pg/mL after silver enhancement, 8 pg/mL after galvanic-assisted gold deposition (the authors' new step) and 1 pg/mL after gold enhancement, which corresponds to a 488-fold reduction between the conventional strip and gold enhancement, calculated from the stated values (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229). These LODs were calculated from grayscale images of a flatbed scanner, not from visual reading. Galvanic and gold enhancement produced nonspecific colouring of the test zone, and the authors needed an optical quantification instrument to reach 1 pg/mL (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229). With inactivated SARS-CoV-2, gold enhancement detected the virus at a 500-fold higher dilution than the conventional strip. No patient samples were tested (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229).

Growth of 20 nm gold particles to about 350 nm after the assay lowered the LOD for potato virus X 240-fold compared with a conventional LFIA, in potato leaf samples (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-018-3052-7). A self-enhancement strip for SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein in saliva reached 0.50 ng/mL by naked eye and 0.10 ng/mL with calibration, about 10 times lower than a conventional strip (DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2023.133898). Gold nanoflowers of 135 nm combined with gold staining reached 25 pg/mL for SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, and the nanoflowers were 5 times more sensitive than AuNPs of the same size (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-024-06502-1).

Electrophoresis-assisted multilayer assembly of AuNPs through biotin-streptavidin binding increased the sensitivity of an HBV assay from 73% to 98% on 118 clinical samples, while specificity stayed at 95%; the reference method is not stated in the abstract (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202215548).

Signal amplification is not limited to antigen assays. In a serological strip for SARS-CoV-2 N-protein IgM and IgG, a complex of anti-N IgG on gold bound to five N-protein-gold conjugates increased the test-line signal by more than two-fold over N-protein-gold alone (DOI: 10.1002/gch2.202200008). The text describes 150 nm gold nanoshells, whereas the abstract says gold nanoparticles. The LOD is expressed as ELISA optical density (visual detection at 0.55 for IgM and 0.4 for IgG, equal to the ELISA cut-off) and not as a concentration (DOI: 10.1002/gch2.202200008). Against an in-house ELISA, 112 RT-PCR-positive and 52 pre-pandemic sera gave a sensitivity of 94% for IgM (103/109) and 96% for IgG (107/112), and a specificity of 93% for IgM (51/55) and 98% for IgG (51/52), read with a smartphone and ImageJ from 2 uL of serum (DOI: 10.1002/gch2.202200008). The comparator is therefore an ELISA on the same antibody class, not PCR.

## 3.1.3 Alternative plasmonic structures

Clusters. Plasmon coupling in close-packed AuNP clusters shifts absorption into the near-infrared and weakens the visible colour. Oh and colleagues avoided this with streptavidin-core clusters whose biotinylated-antibody satellites keep gaps of about 15 nm, so the red colour is preserved (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Read with an imager (signal-to-noise ratio 5), the LOD for SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein was 0.910 ng/mL for 15 nm AuNPs, 0.226 ng/mL for 40 nm AuNPs, 0.551 ng/mL for a 40-15 cluster and 0.038 ng/mL for a 40-40 cluster, which is 5.9 and 23.8 times lower than the 40 nm and 15 nm single particles (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). The 40-15 cluster was less sensitive than single 40 nm particles. In saliva spiked with virus (12-355 TCID50/mL) the LOD was 54 TCID50/mL; the authors call this a clinical feasibility test, but it used spiked saliva and no patient samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Gold dendrimer nanochains with a red-to-blue shift reached 5 ng/mL for HIV p24 in pseudo-serum (DOI: 10.1039/c7tb01394a), and core-satellite SiO2@Au particles reached 0.24 pg/mL for SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, stated as two orders of magnitude below colloidal gold (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.2c13303).

Dimers. AuNP dimers prepared by silver-ion soldering (30 nm AuNPs; plasmon peaks at 520 and 630 nm) gave a naked-eye LOD of 2.0 x 10^2 TCID50/mL for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus, while a commercial colloidal-gold strip gave only a faint line at 1.0 x 10^4 TCID50/mL, hence the stated 50-fold (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06013-5). The comparator is a commercial strip with its own antibodies, so the figure combines label and antibody effects. On 109 pig faecal samples against gel-read RT-PCR (90 positive, 19 negative) the agreement was 100%, with no discordant sample; the authors note that dimers are difficult to produce at scale and that long-term stability was not tested (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06013-5).

Hollow alloy shells. Hollow Ag-rich Ag-Au nanoshells (about 40 nm, 6 nm wall) used as the label in a bovine viral diarrhea virus NS3 antigen strip gave a visual LOD of 1.95 x 10^3 TCID50/mL, against 7.81 x 10^3 TCID50/mL for the authors' own AuNP strip using the same antibodies, a 4-fold difference under controlled conditions (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-026-08241-x). Against a commercial NS3 antigen ELISA, 215 bovine serum and ear-tissue samples gave 45 true positives, 3 false negatives, 1 false positive and 166 true negatives: positive agreement 93.75% (45/48), negative agreement 99.40% (166/167) and overall agreement 98.14% (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-026-08241-x). The reference is an ELISA, not RT-PCR, and serum and tissue results are pooled. The strip detected BVDV-1a, 1b, 1c, 1d, 1m and BVDV-2, was negative for classical swine fever virus, infectious bovine rhinotracheitis virus, bovine parainfluenza virus 3 and Mycoplasma bovis, and kept more than 85% of its signal after 21 days at 37 C in an accelerated test (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-026-08241-x).

Other composite particles. Au/Fe3O4 core-shell particles detected avian influenza H7 at 10^3.5 EID50, with 7 positives among 200 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1039/c8ra00185e). Magnetic-gold nanostars reached 2.24 pg/mL for Rift Valley fever virus nucleoprotein, stated as 1000 times lower than conventional AuNP assays, but only in PBS buffer (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.5c21544). Two-wavelength imaging of composite nanoparticles reached 170 fM for HIV capsid protein, 58-fold better than single-wavelength imaging of 50 nm AuNPs (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114133); this relies on instrument imaging rather than naked-eye reading.

Boundary case: plasmonic scattering. Lee and colleagues make the nitrocellulose membrane transparent by refractive-index matching and place it over a black backing card, so that 100 nm AuNPs appear as orange lines by scattering, not by absorption (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). The authors describe the principle as distinct from absorbance-based colorimetric detection, and the author team of this review decided to keep it in the section as a flagged boundary case with its LOD reported separately. For H1N1 particles the LOD was 2.09 pfu/mL, against 755.47 pfu/mL for the authors' own conventional 40 nm AuNP strip (361-fold) and 5,417-9,219 pfu/mL for three commercial strips (the stated 2600-4400-fold); for nucleocapsid protein it was 1.17 against 234.69 pg/mL (201-fold) (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). On 30 qRT-PCR-positive influenza A swabs and 12 healthy controls the sensitivity was 90% (27/30) and the specificity 100%, against 23-30% for the commercial strips and 40% for the authors' conventional strip (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). The clinical samples were diluted to 10% in buffer, which the authors acknowledge disadvantages the commercial strips. The lines were read with a gel imager, and a smartphone camera gave the same positive rate (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z).

## 3.1.4 Replacing the antibody on the particle

Nanobodies. A strip for SARS-CoV-2 S1 antigen used AuNP-nanobody conjugates as detector and AuNP-ACE2 as test-line capture probe, read with a gel imager (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-37347-y). The LOD for gamma-irradiated virus was 4 times lower with the nanobody than with a monoclonal antibody in an otherwise identical strip (0.4 versus 1.6, x 10^n copies/mL; the exponent could not be recovered from the retrieved text and must be checked against the PDF) (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-37347-y). In saliva from 320 symptomatic people (180 RT-PCR-positive, 140 negative), the nanobody strip reached a sensitivity of 97.14% and a specificity of 98.57% at Ct values of 30 or below; the abstract gives 90.04% sensitivity for the monoclonal strip at the same Ct, while the results text gives 83.33%, so the comparison needs checking against the tables (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-37347-y). Other nanobody strips report abstract-level data only: 500 pg/mL for SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein with a double-Y nanobody assembly (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.4c00509), picomolar sensitivity for S1 with multivalent nanobodies (DOI: 10.1039/d4nr02762k), 10^3.15 PFU/mL (DOI: 10.1039/d5nr02568k), 40 ng/mL (DOI: 10.3390/bios16020132), and 1.56 ng/mL for chikungunya virus E protein against 49 pg/mL for a nanobody ELISA (DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2025.1707358). A nanobody strip for human norovirus gave a sensitivity of 80% and a specificity of 86% on outbreak specimens (DOI: 10.1128/msphere.00219-16).

Aptamers. A competitive strip with a DNA aptamer on AuNPs, in which the test line weakens as antigen increases, detected SARS-CoV-2 spike protein on cold-chain food packaging with an LOD of 91.2 ng/mL (0.68 nM, 3 sigma/slope) using a dedicated reader; the visual range was 0.1-1 ug/mL and recoveries were 83.3-108.8% (relative standard deviation 2.3-6.2%) in thawed package-ice solutions spiked with recombinant spike protein (DOI: 10.3390/molecules29081776). No virus or patient samples were tested, and the authors state that the sensitivity is lower than that of other methods (DOI: 10.3390/molecules29081776). Spike N-terminal-domain aptamers reached 5 x 10^5 copies/mL with UV-inactivated virus (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202107730), an antibody-free aptamer sandwich 10^6 copies/mL (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c00554), and a hybrid aptamer-antibody format 0.1-0.5 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1186/s12951-023-02191-9).

Other binders. Sialic-acid glyconanoparticles detected spike protein through glycan recognition (DOI: 10.1021/acscentsci.0c00855), a lectin-based strip was applied to patient samples for severe dengue (DOI: 10.1080/15321819.2023.2260480), and a miniprotein binder was used for variants of concern (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06113-2). The abstracts report no accuracy data.

## 3.1.5 Conventional gold strips without engineering

A large share of the 97 records describe new colloidal-gold strips with no engineering step. Many target SARS-CoV-2 antigen or antibody. Examples with clinical data in the abstract: a multi-centre IgG/IgM strip correctly identified 158 of 170 patients and 296 of 300 controls (area under the curve 0.958, kappa 0.926) (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.23619); another IgM strip reached a sensitivity of 90.67% and a specificity of 97.84% on 75 cases and 139 controls (DOI: 10.1002/ctm2.90); an RBD antigen strip read by smartphone gave a sensitivity of 94.3% and a specificity of 90.9% against RT-PCR on 100 clinical samples, with an LOD of 1 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.28416). In veterinary virology a canine parvovirus strip had an LOD of 6.6 x 10^5 TCID50/mL on faecal samples and a sensitivity/specificity of 71%/92% against haemagglutination and 71%/100% against a commercial immunochromatographic test (DOI: 10.1007/s00705-018-3846-2). Two further animal-virus strips, for avian leukosis virus p27 (LOD 1.53 ng/mL against 0.2 ng/mL by ELISA, 35 egg samples) and for monkeypox virus A29 (50 pg/mL of purified antigen, no cross-reaction with vaccinia or cowpox), are currently known from abstracts only (DOI: 10.1039/d4an00180j; DOI: 10.1039/d2an02043b).

## 3.1.6 Summary of the evidence in this section

Within-paper improvements for engineered AuNP labels range from about 4-fold to several hundred-fold, depending on the comparator: the paper's own conventional strip (for example 4-fold for nanoshells, 5.9- and 23.8-fold for clusters, 361-fold for scattering), AuNPs of the same size (5-fold for nanoflowers), or a commercial strip (50-fold for dimers; 2600-4400-fold for scattering). Because the comparators differ, these numbers should not be ranked against each other.

Three limitations recur. First, many LODs come from scanners, imagers or readers and not from the naked eye. Second, clinical evaluation is the exception among engineering papers. Where it exists, sample numbers range from 42 to 320 samples and the reference method varies (ELISA, RT-PCR, gel-read RT-PCR). Several engineering papers tested only spiked buffer, saliva or recombinant protein. Third, conjugation and surface-chemistry studies are largely mechanistic and give few diagnostic data.

Two key papers for this section, the avian leukosis virus and monkeypox strips, have not yet been read in full. Their statements remain at abstract level and are marked as such in Table A1.
