Each subsection follows the same four questions: what limits the signal at this point, which substances or technologies address it, what the papers report against their own baseline, and what the change costs. The target virus is named for every method in the text and in the tables. Numbers are in the tables; Table A4 gathers the within-paper changes in the colour signal. † = read in full; all other rows are abstract only (NR = not reported in the abstract). # 3.1 Gold nanoparticles: how engineering changes the colorimetric signal

## 3.1.0 What limits the colour at the test line, and how this section is organised

In a colorimetric lateral flow immunoassay (LFIA) the result is the colour that builds up at the test line. The strength of that colour depends on three things: how many particles the sandwich captures, how strongly each captured particle changes the white membrane, and how clearly that change can be seen against the background. Gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) became the default label because they absorb visible light strongly, are easy to make and are easy to couple to antibodies (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Their weakness is a high limit of detection (LOD) when only a few particles reach the line (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229).

The studies in this section are arranged by which of these limits they act on. The first group improves how many particles reach and bind at the line, by tuning the particle and what it carries (3.1.1). The second leaves the particle unchanged and makes the captured gold more visible after the immunoreaction (3.1.2). The third replaces the sphere with a structure that has a larger optical effect per particle (3.1.3). The fourth changes the binder or antigen that decides what the particle captures (3.1.4). The fifth deals with the assay format, which sets what the line can show (3.1.5). The sixth changes the optics so the background hides less signal (3.1.6), and the last deals with conventional strips that apply none of these changes and serve as baseline (3.1.7). Section 3.1.8 states what the evidence supports.

Table A4 collects the reported change in the colour signal for the studies that report one against their own baseline. Papers read in full are marked † in the tables. The section covers 93 included records, all from 2015-2026.

**How the signal is read.** Reading the abstracts of the 93 records in this section, 25 state that the strip is read by eye, 1 state that a device is used (smartphone, reader, scanner or imager), 4 state either eye or device, 62 describe a strip without saying how it is read, and 1 has no abstract. 3 records report a second readout or a non-colour comparator (fluorescence, SERS, thermal, electrochemical); they are marked ‡ in the tables, and only the colorimetric channel is counted as a result of this review. "Not stated" means only that the abstract is silent.

## 3.1.1 Getting the standard particle right: size and surface

The first design question is the particle itself. Large AuNPs carry more colour, but they also move more slowly through the membrane and may not reach the test line in useful numbers (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229; DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). Studies that vary particle diameter treat this trade-off directly: a hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) strip was built around a series of particle sizes (34.2-137.8 nm), and a dengue virus NS1 strip around large (80 nm) particles (DOI: 10.3390/s16122154; DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics16030395).

What sits on the gold matters as much as the gold itself. Oriented conjugation lets an antibody bind through a defined site and reduces the amount of antibody needed, shown on an HBsAg strip for hepatitis B virus (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2021.338623). An additive that chelates the gold surface (EDTA-K2) improved a SARS-CoV-2 IgM/IgG antibody test without raising false positives (DOI: 10.2147/ijn.s281594). Studies of the protein corona and of the aggregation state of the probe show that the layer of adsorbed protein changes how the probe binds in NS1 sandwich assays for Zika virus and dengue virus (one of the three is a dengue model system) (DOI: 10.1021/acs.bioconjchem.6b00523; DOI: 10.3390/pharmaceutics14112439; DOI: 10.1021/acsami.0c08628).

These studies are valuable because they explain why nominally identical strips perform differently. Most of them, however, are mechanistic, and their abstracts report few diagnostic data (Table A1a). Even a well-tuned conventional strip reaches a limit, which leads to the next approach: making more of the gold that has already been captured.

Several further studies change how the antibody is attached to the gold or what else is on the particle. A comparison of antibody-conjugation strategies in dengue NS1 sandwich assays is mechanistic and gives no values in its abstract (DOI: 10.1080/15321819.2016.1269338). Bacterial protein A and protein G on gold were used as flexible detectors for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies and tested on 105 sera (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-022-03939-2), and a recombinant streptavidin fusion protein served as the signal reporter in a hepatitis C virus antibody strip tested on patient sera (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.22701). The particle itself is varied: laser-ablated gold nanoparticles for the SARS-CoV-2 spike antigen (DOI: 10.5599/admet.2079); hollow gold nanoparticles, where the interaction between particle and antibody was studied to sensitise detection of the HIV p24 antigen (DOI: 10.1039/d4ra05277c); and gold magnetic nanoparticles in a TORCH IgM strip (rubella virus, cytomegalovirus and herpes simplex virus), which reported 100% sensitivity and specificity in 162/162 sera and higher sensitivity than commercial colloidal-gold strips, with the value not given in the abstract (DOI: 10.3892/ijmm.2015.2333). Ligands matched to the target include an oseltamivir derivative on gold that distinguished oseltamivir-resistant from wild-type influenza virus (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-018-31311-x), dextrin-capped gold in a nucleic-acid biosensor for dengue-1 RNA with a cut-off of 1.2 x 10^4 PFU/mL (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics9030074), and gold-oligonucleotide nanoconstructs designed for conserved enterovirus sequences, whose abstract reports detection of 96% of known enteroviruses (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070238). A thermoplasmonic gold-nanoprism lateral flow assay for SARS-CoV-2 RNA is listed here as a boundary case, because its signal is read from heat and not from colour alone (DOI: 10.1002/smll.202514946).

The limit addressed in 3.1.1 is therefore the number of active particles at the line. The cost is that gains from conjugation chemistry are hard to see in abstracts, because few of these papers give a value against their own baseline strip.

## 3.1.2 Making more of the captured gold: enlargement and amplification

After the sandwich has formed, the particles at the test line can act as seeds. Depositing more metal on them enlarges them, and larger or more irregular particles colour the line more strongly (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229). Panferov and colleagues compared silver enhancement, a new gold-after-silver step based on galvanic replacement, and gold enhancement on one strip format for the SARS-CoV-2 receptor-binding domain with the same antibodies and the same AuNPs. Gold enhancement gave the lowest LOD, by more than two orders of magnitude over the unenhanced strip, and used stable reagents, but it brought nonspecific colouring and needed an optical instrument to reach the lowest value (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229). The pattern repeats in other studies: growing 20 nm particles into much larger ones after the assay, which for potato virus X in potato leaf extracts turned most of the conventional strip's false negatives into positives (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-018-3052-7), a self-enhancement design for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein in saliva (DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2023.133898), and gold nanoflowers combined with gold staining for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, which were tested in buffer and artificial saliva only (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-024-06502-1). Each lowers the LOD within its own strip format. Each also adds a step beyond the conventional run, and the lowest values often rely on calibration or a reader.

A different route is to build up the label at the line instead of growing it. Layer-by-layer assembly of AuNPs through biotin-streptavidin binding, driven by electrophoresis, lowered the LOD of a hepatitis B surface antigen strip by more than 60-fold (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202215548). The design is a two-stage workflow: the conventional strip is read first, and enhancement is applied only to samples that look negative or weakly positive. On 118 clinical samples this raised diagnostic sensitivity against a laboratory chemiluminescent assay without changing specificity, but it needs a small power supply, a holder and an extra reagent step (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202215548). In SARS-CoV-2 antibody (serology) strips, a complex of antibody-gold and antigen-gold conjugates increased the test-line signal over antigen-gold alone, and brought the visual limit to the cut-off of the reference ELISA (DOI: 10.1002/gch2.202200008).

The trade-off in this group is consistent. The gains are real and often large, but they are paid for with extra reagents, extra time, more background or dependence on instrument reading (Table A1b). That limits their use where the strip must work with no equipment. The alternative is to design a better particle in advance.

## 3.1.3 Better particles: clusters, dimers, shells and composites

The optical response of a particle depends on its size, shape, composition and the distance to its neighbours. Several groups exploit this to give each captured particle more effect on the line.

Clusters add absorption by putting several particles at each binding event. The difficulty is plasmon coupling: when particles sit closer than about 10 nm the absorption shifts towards the near-infrared and the visible colour fades (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Streptavidin-core clusters held about 15 nm apart by biotinylated antibodies, tested on the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, avoid this and keep the red colour, and the 40-40 cluster outperformed single particles, whereas the 40-15 cluster did not (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Gold dendrimer nanochains with a red-to-blue shift have also been tested for HIV p24 (DOI: 10.1039/c7tb01394a). Core-satellite SiO2@Au particles, in which a silica core carries many small gold satellites, lowered the LOD by two orders of magnitude against 40 nm gold colloids with the same assay, although only recombinant SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein and inactivated culture virus were tested and the visual limit was much higher than the calculated LOD (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.2c13303).

Dimers use the opposite logic. Two particles joined with a sub-nanometre gap couple strongly and show two plasmon peaks, giving a stronger signal than single spheres, and they gave a clearly lower naked-eye LOD than a commercial strip for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus, with full agreement with RT-PCR on faecal samples (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06013-5). The authors note that dimers are hard to prepare at scale and that their long-term stability has not been tested (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06013-5).

Hollow alloy shells widen the absorption band. Silver-rich Ag-Au nanoshells have two plasmon peaks and a larger integrated absorbance than same-size AuNPs. Used as label in a bovine viral diarrhea virus (BVDV) strip, they gave a lower visual LOD than the authors' own AuNP strip with the same antibodies, and good agreement with a commercial ELISA on field samples (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-026-08241-x).

Composite particles combine gold with a second material, for example a magnetic Fe3O4 core used as a performance enhancer in an avian influenza H7 strip (DOI: 10.1039/c8ra00185e) or magnetic-gold nanostars for the Rift Valley fever virus nucleoprotein, which add magnetic pre-concentration to a spiked plasmonic shell and gave a very large gain over the authors' own gold-sphere strip in buffer and spiked serum, but require a magnet and a pre-incubation step and were not tested on patient samples (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.5c21544). One study used two-wavelength imaging of composite particles for the HIV capsid protein, which gives a large gain but depends on an imager rather than the eye (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114133).

The strongest evidence in this group comes from papers that compare their particle with a conventional strip made with the same antibodies (clusters, shells). Others compare with commercial strips, which mixes label and antibody effects (dimers), or have been tested only in buffer (nanostars) (Table A1c). All of these designs still read absorbed colour against a white membrane. One study changes that.

## 3.1.4 Replacing the binder and choosing the antigen

A brighter label cannot help if the binder is weak. A parallel line of work therefore replaces the conventional antibody with a binder that is smaller, more stable or easier to produce.

Nanobodies are small single-domain fragments. In a SARS-CoV-2 spike S1 strip, a nanobody-gold detector with an ACE2-gold capture probe lowered the LOD compared with a monoclonal-antibody version of the same strip, and gave higher sensitivity at low viral loads in saliva (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-37347-y). The abstract and the results text disagree on the comparator's sensitivity, so this comparison needs checking against the source tables (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-37347-y). Other nanobody designs, including multivalent and Y-shaped assemblies, have been reported for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid and spike S1 proteins and for the chikungunya virus E protein, and a nanobody strip has been used on human norovirus outbreak specimens (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.4c00509; DOI: 10.1039/d4nr02762k; DOI: 10.1039/d5nr02568k; DOI: 10.3390/bios16020132; DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2025.1707358; DOI: 10.1128/msphere.00219-16).

Aptamers are chemically synthesised and can be produced more reproducibly and cheaply than antibodies (DOI: 10.3390/molecules29081776). They can be used in sandwich or competitive formats. A competitive aptamer strip for SARS-CoV-2 spike protein on food packaging used an aptamer chosen for broad binding to variant spike proteins, but its sensitivity was lower than other methods and it was tested only with recombinant protein (DOI: 10.3390/molecules29081776). Other aptamer strips for SARS-CoV-2 (spike protein, inactivated virus and nucleocapsid protein) were evaluated with inactivated virus or spiked samples (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202107730; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c00554; DOI: 10.1186/s12951-023-02191-9).

Further binders include sialic-acid glyconanoparticles that recognise spike through glycans, a lectin for severe dengue virus infection, and a miniprotein binder aimed at SARS-CoV-2 variants of concern (DOI: 10.1021/acscentsci.0c00855; DOI: 10.1080/15321819.2023.2260480; DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06113-2). Their abstracts give no accuracy data (Table A1d).

In principle, binder replacement is promising for supply independence and for coping with sequence variation. The evidence so far is mostly analytical, and the clearest like-for-like comparison is the nanobody strip.

Two further parts of the strip decide what the particle can capture: the pair of antibodies, and the antigen used in serology strips. For SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid, two antibody pairs (NP-mAb-7 and -40) reached 8 pg of nucleocapsid protein and 625 TCID50/mL of virus on 60 clinical samples (DOI: 10.3390/ijms222212412), and a detection antibody aimed at a linear epitope reached 10 pg/mL (DOI: 10.3390/ijms25084436). Comparing nucleocapsid and spike as targets for strips was the subject of one study (DOI: 10.1016/j.molimm.2021.01.005). In serology strips the antigen can be the variable: an acid-treated p72 antigen on gold detected African swine fever virus antibodies at a 1:10,000 serum dilution (DOI: 10.3389/fchem.2021.804981); the protrusion domain of the infectious bursal disease virus VP2 protein was used as a highly immunogenic diagnostic antigen (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-020-10458-6); plant-produced receptor-binding domain gave sensitivity 94.1% and specificity 98% on 51 sera (DOI: 10.3877/cma.j.issn.2096-112x.2021.01.006); and baculovirus-expressed nucleocapsid and receptor-binding domain were tested on 103 convalescent sera (DOI: 10.1007/s10529-022-03316-0). The shell domain of norovirus was chosen as the antigen for a broad-range strip tested on 122 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1186/s12866-020-02084-z).

Aptamers and synthetic binders continue the theme. A pair of sandwich aptamers detected whole avian influenza H5N2 particles with enhanced specificity over other H5Nx viruses (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2019.03.061), an aptamer system for H3N2 selected by virus-based SELEX is a scope check, because the abstract describes no strip data (DOI: 10.1166/jbn.2019.2789), an aptamer-based isolation step was combined with a strip for grouper nervous necrosis virus (5 ng/mL coat protein) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2020.00886), a nanogold-and-aptamer proof of concept was proposed for at-home detection of SARS-CoV-2 mutants (DOI: 10.1016/j.nano.2022.102590), and a DNA aptamer for the Omicron receptor-binding domain was developed for multiplexed detection and pseudovirus neutralisation, again a scope check (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c01993). A hydrogel nanoparticle acting as an abiotic antibody mimic on a gold core was developed for SARS-CoV-2 spike detection (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2025.129324).

In every case in this group the abstract gives either a detection limit or a specificity statement, not a like-for-like comparison with an antibody version of the same strip. The exception is the nanobody strip above (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-37347-y).

## 3.1.5 Assay format: what the line is able to show

Even with a good label, the format of the immunoassay limits what colour can mean. In a sandwich strip, too much antigen can suppress the line (the hook effect). A study of lumpy skin disease virus investigated this effect directly, reporting a detection limit of 10^3.4 TCID50/mL, a coefficient of variation below 5.3% and no cross-reaction with 12 viruses (DOI: 10.3390/bios12090739). In competitive formats the line gets weaker as the target increases, which suits small analytes and functional assays: a competitive gold strip for SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies gave 20.66 IU/L with 100% specificity (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.2c03677), and a biomimetic mock-virus particle with trimeric SARS-CoV-2 spike measured neutralising antibody at 59 IU/mL, benchmarked to the WHO standard (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04372). Quantitative comparison of kits is another use of the format: a quantitative colloidal-gold assay measured the detection limit of nine approved SARS-CoV-2 antigen kits against the nucleocapsid protein and found nucleic-acid testing at least 1835 times more sensitive than antigen testing on a Delta strain (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2023.124462). A Japanese encephalitis virus NS1 strip with a smartphone colorimeter reached 10 pg/mL by eye in spiked serum and was stable for one month at 4 C (DOI: 10.1039/d2na00463a).

The limit addressed here is interpretation: whether the colour on the line has a one-to-one relation with the amount of target. The cost is that each format has a different dynamic range, and most of these abstracts do not state it.

## 3.1.6 Changing the optics: plasmonic scattering as a boundary case
All the strategies above increase absorbed colour. One study instead removes the white background that hides a different optical signal. When the nitrocellulose membrane is made transparent by refractive-index matching and placed over a black backing card, large AuNPs appear as orange lines by scattering rather than absorption, and the background almost disappears; the study targeted influenza A virus nucleoprotein (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). The authors report a very large gain against their own conventional strip and against commercial strips, a higher sensitivity on a small set of clinical swabs, and a naked-eye or smartphone readout (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). The approach needs an extra reagent step and sufficient light, and the authors place it beyond absorbance-based colorimetric detection. We therefore keep it as a flagged boundary case and report its figures separately from absorption-based strips (Table A1c).

## 3.1.7 Conventional gold strips without engineering

Many included records describe new colloidal-gold strips that apply none of the steps above. They serve as baseline: they show what the unmodified format achieves, and they are the strips that the engineering papers set out to improve. Their abstracts rarely give a within-paper change, so they are summarised here by target, with values in Table A1f.

For SARS-CoV-2 antibodies, IgM and IgG strips reported sensitivity and specificity against patient sera: sensitivity 100% and specificity 93.3% (kappa 0.872) for IgM (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.0c01554), sensitivity 90% and specificity 96.6% against real-time PCR on 60 samples (DOI: 10.1089/mab.2021.0027), and others without values in the abstract (DOI: 10.1039/d0an00629g; DOI: 10.1097/cm9.0000000000000922); the colloidal-gold assays in Table A1e add 158/170 positives with AUC 0.958 (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.23619) and IgM sensitivity of 90.67% (DOI: 10.1002/ctm2.90). A nasal secretory IgA strip detected spike-specific IgA purified from people infected with BA.5 (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2024.1386891). For SARS-CoV-2 antigen, a spike strip detected 62.5 ng/mL in a subunit vaccine (DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2021.635677), a nucleocapsid kit more than 0.2 ng/mL recombinant protein without cross-reaction (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2021.114183), a gold-nanoparticle receptor-binding-domain strip detected 1 ng/mL with sensitivity 94.3% and specificity 90.9% against RT-PCR on 100 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.28416), a nanogold test reached sensitivity 95% and specificity 98% on clinical specimens (DOI: 10.3390/nano12142477), another detected 2 ng/mL with 98% concordance (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07299-3), and the CoV-SCAN strip detected 10 variants at 1.6-12.5 TCID50/swab (DOI: 10.1016/j.jcvp.2022.100080). A nucleic-acid sandwich strip for the N3 gene reached 5 pM (DOI: 10.1016/j.jbiosc.2022.11.001).

For other human viruses: an HIV-1 p24 strip with in-house monoclonal antibodies reached 25 pg/mL and detected 10/10 positives with specificity 98.03% (DOI: 10.1155/2016/6743904); an SFTSV strip reached 1 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.8b02366); a feline coronavirus antibody strip was tested on plasma, serum, whole blood and ascitic fluid (DOI: 10.1007/978-1-4939-2438-7_3); a dengue competitive strip with 4G2 reached 5.12 x 10^2 PFU (DOI: 10.3390/bios12070495); a varicella-zoster strip reached 30 ng/mL gE without cross-reactivity with EV71, HSV-1 or HSV-2 (DOI: 10.1016/j.virol.2023.07.008); and an HTLV-I IgG strip showed sensitivity 96.7% and specificity 100% (DOI: 10.3390/v15010129). For animal and aquatic viruses: a nervous necrosis virus strip for whole virions on fish tissue (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-020-63553-z), porcine circovirus 2 at 10^3.18 TCID50/mL (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.01953-22), pseudorabies virus at 1.336 x 10^3 copies/uL (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2024.1399123), a canine parvovirus strip on faecal samples (sensitivity 71%, specificity 92% against haemagglutination) (DOI: 10.1007/s00705-018-3846-2), canine parvovirus strips, one in colloidal gold and fluorescent format on 60 clinical samples and one in nanogold with sensitivity 96.4% and specificity 88.2% against qPCR (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-023-12604-2; DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-55548-x).

These records show how widely the format has been adapted, but they say little about signal improvement, because no engineered comparison strip is run beside them. A strip is listed in this group when its abstract describes no deliberate change to the label, binder or readout.

## 3.1.8 What the evidence supports

For the question of this review, the studies give a consistent picture. The largest changes to the colour signal come from making the captured gold more visible, either after capture (enlargement, layer assembly, an amplifier complex) or through a better particle (clusters, dimers, shells, nanoflowers, nanostars), and these are the changes with the best-supported comparisons, because the same paper runs its own conventional strip (Table A4). The size of the change, within a paper, ranges from about 2-fold to about 1000-fold, and the high end belongs to designs with extra hardware or reagents (a magnet, an electrode set, an imager). Conjugation chemistry, binder replacement, antigen design and assay format address other limits (activity of the antibody, supply, variants, interpretation of the line); they matter for performance but their abstracts seldom state a change in signal against a baseline.

Three limitations apply across the section. First, many reported LODs come from scanners, imagers or readers rather than the naked eye, so they describe instrument-assisted performance, not equipment-free performance. Second, clinical evaluation is the exception among engineering papers: several tested only spiked buffer, saliva or recombinant protein, and the reference method varies (ELISA, RT-PCR, gel-read RT-PCR). Third, comparators differ (own conventional strip, same-size particles, commercial strip), so fold-changes cannot be ranked. Reporting quality of the papers read in full is in Table A3. Two animal-virus strips (DOI: 10.1039/d4an00180j; DOI: 10.1039/d2an02043b) are still known from abstracts only. Thirteen of the 93 papers have been read in full, in some cases in the main text only.
