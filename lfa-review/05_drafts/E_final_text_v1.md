# 3.5 Nucleic-acid amplification with a strip readout

## 3.5.0 Why amplify the nucleic acid, and how a strip reads it

Immunoassay strips detect proteins, and their sensitivity is limited by antibody affinity and label. Nucleic-acid tests work differently: a short amplification step makes millions of copies of a chosen sequence, and the strip only has to show that amplicons are present. The strip is read by labelling the amplicon on both ends, typically with biotin and a hapten such as FAM, so that a captured and labelled amplicon forms a coloured line, usually with gold particles or latex (DOI: 10.1186/s12916-025-04412-4). This design trades the simplicity of a protein strip for the sensitivity of molecular testing, at the cost of a heating step, a sample-preparation step and the risk of contamination by amplicons.

This section groups the included records by the amplification chemistry that precedes the strip: PCR and RT-PCR (3.5.1), loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) (3.5.2), recombinase polymerase and recombinase-aided amplification (RPA, RAA) (3.5.3), CRISPR-Cas detection, with and without a preamplification step (3.5.4), and other isothermal, amplification-free and unclassified nucleic-acid strips (3.5.5). Section 3.5.6 deals with integrated, one-pot and multiplex devices, and 3.5.7 states what the evidence supports. Many studies combine methods; each record is placed in the group of its main reaction and cross-referenced where relevant, and CRISPR studies that use RPA or LAMP stay in the CRISPR group. Numerical details are in Tables E1a-E1e. Papers read in full are marked † in the tables; all other statements rest on abstracts. The section covers 402 included records, 382 of them from 2015-2026.

## 3.5.1 PCR and RT-PCR with a strip readout

Strip readouts of PCR products predate the isothermal methods. Early studies combined multiplex PCR with a dipstick to detect hepatitis B, hepatitis C and HIV amplicons together, and PCR-ligase detection with a strip to detect drug-resistance mutations (DOI: 10.1128/jcm.43.8.4015-4021.2005; DOI: 10.1002/jmv.21811). Later PCR-strip assays addressed influenza, respiratory viruses, HPV, chikungunya virus, fish viruses and SARS-CoV-2 (DOI: 10.1128/jcm.43.8.4015-4021.2005; DOI: 10.1128/jcm.01487-09; DOI: 10.1002/jmv.21811; DOI: 10.1007/s10096-011-1300-4; DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2011.12.024; DOI: 10.1039/c2an16294f; DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2012.10.078; DOI: 10.1016/j.clinbiochem.2013.10.013; DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2014.06.006; DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2014.10.009; DOI: 10.1016/j.mcp.2015.03.005; DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2017.04.047; DOI: 10.1155/2018/7691014; DOI: 10.1007/s10096-018-03453-3; DOI: 10.1007/s13337-020-00577-z; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.0c03202; DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0241420; DOI: 10.1007/s00284-021-02621-7; DOI: 10.4014/jmb.2108.08025; DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2022.339514; DOI: 10.1007/s00604-022-05449-5; DOI: 10.1039/d2mh01267g; DOI: 10.1080/21691401.2023.2203198; DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2024.114890; DOI: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e32784; DOI: 10.1186/s12879-024-10296-1; DOI: 10.1016/j.diagmicrobio.2025.116679; DOI: 10.2147/mder.s508723; DOI: 10.1002/adhm.71600). ## 3.5.6 Integrated, one-pot and multiplex devices

The step that users find hardest is often sample preparation and transfer, not amplification. Recent designs therefore integrate nucleic-acid release, amplification and strip in one closed device, as in the monkeypox kit above, or perform amplification and Cas cleavage in the same tube. Multiplex designs use two or more test lines or two colours for dual targets such as dengue serotypes, SARS-CoV-2 with influenza, respiratory pathogens, or two avian viruses (Table E1b-E1e). Contamination by amplicons is a practical risk in open designs, and the monkeypox study gives it as the reason for its closed device.

## 3.5.7 What the evidence supports

Nucleic-acid strips report the lowest detection limits in this review, in several studies single-digit copy numbers per reaction and sensitivities equal to or above those of the reference PCR in the same paper. The costs are a heating step, sample preparation, amplicon contamination risk and, for CRISPR, an extra reaction step. Three cautions apply. First, "copies per reaction", "copies per microlitre" and "copies per millilitre" are not interchangeable, so LODs are not compared across studies. Second, reference PCRs differ in design, and positive agreement is sometimes counted over repeated tests of the same samples. Third, many abstracts do not report the sample matrix, number of samples or LOD definition (Table E2).

### Table E2. Strategy overview for nucleic-acid amplification with a strip readout

| Method | What it adds | Examples | Costs and limits seen | Table |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PCR, RT-PCR | established amplification | multiplex dipsticks for hepatitis, influenza, HPV, fish viruses | thermocycler | E1a |
| LAMP | single-temperature amplification | influenza, Ebola, Zika, HIV, animal viruses | complex primer sets; amplicon contamination | E1b |
| RPA, RAA | amplification at 37-42 C, often equipment-free | livestock, poultry, fish, plant and human viruses | amplicon contamination in open designs | E1c |
| CRISPR-Cas | sequence-specific detection, often after RPA or LAMP | African swine fever, SARS-CoV-2, Ebola, Lassa, HBV | extra reaction step; fluorescence often used alongside the strip | E1d |
| Other and amplification-free | alternative isothermal chemistries and probes | NASBA, CPA, rolling circle, XNAzyme, drug-resistance tests | fewer clinical data | E1e |
| Integrated or one-pot devices | fewer user steps | closed RPA-strip device, microfluidic, paper devices | few devices tested at scale | E1b-E1e |
