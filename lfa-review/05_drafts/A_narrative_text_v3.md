# 3.1 Gold nanoparticle labels: engineering the colorimetric signal

## 3.1.1 Where the colour of a strip comes from, and where it is lost

A colorimetric lateral flow immunoassay (LFIA) gives its answer as a coloured line. That line appears when the labelled immune complex is captured at the test line and the label absorbs enough visible light to stand out from the white nitrocellulose membrane. Its intensity is therefore set by three quantities: how many labelled particles reach the line and are held there, how much light each captured particle removes, and how clearly the result can be distinguished from the background by the eye or by a reader. Gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) became the standard label because they absorb visible light strongly, are simple to synthesise and conjugate readily to antibodies (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Their weakness follows from the same physics: when only a few particles are captured, a red line made of a few particles is too faint to see, and the limit of detection (LOD) of a gold strip is comparatively high (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229).

Each of the three quantities offers a lever, and the literature on gold labels reads as a sequence of attempts to pull them in turn. The first attempts work on the standard particle itself, its size and its surface, so that every particle that reaches the line is an active one (Section 3.1.2). When that is not enough, the captured gold can be made larger after the assay has run (Section 3.1.3). Rather than enlarging gold after capture, a third group designs particles that are optically stronger from the outset, by assembling, alloying or combining them with other materials (Section 3.1.4), and a fourth, much larger group shapes them into stars, cages and crowns, often to carry a second, instrumental signal as well (Section 3.1.5). The particle is only half of the probe, so the binder and the antigen it carries come next (Section 3.1.6), followed by the format of the assay (Section 3.1.7) and, in one study, the optics of the membrane itself (Section 3.1.8). Conventional colloidal gold strips that apply none of these changes form the baseline (Section 3.1.9), and Section 3.1.10 states what the evidence supports.

Three rules hold throughout. A change is treated as an improvement only when the same study runs a conventional or unmodified strip beside it, because fold-changes from different studies rest on different baselines, matrices and readouts and cannot be ranked. Where a label also provides a surface-enhanced Raman scattering (SERS), photothermal or fluorescence readout, only the colour channel is considered; the second-channel figures are listed in Tables A1g and A4 for reference. And the target virus is named for every method, with the numerical details of every study in Tables A1a to A1g.

## 3.1.2 The standard particle: size, surface and what it carries

The first lever is the most obvious one: a bigger particle carries more colour. It also migrates more slowly through the membrane and may not reach the test line in sufficient numbers, so size is a compromise rather than a quantity to maximise (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229; DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). Two studies located that compromise experimentally. For the hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), particles were grown stepwise from a seed to diameters between 34.2 and 137.8 nm; those of 42.7 nm gave the strongest test line at 10, 100 and 500 ng/mL of antigen and, in human whole blood spiked with 500 ng/mL HBsAg, a clearer line than particles made by the Turkevich-Frens method or a commercial HBV kit, although no LOD value is reported (DOI: 10.3390/s16122154). For dengue virus NS1, 40 nm colloidal gold, 80 nm gold nanospheres and 150 nm gold nanoshells were compared with the same antibody: the 80 nm particles gave the most distinct and consistent lines, while the 150 nm shells produced false-positive signals in every buffer tested (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics16030395). Both point to an optimum between 40 and 80 nm, not to the largest particle that can be made.

A particle of the right size is still wasted if the antibody on it cannot bind. Random adsorption leaves part of the antibodies with their binding sites turned towards the gold, and oriented coupling corrects this. On 100 nm AuNPs for HBsAg, a thiolated hydrazide ligand attached the antibody through its Fc region so that the antigen-binding sites faced outward. The same performance then required about seven times less antibody (0.2 against 1.5 mg per pmol of particles), conjugation took 10 minutes instead of 90, and the LOD fell from 5 to 2 ng/mL relative to carbodiimide coupling in the same study; with a portable strip reader, 35 positive and 15 negative sera agreed with a chemiluminescence immunoassay (R2 = 0.9527) without false positives (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2021.338623).

The surface that holds the antibody also meets the sample, and here the gold can work for or against the assay. In a colloidal gold strip for SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG, plasma anticoagulated with EDTA-K2 gave stronger lines and more IgM-positive results than serum from the same 112 patients: 17 weak and 15 clear IgM positives in plasma were negative in serum, and 40 IgG samples that were merely positive in serum read as strongly positive in plasma. The authors attribute this to chelation and aggregation of the colloidal gold by EDTA-K2, an effect that sodium citrate and lithium heparin did not produce; no false positives arose in 200 healthy controls, and EDTA-K2 plasma agreed best with the chemiluminescence reference (DOI: 10.2147/ijn.s281594). The protein layer that adsorbs onto a probe in biological fluids, the protein corona, has an equally strong influence, and three studies traced it in NS1 assays. For Zika virus NS1 on gold nanostars, strips run in buffer or bovine serum albumin showed false-positive lines that disappeared in human serum or on serum-pretreated nitrocellulose, and a preformed corona restored the specificity of antibody-antigen binding (LOD 1.6 nM, effective dissociation constant 12.7 nM in serum) (DOI: 10.1021/acs.bioconjchem.6b00523). For dengue virus serotype 2 NS1, the identity of the corona protein decided the outcome: a preformed apolipoprotein A1 corona gave an LOD of 1.24 nM, whereas an albumin corona lowered the test-line intensity by 33% at a given antigen concentration, so that no meaningful LOD could be determined (DOI: 10.3390/pharmaceutics14112439). A systematic comparison of probe chemistries, with mouse IgG, Zika NS1 and dengue NS1 as model pairs in albumin and serum, then showed why: physisorbed, PEG-backfilled probes aggregated and lost signal as antigen rose, covalently grafted probes (NHS, hydrazide) stayed stable although the NHS probes performed worst in serum, and sonication or a single pre-wash restored the signal (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.0c08628). With an earlier comparison of conjugation strategies for dengue NS1 (DOI: 10.1080/15321819.2016.1269338), these mechanistic studies explain why nominally identical strips behave differently in different matrices, even though they report few diagnostic data (Table A1a).

Once size and surface are controlled, the particle itself can be varied. Hollow gold nanoparticles have a molar extinction coefficient (1.48 x 10^10 M^-1 cm^-1) that the authors report to be much larger than that of solid particles of the same size; as a label for HIV-1 p24 antigen they gave darker blue lines and higher optical density in serum than the commercial Alere HIV-1/2 Ag/Ab Combo test (LOD 0.02 ng/mL), a sensitivity about twice as high, and detected one of six preclinical samples that the commercial strip missed (DOI: 10.1039/d4ra05277c). The method of synthesis, by contrast, did not matter: laser-ablated gold, made in water without chemical reductants, detected 10 ng of SARS-CoV-2 spike antigen on a dot-format dipstick, exactly as commercial gold conjugated with the same antibody did (DOI: 10.5599/admet.2079). Gold magnetic nanoparticles in a TORCH IgM strip (rubella virus, cytomegalovirus and herpes simplex virus) gave 100% sensitivity and specificity in 162 sera and are reported to be more sensitive than commercial colloidal gold strips, without a stated value (DOI: 10.3892/ijmm.2015.2333), and gold-oligonucleotide nanoconstructs designed for conserved enterovirus sequences detected 96% of known enteroviruses (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070238).

The molecule on the gold need not be an antibody at all, and some of the most inventive probes recognise what a virus does rather than what it looks like. An oseltamivir derivative immobilised on gold binds the neuraminidase of oseltamivir-resistant (H275Y) influenza virus much more strongly than that of the wild type; the particles turn from deep red to purple in the presence of the resistant enzyme, and a strip built on them detected the resistant virus at 5 x 10^2 to 10^3 PFU per test (1 x 10^4 PFU/mL), also in mixtures with wild-type virus (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-018-31311-x). For dengue-1 RNA, dextrin-capped gold carried a DNA reporter probe in a sandwich hybridisation strip; the visual cut-off, the lowest concentration giving a visible red band, was 0.01 uM of synthetic target, and viral RNA from pooled human sera, amplified by nucleic acid sequence-based amplification (NASBA), was detected at 1.2 x 10^4 PFU/mL in 20 minutes, a figure that depends on the amplification step (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics9030074). Bacterial protein A and protein G served as flexible detectors for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies in 105 sera (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-022-03939-2), and a recombinant streptavidin fusion protein served as the reporter in a hepatitis C virus antibody strip (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.22701).

All of these changes improve the particle that is captured. The boldest change at this level instead increases how many particles each captured target brings with it. The RPA70A domain of replication protein A, a single-stranded DNA-binding protein, was conjugated to AuNPs so that several particles decorate the single-stranded region of one aptamer. For the influenza A nucleoprotein the line was visible to the naked eye at 0.01 ng/mL in buffer, the LOD in tenfold-diluted human nasal fluid was 0.26 pg/mL, and a conventional two-step design with streptavidin-conjugated AuNPs and a biotinylated aptamer, run in the same study, was visible only at 10 ng/mL, a thousandfold difference (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.9b01827).

The limit addressed at this level is therefore the number of active particles at the line. Where a like-for-like comparison exists, the gains are the thousandfold of the multi-particle aptamer design, the 2.5-fold of oriented conjugation and the twofold of hollow gold against a commercial strip; for most other surface modifications the studies explain behaviour rather than quantify gain. A well-made particle still meets a ceiling, and the next group of studies goes past it by enlarging the gold after it has been captured.

## 3.1.3 Making more of the captured gold

Once the immune complex has formed, every captured particle is a potential seed. Depositing more metal on it enlarges it or roughens it, and a larger particle absorbs more light per captured event (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229). The most careful comparison of the available chemistries placed silver enhancement, a gold-after-silver step based on galvanic replacement, and direct gold enhancement on one strip format for the SARS-CoV-2 receptor-binding domain, with the same antibodies and the same AuNPs. Gold enhancement won, lowering the LOD by more than two orders of magnitude below the unenhanced strip with stable reagents, but it introduced nonspecific colouring and needed an optical instrument to reach the lowest value (DOI: 10.3390/bios11070229).

The rest of this subsection is, in effect, a series of attempts to keep that gain while removing its cost. Growing 20 nm particles into much larger ones after the assay turned most of the false negatives of a conventional strip for potato virus X into positives in potato leaf extracts, but as a separate post-assay step (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-018-3052-7). For the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, the enhancement reagents (Au3+ and hydroxylamine) were dried onto an additional pad between the sample and conjugate pads, so that enlargement happens during the normal run with no extra operation. The naked-eye limit fell from 5.0 to 0.5 ng/mL relative to the same strip without the pad, the calibration-curve LOD was 0.10 ng/mL, and in 207 clinical saliva samples the strip reached a sensitivity of 96.10% and a specificity of 99.23% against RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2023.133898). The same idea appears with gold nanoflowers plus gold staining for the nucleocapsid protein in buffer and artificial saliva (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-024-06502-1), and, for two sugarcane mosaic-associated viruses, with a positively charged cysteamine-gold label followed by nanogold enhancement, which extended detection from a linear range of 10^-6 to 10^-9 g/mL down to 10^-12 g/mL (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-022-07950-6).

Enlarging each particle is one way to add colour at the line; adding more particles is another. Layer-by-layer deposition of AuNPs through biotin-streptavidin binding, driven by electrophoresis, lowered the LOD of an HBsAg strip more than 60-fold. Its designers accepted the extra hardware and limited its use: the conventional strip is read first, and enhancement is applied only to samples that look negative or weakly positive. In 118 clinical samples this raised diagnostic sensitivity against a laboratory chemiluminescence assay without loss of specificity, at the cost of a small power supply, a holder and an additional reagent step (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202215548). In SARS-CoV-2 serology, a complex of antibody-gold and antigen-gold conjugates achieved a similar effect chemically, increasing the test-line signal over antigen-gold alone and bringing the visual limit to the cut-off of the reference ELISA (DOI: 10.1002/gch2.202200008).

The gains at this level are consistently large, from tenfold for the single-step pad to more than two orders of magnitude for gold enhancement, and they are achieved with the same antibodies and particles as the baseline strip. Their price is reagents, time or background and, for the lowest values, an instrument (Table A1b). This is what motivates the alternative: rather than enlarging gold after capture, design a particle that is brighter from the start.

## 3.1.4 Brighter by design: clusters, dimers, shells and composites

The optical response of gold depends on the size, shape and composition of a particle and on the distance to its neighbours. Each of these parameters has been turned into a design strategy, and the studies are easiest to follow as variations on one question: how close should gold sit to other gold?

Clusters answer it by keeping particles apart. Placing several particles at each binding event multiplies the colour, but particles closer than about 10 nm couple plasmonically, their absorption shifts towards the near-infrared, and the visible colour fades. Clusters built around a streptavidin core, with biotinylated antibodies holding the particles about 15 nm apart, avoided this coupling and kept the red colour; for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein a cluster of 40 nm particles outperformed single particles, while a mixed cluster of 40 and 15 nm particles did not (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114094). Gold dendrimer nanochains, which show a red-to-blue shift, have been tested for HIV-1 p24 (DOI: 10.1039/c7tb01394a). Core-satellite particles, in which a silica core carries many small gold satellites, lowered the LOD by two orders of magnitude relative to 40 nm colloidal gold in the same assay for recombinant SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein and inactivated virus, although the visual limit remained much higher than the calculated LOD (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.2c13303).

Dimers answer the same question the opposite way, by bringing gold as close as possible. Two particles joined with a sub-nanometre gap couple strongly, show two plasmon peaks and give a stronger signal than single spheres. For porcine epidemic diarrhea virus such dimers gave a clearly lower naked-eye LOD than a commercial strip and full agreement with RT-PCR on faecal samples, though the authors note that dimers are hard to prepare at scale and that their long-term stability is unknown (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06013-5).

Hollow alloy shells change composition instead of spacing. Silver-rich Ag-Au nanoshells show two plasmon peaks and a larger integrated absorbance than AuNPs of the same size, and in a bovine viral diarrhea virus strip they gave a lower visual LOD than the authors' own AuNP strip with the same antibodies, with good agreement with a commercial ELISA on field samples (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-026-08241-x).

Composites, finally, add a second material that brings a function gold lacks, most often magnetism. In an avian influenza H7 strip, Au/Fe3O4 core-shell particles gave a naked-eye limit of 10^3.5 EID50, ten times lower than gold particles carrying the same antibody in the same study, while Fe3O4 alone did not produce a valid strip; 200 clinical samples were tested, of which 7 were positive (DOI: 10.1039/c8ra00185e). Magnetic-gold nanostars for the Rift Valley fever virus nucleoprotein combined a spiked plasmonic shell with magnetic pre-concentration and gave a very large gain over the authors' own gold-sphere strip in buffer and spiked serum, at the cost of a magnet and a pre-incubation step and without testing on patient samples (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.5c21544). Composite plasmonic particles imaged at two wavelengths for the HIV capsid protein gave a large gain that depends on an imager rather than on the eye (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114133).

The strongest evidence at this level comes from studies that compare the new particle with a conventional strip made with the same antibodies, as for the clusters, the shells and the Au/Fe3O4 composite. Comparisons with commercial strips, as for the dimers, mix the effect of the label with that of the antibodies, and some particles have been tested only in buffer (Table A1c). The next group of studies takes shape and composition much further, and in doing so adds a second, instrumental signal to the colour.

## 3.1.5 Shaped gold and the second signal: what the colour gains

A large and recent body of work builds structured gold labels that provide two signals at once: the colour of the line, read by eye, and a SERS, photothermal or fluorescence signal, read with an instrument. The headline sensitivity of these papers almost always belongs to the instrumental channel. For the purpose of this review they are valuable for another reason: most of them report the naked-eye limit of the same strip, and many run a conventional gold strip beside it, so together they form the largest controlled experiment available on which structural changes to gold improve its colour. The second-channel figures are in Table A1g and are not repeated here.

The answer they give is clear: shape matters most. A gold nanocrown, a gold shell decorated with external nanospheres, gave an intense dark-blue line for the SARS-CoV-2 spike S1 protein. Against gold nanospheres in the same study the line was visible by eye at 0.1 ng/mL instead of 10 ng/mL, and the colorimetric LOD was 91.24 pg/mL against 5.9 ng/mL, about 65-fold by calculation (the paper states 100-fold); the antigen was spiked into buffer and saliva (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04361). Gold nanocages showed that geometry can be tuned systematically: the size and core-to-shell gap were varied, the largest cage was the brightest, and its colorimetric LOD for the influenza A (H1N1) nucleoprotein was 1.8 ng/mL against 12.5 ng/mL for gold spheres, a sevenfold gain, with 1.7 ng/mL in spiked saliva (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.4c05384). One-pot Au@Pt nanostars detected antibodies to the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein by eye at 1 ng/mL, about 100 times lower than colloidal gold strips run in the same study, and kept 93% of their signal after eight weeks; in sera from 83 vaccinated and 11 unvaccinated volunteers, however, the positive counts are reported for the photothermal channel (56 positives, against 32 for the colloidal gold strips), not for colour alone (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2024.342241). The sub-nanometre-gap dimers of Section 3.1.4 reappear here as gold nanoantennas carrying a Raman reporter: for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus spiked into negative faeces (recoveries 94.9% to 102.1%, n = 6) the naked-eye LOD was 1.0 x 10^2 TCID50/mL, about 59-fold below the 5.9 x 10^3 TCID50/mL that the paper quotes for traditional colloidal gold strips, a baseline cited rather than measured, and no clinical samples were tested (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2025.117699). Other shapes extend the series without a stated baseline: gold nanorods with a tunable absorption peak detected the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein by eye at 2 ng/mL, with 87.5% agreement with nucleic acid testing in 40 nasal swabs (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay00347k); Ag-coated gold nanostars detected influenza A antibody at 0.1 ng/mL by colour (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2024.127351); Ag@Au nanoplates gave a very low background for the nucleocapsid protein in 35 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c05787); and gold-silver alloy hollow nanoshells, used in a competitive format in which the blue line fades as SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibody rises, gave a naked-eye limit of 160 ng/mL, with a neutralising effect, among 107 sera, in 79 of 98 vaccinated and none of 9 unvaccinated volunteers, though no neutralisation assay was run on the same sera (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2023.341102).

Alloying is the second structural route, and it gives smaller gains. Silver absorbs more strongly than gold, and a thin gold shell keeps a silver core stable and easy to conjugate. Ag(MBA)@Au particles with a gold shell about 2 nm thick gave a SARS-CoV-2 IgG line visible by eye at 10^-7 mg/mL in buffer and 10^-6 mg/mL in serum, and in 98 vaccinated volunteers the clear colour band identified 26 strong positives, against 11 positives for commercial colloidal gold strips on the same sera and 34 for ELISA (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c01286). The same particles assembled on Fe3O4 reached a visual limit of 10^-8 mg/mL for antibodies to the nucleocapsid protein and found 49 strong positives among 98 vaccinated volunteers, without a gold-strip comparator (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-022-04437-1). Au@Ag particles gave a colorimetric LOD of 300 pg/mL for the nucleocapsid protein, with 100% negative and 85% positive accuracy in 20 patients and 20 healthy subjects (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2025.344149), and Au@MBA@Ag particles gave visual LODs of 6.25 x 10^2 TCID50/mL for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus and 7.42 x 10^2 copies/uL for porcine rotavirus A in faeces, with 93.3% agreement with RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.saa.2025.125863). Where a baseline was run, the gain was modest: integrating polydopamine into Au@Ag doubled the colour sensitivity relative to plain Au@Ag tags and gave visual limits of 3.3 x 10^5 copies/mL for inactivated H1N1 and 1.23 x 10^5 copies/mL for SARS-CoV-2, five- and twofold better than commercial gold strips, with most positives among 32 nasopharyngeal swabs visible as a colour band and four weak positives seen only by SERS (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07587-y).

The third route returns to the composites of Section 3.1.4, and here the most effective addition turns out to act on the number of particles rather than on the particle. High-density gold nanoshells on Fe3O4 nanoclusters allowed the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein to be enriched magnetically before the strip, and the naked-eye limit fell from 100 ng/mL without enrichment to 1 ng/mL with it, against 40 ng/mL for a self-made colloidal gold strip; spiked artificial saliva and 18 real samples were tested (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2023.115688). A Fe3O4/Au/quantum-dot probe carrying wheat germ agglutinin, a lectin that binds viral membrane glycoproteins broadly, gave a visual sensitivity of 0.1 to 0.5 ng/mL for SARS-CoV-2 and mpox virus in 33 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/smll.202406053). Fe3O4-Au heterodimers gave better coloration than ordinary AuNPs in a three-line nucleic acid strip for influenza H1N1, H3N2 and H9N2 (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay01010h); Janus Au(shell)-Fe3O4 particles gave naked-eye LODs of 50 ng/mL for both H3N2 influenza and SARS-CoV-2 in a multiplex strip (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2024.117030); and Fe3O4-Au(shell) heterodimers for mpox virus gave a 5 ng/mL naked-eye threshold (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2025.128670). Non-magnetic supports work through surface area: 30 nm AuNPs layered on MoS2 nanosheets gave a visual limit of 0.2 ng/mL for the mpox virus A29L antigen against 1 ng/mL for a standard AuNP strip, a fivefold colour gain, in spiked throat swabs and lake water (DOI: 10.1016/j.jhazmat.2023.132136); rhodamine B-loaded UiO-66 with AuNPs on its surface gave a colorimetric LOD of 31.2 pg/mL for potato virus S against 462.4 pg/mL for a conventional AuNP strip, about 15-fold (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2026.1876426); dual-signal nanospheres (RNs@Au) detected Ebola virus glycoprotein by eye at 2 ng/mL in 20 minutes (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.7b02222); a PDA@Au core-shell probe for influenza A (H1N1) gave a visual sensitivity of 93.9% and specificity of 95.8% in 90 throat swabs (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07611-1); and Tris-stabilised AuNPs allowed simultaneous visual detection of porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (1.25 x 10^3 TCID50/mL) and porcine rotavirus (3.13 x 10^2 pg/mL), with 95% and 100% concordance with RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.saa.2024.124670). Silica cores carrying gold and quantum dots show that a second signal need not help the colour at all: for SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG the colorimetric performance was stated to equal that of a traditional gold strip, with bands visible at a 1:10^4 serum dilution and hard to see in 8 early-stage sera (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.0c03484); for the SARS-CoV-2 S and N proteins the colorimetric limit of 50 pg/mL was five times better than a standard AuNP strip (DOI: 10.1021/acsami.2c21042); and for the mpox virus A29L antigen the colorimetric limit of 0.5 ng/mL equalled that of the conventional AuNP strip (DOI: 10.1186/s12951-023-02215-4).

The final group makes the same point more starkly. Eleven studies attach a Raman reporter or a lanthanide to gold without changing its shape, and in all of them the colour channel behaves like an ordinary gold strip. Gold nanostars with a Raman reporter gave a faintly visible line at 1.02 x 10^3 copies/uL of group A rotavirus, with 95% agreement with RT-PCR in 80 faecal samples from pigs, cattle and poultry (DOI: 10.1016/j.saa.2026.127441). Au/DTNB@Ag/DTNB tags gave a naked-eye limit of 80 pg/mL for human rotavirus and, in 95 clinical faecal samples, agreed completely with both RT-qPCR and a commercial colloidal gold strip, so the colour offered nothing the commercial product did not (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-020-04670-4); the same tags reached 10 ng/mL for West Nile virus NS1 protein (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-021-04857-3). For the SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain, a Raman-tagged strip read as a colour strip gave a scanner-quantified limit of 1.2 ng/mL (visible above 1 ng/mL), against 0.4 ng/mL for an ELISA with the same immunoreagents (DOI: 10.3390/bios11120510). For SARS-CoV-2 IgM and IgG in patient serum, the line on SERS-active gold nanostars was visible at a 1:100 serum dilution, barely at 1:200 and not at 1:1000, and IgG lines in sera taken 3.5 and 4 months after infection stayed below the visual threshold (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.1c02305). Gold-nanobridged nanogap particles for the nucleocapsid protein gave a visible line at 0.1 ng/reaction and, placed into a commercial rapid antigen kit, left its visual limit unchanged at 0.5 ng/reaction (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07179-w). A digital SERS strip for the same antigen is the one case in which the colour of the new strip was worse than commercial strips: only the highest concentration tested (0.1 ug/mL) was visible by eye, whereas three commercial colorimetric tests reached 1 to 10 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1002/smsc.202400259). Magnetic Fe3O4@Au nanotags carrying wheat germ agglutinin captured influenza A virus before the strip, with 100% SERS accuracy in 110 throat swab or sputum samples against 81% for a commercial colloidal gold strip, but the visual limit of the strip itself is not stated (DOI: 10.1016/j.nano.2025.102853). Two nucleic acid strips without amplification gave visible lines at 80 pg/mL of HIV-1 DNA, the same limit as a commercial fluorescence kit run in the same study (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2015.11.099), and at about 20 pM of Kaposi's sarcoma-associated herpesvirus DNA on a two-line strip whose second line detected a bacterial marker (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.6b03536). The lanthanide composite, cysteamine-modified gold-europium nanoparticles for H5N1 avian influenza spiked into swab specimens, gave a naked-eye limit of 10 HAU/mL against 20 HAU/mL for commercial gold nanoparticles in the same study, a twofold gain (DOI: 10.3390/ijms23147957).

Read together, the colour channels of these dual-signal labels rank the structural strategies. Where the same study runs a gold baseline, shaped gold gives the largest gains (nanostars about 100-fold, nanocrowns about 65-fold, nanocages sevenfold); magnetic enrichment adds 100-fold, but through the number of particles delivered rather than through the particle; and silver-gold, polydopamine, MoS2, metal-organic framework and lanthanide composites give between twofold and 15-fold. Several designs give no colour gain, and none of the Raman-tagged strips gives one. In all 22 studies of this group that were read in full, the figure quoted in the abstract belongs to the instrumental channel. The cost is a multi-step synthesis and, for the sensitivity that the abstracts advertise, an instrument. However bright the particle, it captures nothing without a binder, and that is where the next group of studies turns.

## 3.1.6 The binder and the antigen

A brighter label cannot compensate for a weak binder, and a parallel line of work replaces the conventional antibody with a recognition element that is smaller, more stable, easier to produce or better suited to a changing target. The alternatives form a progression from engineered antibodies to fully synthetic polymers.

Nanobodies, the single-domain fragments of camelid antibodies, stand closest to the antibody and are the best documented. In a SARS-CoV-2 spike S1 strip, a nanobody-gold detector with an ACE2-gold capture probe reached 0.4 x 10^4 copies/mL of gamma-irradiated virus against 1.6 x 10^4 copies/mL for a monoclonal-antibody version of the same strip, a fourfold gain, and in 320 saliva samples with 180 RT-PCR positives it reached a sensitivity of 97.14% and a specificity of 98.57% for samples with Ct of 30 or below, against 90.04% and 97.86% for the antibody strip (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-37347-y). It is the one binder study in this section with a like-for-like comparison. Multivalent and Y-shaped nanobody assemblies have been built for the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid and spike S1 proteins and for the chikungunya virus E protein, and a nanobody strip has been applied to human norovirus outbreak specimens (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.4c00509; DOI: 10.1039/d4nr02762k; DOI: 10.1039/d5nr02568k; DOI: 10.3390/bios16020132; DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2025.1707358; DOI: 10.1128/msphere.00219-16). Nanobodies can also be displayed in many copies on a carrier: yeast cell-wall nanofragments presenting anti-spike nanobodies, linked to AuNPs through the SpyTag/SpyCatcher system, gave a red line for SARS-CoV-2 virions that weakened with concentration and failed below 2 x 10^4 virions/mL, although only one positive and one negative clinical sample were run on the strip (DOI: 10.1038/s41565-023-01415-1).

Aptamers move one step further from biology: they are synthesised chemically and can be produced more reproducibly and cheaply than antibodies (DOI: 10.3390/molecules29081776). The same study shows their current limit, however: a competitive aptamer strip for SARS-CoV-2 spike protein on food packaging used an aptamer selected for broad binding to variant spikes, but its sensitivity was below that of other methods and it was tested only with recombinant protein (DOI: 10.3390/molecules29081776). Other aptamer strips for SARS-CoV-2 spike, inactivated virus and nucleocapsid protein were evaluated with inactivated virus or spiked samples (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202107730; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c00554; DOI: 10.1186/s12951-023-02191-9); a pair of sandwich aptamers detected whole avian influenza H5N2 particles with better specificity over other H5Nx viruses (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2019.03.061); an aptamer-based isolation step preceded a strip for grouper nervous necrosis virus (5 ng/mL coat protein) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2020.00886); and a gold-and-aptamer proof of concept was proposed for home detection of SARS-CoV-2 mutants (DOI: 10.1016/j.nano.2022.102590). Two further papers select aptamers for H3N2 and for the Omicron receptor-binding domain without reporting strip data (DOI: 10.1166/jbn.2019.2789; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c01993).

At the end of the progression lie fully synthetic binders. A hydrogel copolymer of aromatic amino-acid monomers, screened for affinity to the SARS-CoV-2 spike trimer and then polymerised in situ on AuNPs, acted as an abiotic antibody mimic: the core-shell probe gave a visual limit of 30 ng/reaction of spike trimer in a direct format and detected pseudovirus at 1 x 10^5 TU/mL in saliva in a sandwich format, without an antibody comparator (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2025.129324). Sialic-acid glyconanoparticles that recognise the spike through its glycan-binding activity, a lectin for severe dengue virus infection and a miniprotein binder aimed at SARS-CoV-2 variants of concern complete the range, without accuracy data (DOI: 10.1021/acscentsci.0c00855; DOI: 10.1080/15321819.2023.2260480; DOI: 10.1007/s00604-023-06113-2).

The conventional antibody is not standing still either: choosing the right pair and the right antigen can matter as much as replacing it. For the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein, two selected antibody pairs reached 8 pg of protein and 625 TCID50/mL of virus in 60 clinical samples (DOI: 10.3390/ijms222212412), a detection antibody directed at a linear epitope reached 10 pg/mL (DOI: 10.3390/ijms25084436), and one study compared the nucleocapsid and the spike as targets (DOI: 10.1016/j.molimm.2021.01.005). In serology strips the antigen is the variable that can be engineered: an acid-treated p72 antigen detected African swine fever virus antibodies at a 1:10,000 serum dilution (DOI: 10.3389/fchem.2021.804981); the protrusion domain of the infectious bursal disease virus VP2 protein served as a highly immunogenic antigen (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-020-10458-6); plant-produced receptor-binding domain gave a sensitivity of 94.1% and a specificity of 98% in 51 sera (DOI: 10.3877/cma.j.issn.2096-112x.2021.01.006); baculovirus-expressed nucleocapsid and receptor-binding domain were tested in 103 convalescent sera (DOI: 10.1007/s10529-022-03316-0); and the shell domain of norovirus was chosen for a broad-range strip tested on 122 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1186/s12866-020-02084-z).

Binder replacement promises independence from animal immunisation and resilience to sequence variation. So far the evidence is largely analytical: apart from the nanobody strip with its fourfold gain, these studies report a detection limit or a specificity statement rather than a comparison with an antibody version of the same strip (Table A1d). With the probe complete, what remains to decide is what its colour on the line actually means.

## 3.1.7 Assay format: what the line can mean

The format of the immunoassay determines how the colour relates to the amount of target, and the same particle can say different things in different formats. In a sandwich strip the line darkens with the target until excess antigen saturates both antibodies and the line weakens again, the hook effect; a lumpy skin disease virus strip that examined this effect reported a detection limit of 10^3.4 TCID50/mL, a coefficient of variation below 5.3% and no cross-reaction with 12 other viruses (DOI: 10.3390/bios12090739). In a competitive strip the logic is inverted, the line fading as the target rises, which suits functional assays: a competitive gold strip for SARS-CoV-2 neutralising antibodies reached 20.66 IU/L with 100% specificity (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.2c03677), and a biomimetic mock-virus particle displaying trimeric spike measured neutralising antibody at 59 IU/mL against the WHO standard (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c04372). A quantitative colloidal gold assay can even turn the strip into a measuring instrument for other tests: one measured the detection limits of nine approved SARS-CoV-2 antigen kits and found nucleic acid testing at least 1835 times more sensitive than antigen testing for a Delta strain (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2023.124462). Reading can also be extended without changing the format: a Japanese encephalitis virus NS1 strip read with a smartphone colorimeter detected 10 pg/mL by eye in spiked serum and was stable for a month at 4 C (DOI: 10.1039/d2na00463a). The limit addressed at this level is interpretation, and the cost is that each format has its own dynamic range, which most reports do not state (Table A1f).

## 3.1.8 Changing the optics: scattering instead of absorption

Every strategy described so far adds absorbed colour to a white membrane. One study asked whether the membrane itself is the obstacle. When nitrocellulose is made transparent by refractive-index matching and placed over a black backing card, large AuNPs appear as orange lines by scattering light rather than absorbing it, and the background almost disappears. For the influenza A virus nucleoprotein the authors report a very large gain over their own conventional strip and over commercial strips, higher sensitivity in a small set of clinical swabs, and reading by eye or smartphone; the approach needs an additional reagent step and enough light, and the authors themselves place it beyond absorbance-based colorimetry (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58663-z). It is therefore treated here as a boundary case, with its figures kept apart from those of absorption-based strips (Table A1c). It also explains, in reverse, why large particles fail on an ordinary membrane: what they add in scattering is invisible against white.

## 3.1.9 Conventional gold strips: the baseline

All the engineering above is measured against the strip it set out to improve, and many of the included studies describe exactly that strip: a new colloidal gold assay that applies none of the modifications discussed. These studies define what the unmodified format achieves across viruses. Because they rarely run a modified strip beside them, they report no within-study change, and their values are listed in Table A1e.

In SARS-CoV-2 serology, colloidal gold IgM and IgG strips reached a sensitivity of 100% and a specificity of 93.3% for IgM (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.0c01554), a sensitivity of 90% and a specificity of 96.6% against real-time PCR in 60 samples (DOI: 10.1089/mab.2021.0027), 158 of 170 positives with an area under the curve of 0.958 (DOI: 10.1002/jcla.23619) and an IgM sensitivity of 90.67% (DOI: 10.1002/ctm2.90), with further strips reported without values (DOI: 10.1039/d0an00629g; DOI: 10.1097/cm9.0000000000000922); a nasal secretory IgA strip detected spike-specific IgA from people infected with the BA.5 variant (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2024.1386891). For SARS-CoV-2 antigen, a spike strip detected 62.5 ng/mL in a subunit vaccine (DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2021.635677), a nucleocapsid kit detected above 0.2 ng/mL of recombinant protein without cross-reaction (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2021.114183), a receptor-binding-domain strip detected 1 ng/mL with a sensitivity of 94.3% and a specificity of 90.9% against RT-PCR in 100 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.28416), a nanogold antigen test reached a sensitivity of 95% and a specificity of 98% (DOI: 10.3390/nano12142477), another detected 2 ng/mL with 98% concordance (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07299-3), and the CoV-SCAN strip detected ten variants at 1.6 to 12.5 TCID50 per swab (DOI: 10.1016/j.jcvp.2022.100080). A nucleic acid sandwich strip for the N3 gene reached 5 pM (DOI: 10.1016/j.jbiosc.2022.11.001).

Beyond SARS-CoV-2, the same format covers a wide range of human viruses: an HIV-1 p24 strip with in-house monoclonal antibodies reached 25 pg/mL and a specificity of 98.03% (DOI: 10.1155/2016/6743904); a strip for severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus reached 1 ng/mL (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.8b02366); a competitive dengue strip with the 4G2 antibody reached 5.12 x 10^2 PFU (DOI: 10.3390/bios12070495); a varicella-zoster strip reached 30 ng/mL of glycoprotein E without cross-reaction with EV71, HSV-1 or HSV-2 (DOI: 10.1016/j.virol.2023.07.008); a strip for the mpox virus A29 protein detected 50 pg/mL of purified antigen without cross-reaction with vaccinia or cowpox virus (DOI: 10.1039/d2an02043b); and an HTLV-I IgG strip reached a sensitivity of 96.7% and a specificity of 100% (DOI: 10.3390/v15010129). It extends equally to animal and aquatic viruses: feline coronavirus antibodies in plasma, serum, whole blood and ascitic fluid (DOI: 10.1007/978-1-4939-2438-7_3), nervous necrosis virus virions in fish tissue (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-020-63553-z), porcine circovirus 2 at 10^3.18 TCID50/mL (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.01953-22), pseudorabies virus at 1.336 x 10^3 copies/uL (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2024.1399123), avian leukosis virus p27 protein at 1.53 ng/mL in 35 egg samples, against 0.2 ng/mL for ELISA (DOI: 10.1039/d4an00180j), and canine parvovirus in faeces, with a sensitivity of 71% and a specificity of 92% against haemagglutination in one study (DOI: 10.1007/s00705-018-3846-2), a colloidal gold and fluorescent format in 60 clinical samples in another (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-023-12604-2), and a sensitivity of 96.4% and a specificity of 88.2% against qPCR in a third (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-55548-x). The breadth of this list is itself a finding: the unmodified gold strip already serves almost every virus family for which a good antibody exists, and the engineering of the preceding sections is aimed at its sensitivity, not at its reach.

## 3.1.10 What the evidence supports

Read as a whole, the studies of this section give a consistent account of how the colour of a gold strip is strengthened, and it follows the three quantities set out at the start. The number of particles at the line can be raised by attaching several particles to each target or by enriching the target magnetically before the strip, with gains of 100- to 1000-fold that require an extra step or extra hardware. The colour of each captured particle can be raised after capture, by metal enhancement or layer-by-layer assembly, with gains of tenfold to more than two orders of magnitude achieved with the same antibodies and particles as the baseline, at the price of reagents, time or an instrument; or it can be raised before capture, through clusters, shells and above all shaped gold, with gains of about five- to 100-fold in the colour channel. The best-supported comparisons are those in which the same study runs its own conventional strip (Table A4); across them the reported gain ranges from about twofold to about 1000-fold, and the high end belongs invariably to designs that add a magnet, an electrode set, an imager or a pre-incubation.

The dual-signal labels of Section 3.1.5 deserve a separate conclusion. Their headline sensitivity comes from SERS, photothermal or fluorescence readout, and the gain in colour alone is far smaller than their titles suggest: none to about 100-fold, and none at all for the Raman-tagged strips. For the question of this review their value lies elsewhere, in showing that shaping gold improves its colour more than alloying it or combining it with other materials.

Conjugation chemistry, binder replacement, antigen design and assay format address different limits: the activity of the recognition element, its supply, sequence variation and the interpretation of the line. Their contribution to the colour signal is rarely quantified against a baseline.

Three limitations run across the section. Many reported LODs come from scanners, imagers or readers rather than from the naked eye and therefore describe instrument-assisted performance. Clinical evaluation is the exception among engineering studies, several of which were tested only in spiked buffer, saliva or recombinant protein, with reference methods ranging from ELISA to RT-PCR. And the comparators differ, from the authors' own conventional strip through particles of the same size to commercial strips and quoted literature values, so fold-changes cannot be ranked across studies.
