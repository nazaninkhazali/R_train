# 3.5 Nucleic-acid amplification with a strip readout: a stronger signal from a pre-step

## 3.5.0 What limits the signal, and where the improvement comes from

In an immunoassay strip, the colour at the test line is limited by antibody affinity and by how many labels are captured. In the nucleic-acid strips of this section the strip itself is usually an ordinary gold or latex line. What changes is the number of targets reaching it: an amplification step makes many copies of a chosen sequence, and the strip only has to show that amplicons are present. The strip is usually read by labelling the amplicon at both ends, with biotin and a hapten such as FAM, so that a captured amplicon forms a coloured line with gold particles or latex (DOI: 10.1186/s12916-025-04412-4). The improvement in the colorimetric result therefore comes from a pre-step, and it is paid for with a heating step, a sample-preparation step and the risk of amplicon contamination.

The section follows the chemistry that precedes the strip: PCR and RT-PCR (3.5.1), LAMP (3.5.2), RPA and RAA (3.5.3), CRISPR-Cas detection (3.5.4) and other isothermal, enzyme-free and amplification-free approaches (3.5.5). Section 3.5.6 considers integrated and multiplex devices and 3.5.7 states what the evidence supports for the colour signal. Papers read in full are marked † in the tables; all other statements rest on abstracts. The section covers 382 included records, all from 2015-2026; one was read in full. **How the signal is read.** Every record in this section ends in a lateral flow strip. Reading the abstracts, 191 state that the strip is read by eye (32 of them also report a second readout), 10 state that a device is used (smartphone, reader, scanner or densitometer, or either eye or device), 180 describe a strip without saying how it is read (31 of them also report a second readout), and one record has no abstract. Altogether 64 records report a second readout (fluorescence, real-time fluorescence, time-resolved fluorescence, blue light, glucose meter or electrochemical); they are marked ‡ in the tables, and for them the figures in the text are attributed to the strip or to the second readout wherever the abstract allows it. The tables show the reading mode exactly as the abstract gives it, and "not stated" means only that the abstract is silent. No record was excluded under the colorimetric rule: all 382 studies are built around a lateral flow strip readout (one has no abstract, so the strip could not be confirmed from it), and the figures for a second readout are not counted as colorimetric results.

Records combining two methods are described under the method that dominates the design, and CRISPR studies that use RPA, RAA or LAMP are kept in the CRISPR subsection.

## 3.5.1 PCR and RT-PCR with a strip readout

Strip detection of PCR products is the oldest approach in this section. In every study the strategy is the same at its core: the amplicon is labelled during PCR (for example with biotin and a hapten on the primers) so that a strip can show it as a coloured line, and what differs between studies is what the authors changed around that step. The studies within the 2015-2026 window fall into four groups by what was changed, and each is described by that change and by the improvement the abstract reports.

**Putting the thermal cycler inside the strip device.** The strategy here is to remove the laboratory thermocycler. An infrared-heated PCR platform with a compatible strip was built for human papillomavirus (HPV), but its abstract gives no performance value (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2017.04.047). A PCR on a paper strip heated by a Joule heater was developed for SARS-CoV-2, with an incomplete abstract and no values (DOI: 10.1039/d2mh01267g). A portable PCR coupled to a gold-nanoparticle strip was built for HPV16, again without values in the abstract (DOI: 10.2147/mder.s508723). A plasmon-driven photonic PCR integrated with a strip reached 150 copies/mL for Seoul orthohantavirus, but the abstract names no comparator (DOI: 10.1002/adhm.71600). The improvement claimed in this group is portability and speed, not a lower detection limit, and it is not quantified against a laboratory PCR in these abstracts.

**Reading several targets from one reaction.** The strategy is multiplexing, with different capture lines for different amplicons. A multiplex nested PCR with an immunochromatographic test typed HPV16 and HPV18, detecting 100 copies of HPV DNA (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2014.10.009), and an integrated strip platform typed the same two HPV types at 700 copies (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-022-05449-5). A multiplex one-step RT-PCR with dipstick chromatography covered seven respiratory pathogens on 896 samples and was compared with Sanger sequencing (values not reported) (DOI: 10.1007/s00284-021-02621-7). A lateral flow strip membrane assay read two SARS-CoV-2 genes after RT-PCR, detecting 10 copies per test per gene, with positive agreement 100% and negative agreement 99.0% on 162 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.0c03202), and a one-step fast multiplex RT-PCR coupled to a lateral flow immunoassay for SARS-CoV-2 reached sensitivity 88% and specificity 98% against RT-qPCR on 100 samples (DOI: 10.1186/s12879-024-10296-1). A dual-serotype strip read dengue virus serotypes after RT-PCR at 10 copies for serotype 1 and 100 copies for serotypes 2 to 4, with sensitivity 100% for all serotypes and specificity 100% for serotypes 1 to 3 and 95% for serotype 4 (DOI: 10.1016/j.diagmicrobio.2025.116679). Fish viruses were covered with a dual strip for nodavirus genotypes (DOI: 10.1155/2018/7691014) and a PCR-strip assay for red sea bream iridovirus and viral haemorrhagic septicaemia virus (DOI: 10.1007/s13337-020-00577-z). The improvement here is the number of targets read in one test; the abstracts state no loss of sensitivity in the multiplex format, but they also give no single-plex comparison.

**Changing the label or the particle.** The strategy is to change what makes the line visible. For fish nervous necrosis virus, a gold-nanoparticle lateral flow biosensor read the RT-PCR product and detected the amplicon from 270 pg of total RNA (DOI: 10.1016/j.mcp.2015.03.005). For Epstein-Barr virus, a paper-based nucleic-acid strip combined asymmetric PCR, which makes single-stranded product that can hybridise directly to the capture probe, with Au@SiO2 particles, and gave a lower LOD (50 nM target DNA) than the same strip without asymmetric PCR and without Au@SiO2 (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2022.339514). A digoxigenin-labelled primer pair on an HIV-1 RT-PCR strip gave results consistent with gel electrophoresis (DOI: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e32784). In this group the improvement is demonstrated against a within-paper alternative only for the Epstein-Barr virus strip.

**Other formats after PCR.** A single-tag hybridisation chromatographic-printed array strip detected dengue virus in clinical samples with sensitivity 100% and specificity 88.9% against TaqMan (86.6% against an NS1 test) (DOI: 10.1007/s10096-018-03453-3). An open-source nucleic-acid lateral flow immunoassay for chikungunya virus gave sensitivity and specificity of 100% against culture and qRT-PCR, with a LOD of 10 RNA copies/mL (DOI: 10.4014/jmb.2108.08025). A PCR-lateral flow test for cyprinid herpesvirus 3 detected 9 gene copies/uL with specificity 100% and sensitivity 94.81% (DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0241420). A reverse transcription multienzyme isothermal rapid amplification (RT-MIRA) strip detected hepatitis A virus at 1 copy/uL compared with conventional RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1080/21691401.2023.2203198). Cross-priming amplification with a strip for koi herpesvirus detected 675.69 copies/uL, against 8.384 copies/uL for real-time PCR run in the same study, so the strip was about 80 times less sensitive than the real-time reference (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2024.114890).

Limit addressed in 3.5.1: the number of copies reaching the line, through thermal cycling. What the studies show is that PCR strips are mostly validated against the PCR itself; only the Epstein-Barr virus strip and the koi herpesvirus comparison state a within-paper difference in sensitivity. Cost: a thermocycler or an integrated heater, and a separate step before the strip.

## 3.5.2 Loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP)

LAMP amplifies at one temperature with a strand-displacing polymerase and four to six primers. Its products are long and branched, so the main technical question for a strip is how to label and capture them. The studies differ in what they changed: the reporter system, the sample handling and device, or the number of targets. Each is described by its strategy and the improvement it reports.

**Reporters and labelling of the LAMP product.** The strategies here replace or add to the standard biotin-and-hapten primer pair. Nanoparticle-based lateral flow biosensors (LFB) with gold nanoparticles were paired with LAMP for SARS-CoV-2 (multiplex RT-LAMP at 12 copies per reaction per target, with sensitivity 100% and specificity 100% on 33 positive and 96 negative samples; and another multiplex RT-LAMP LFB at 20 copies per reaction) (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2020.112437; DOI: 10.3389/fcimb.2021.581239), for hepatitis B virus (7.5 IU per test, specificity 100%) (DOI: 10.3389/fbioe.2021.731415), for monkeypox virus (5 copies/uL for plasmid and 12.5 copies/uL for pseudovirus, in two studies) (DOI: 10.3389/fpubh.2023.1132896; DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2023.125502), for Epstein-Barr virus (45 copies per reaction) (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-023-12948-9), for HIV-1 (20 copies per test) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2023.1230533), for hepatitis C virus (20 copies per test) (DOI: 10.1186/s12866-024-03220-9), and for hepatitis B and C virus together (20 copies per test) (DOI: 10.1186/s12866-024-03610-z). Gold magnetic nanoparticles in a dual RT-LAMP strip for influenza A and B reached 80 copies/mL with 100% agreement with fluorescent PCR (DOI: 10.1111/1751-7915.70169). Beyond particles, the reporter itself was changed: biotin-labelled dUTP incorporated during LAMP (accuracy 81.66% for SARS-CoV-2) (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-022-03880-4), biotinylated DNA probes read on a lateral flow strip from crude oropharyngeal swabs (3 RNA copies/uL, with the test-line intensity correlating with the qRT-PCR Ct value) (DOI: 10.3390/bios13110988), an avidin-biotin complex lateral flow assay for porcine circovirus 2 (low LOD, value not reported) (DOI: 10.1039/d1ay01189h), and commercial pregnancy test strips used as the readout, where an hCG-SNAP reporter linked LAMP to the strip for Ebola virus at 20 copies (DOI: 10.1002/anie.201609108) and a DNA nanoflower with toehold-mediated strand displacement linked it for hepatitis B virus at 2 copies/uL, identifying wild-type and rtL180M genotypes (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202108827). A study of the LAMP products captured on strips examined what the amplicons look like on the membrane for HPV16 (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-020-02702-9), and a rotating-extraction LAMP-LFA for HPV16 and HPV18 reached sensitivity 94.74% (18/19) on squamous cell carcinoma samples (DOI: 10.1002/advs.202509468). The improvement in this group is mostly in detection limits in the range of single to tens of copies per reaction, and a within-paper comparison is stated for only a few.

**Integrating the steps into one device.** The strategy is to remove pipetting and sample preparation. A centrifugal microdevice combined RT-LAMP and a strip for influenza A (H1N1) at 10 copies of viral RNA (DOI: 10.1039/c4lc01033g). The microRAAD device ran RT-LAMP and a lateral flow immunoassay from whole blood for HIV-1, reaching 100 copies in a tube and 3 x 10^5 viral particles on the device, so the integration cost sensitivity (DOI: 10.1039/c9lc00506d). A one-pot RT-LAMP lateral flow assay for SARS-CoV-2 gave 100% accuracy on clinical samples (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.0c05059), a palm-sized "PaGeR" RT-LAMP with a dipstick detected 15 of 17 positive samples and no false positives in 55 negatives (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2021.113925), and a direct RT-LAMP LFA without RNA extraction had LAMP-LFA sensitivity 100% and specificity 77.27% (against 95% and 75% for LAMP alone) (DOI: 10.1016/j.plabm.2024.e00437). Simple extraction was addressed in a SARS-CoV-2 kit that reached 100 PFU/mL with Chelex extraction and 10^-1 PFU/mL with commercial extraction, with sensitivity 97.8% and specificity 100% (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics13183001). Direct amplification from a lysate gave 10^3 IU/mL for hepatitis B virus in heat-treated serum (DOI: 10.1007/s11033-023-08730-9). A portable sample-in-result-out device for chikungunya virus detected 598.46 copies/mL and distinguished it from dengue, Japanese encephalitis, Zika and yellow fever viruses (DOI: 10.3390/mi15050663). Immunocapture of canine parvovirus before LAMP reached 10^-1 TCID50/mL (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2017.08.009). Helicase-dependent amplification was compared with LAMP in the same strip format for SARS-CoV-2: 3 genome copies per reaction for helicase-dependent amplification and 30 to 300 for RT-LAMP (DOI: 10.3390/biomedicines10092329). The improvement here is operational (fewer steps, no extraction), and it is accompanied by lower sensitivity in some cases (microRAAD, direct RT-LAMP).

**Multiplex LAMP strips.** The strategy is to read several targets or add a control. For influenza A viruses, a multiplex RT-LAMP strip subtyped H1N1, H3N2 and H5N1 at 10 copies of RNA (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2014.10.020), and a multiplex LAMP strip for influenza A and B reached sensitivity 94.1% and 96.6% and specificity 98% against a commercial panel (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics14090967). For animal viruses: a duplex RT-LAMP for Macrobrachium rosenbergii nodavirus and extra small virus was about 100-fold more sensitive than conventional PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.mcp.2018.05.001); a multiplex RT-LAMP for infectious bronchitis virus and Newcastle disease virus gave the same LOD in duplex and single form (DOI: 10.3382/ps/pez372); a duplex LAMP for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus and porcine circovirus 2 had sensitivity, specificity and accuracy of 100% against PCR (DOI: 10.3390/cimb44110368); and a triplex LAMP for porcine epidemic diarrhea virus, porcine rotavirus and porcine bocavirus reached 24.0, 28.9 and 25.2 copies/uL with above 99% consistency with rt-qPCR (DOI: 10.3390/ani13121910). For SARS-CoV-2: a multiplexed RT-LAMP dipstick at 100 copies (DOI: 10.3390/microorganisms11051233), a multiplex RT-LAMP LFA with a human control at 500 copies/mL (kappa 0.876 against RT-qPCR) (DOI: 10.1186/s12879-023-08924-3), and a multiplex test distinguishing Omicron lineages (BA.1 sensitivity and specificity 100%, BA.2 100% and 95%) (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay01798f). A single-tube four-target LAMP strip detected HPV16, 18 and 45 at 100, 10 and 100 copies per reaction (DOI: 10.1016/j.ab.2024.115480), a single-tube single-strip assay detected hepatitis B virus (10 copies) and hepatitis C virus (10^3 copies) (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.29721), and a multi-platform LAMP system with calcein and immunochromatographic readout for human adenovirus 3 and 7 reached 2.5 copies per reaction (DOI: 10.1007/s10096-025-05224-3). A LAMP-NALF strategy for human adenovirus 3 in children showed 98% agreement with targeted next-generation sequencing and qPCR (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay01973c). The improvement is the number of targets per strip; where a single-plex comparison is given, the LOD was unchanged.

**Single-target LAMP-strip assays.** Many studies apply LAMP with a lateral flow dipstick to one virus, and their improvement is stated against another method. Against calcein-based or colorimetric LAMP: 10-fold higher sensitivity for Japanese encephalitis virus (5 pg RNA, with no false positives in 14 sera) (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2014.12.006). Against RT-PCR: 100-fold for Jembrana disease virus (DOI: 10.1007/s13337-015-0277-5), 10-fold for Senecavirus A (11 copies/uL, with a positive rate comparable to RT-PCR) (DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0216245), and 100-fold for avian leukosis virus (10^2 RNA copies/uL) (DOI: 10.1007/s00705-024-05977-w). Against several PCR formats: fowl adenovirus 4 at 10 copies/uL, against 10 for nested PCR, 10^2 for real-time PCR and 10^4 for PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2019.04.026). Equal to the reference: measles virus at 8.8 copies/uL of synthetic RNA, equal to real-time RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.diagmicrobio.2016.02.023), and cyprinid herpesvirus 2 at 0.18 pg/uL, the same as PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.mcp.2020.101507). Others report only a detection limit or agreement: coxsackievirus A16 at 0.55 TCID50 per reaction (93.3% agreement with real-time RT-PCR, specificity 100%) (DOI: 10.1111/lam.12499); West Nile virus at 10 copies (kappa 0.970 against qRT-PCR, with a colorimetric RT-LAMP in the same study) (DOI: 10.1111/jam.15783); hepatitis B virus with sensitivity 92% (138/150) and specificity 100% (DOI: 10.1007/s42770-022-00690-9); swine influenza A virus with sensitivity 83.5% and specificity 89.6% (DOI: 10.1016/j.vetmic.2024.110174); SARS-CoV-2 at 18.92 copies/uL (DOI: 10.3389/fpubh.2026.1798338); and herpesvirus of turkeys vaccines, where all vaccine strains were detected (DOI: 10.3389/fvets.2022.873163). Studies with no values or only partial abstracts include Zika virus (two studies; one using Bst 3.0 polymerase) (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.6b03460; DOI: 10.1039/d1ra01227d), HPV16 and HPV18 (DOI: 10.3892/mmr.2017.6370), African swine fever virus (no abstract) (DOI: 10.1007/s12250-020-00309-z), a new badnavirus in chinaberry (DOI: 10.3390/v13122408), and SARS-CoV-2 with electrochemical readout in the same study (E-sensor LOD log 1.79 pg/uL) (DOI: 10.3390/ijms232113105).

Limit addressed in 3.5.2: amplification at a single temperature. What the abstracts show is detection limits from single to tens of copies and, where a comparison is stated, equality with or 10- to 100-fold improvement over conventional PCR. Cost: long branched products that need a labelling strategy for the strip, the risk of carry-over contamination when tubes are opened, and, in the integrated devices, a loss of sensitivity in some cases.

## 3.5.3 Recombinase polymerase and recombinase-aided amplification (RPA, RAA) and related isothermal methods

RPA and RAA amplify at 37-42 C with recombinase proteins and a strand-displacing polymerase, and the product is read on a strip, usually through an internal probe or labelled primers. This is the largest group in the review (134 records). Most of the studies apply the same recipe to a new virus, so the points of interest are what each study changed around the recipe and what improvement it reports. They are described below by that change: heating, the strip against a real-time readout of the same reaction, multiplexing, reaction design, sample preparation, and then the plant, animal and human viruses for which the main result is a comparison with PCR.

**Removing the heater.** The strategy is to run the reaction without an instrument. Body-heat RPA with a strip detected canine parvovirus 2 in 60 faecal samples (46 positive, equal to real-time PCR, with 1.0 x 10^2 copies per reaction) (DOI: 10.1016/j.mcp.2018.04.004); an incubation-free RT-RPA strip for canine distemper virus gave 62.0% positives (20/32), the same as real-time RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2018.07.007); an equipment-free RT-RPA for foot-and-mouth disease virus (FMDV) gave 45.5% positives (25/55) against 52.7% (29/55) by real-time RT-PCR, so it missed some positives (DOI: 10.1186/s12917-018-1594-x); body-heat RPA-LFD for feline herpesvirus 1 detected 10^3 copies against 10^4 for PCR (DOI: 10.1007/s00705-018-4064-7); and body heat drove an RT-RPA strip for tomato chlorotic spot virus at about 6 pg/uL of total RNA (DOI: 10.1186/s12985-023-02097-w). The improvement is operational, with sensitivity equal to the reference or slightly lower.

**The strip against a real-time readout of the same reaction.** Many papers develop a real-time and a strip version of one RPA assay, which shows what the strip costs in sensitivity. The strip equalled the real-time readout for porcine circovirus 2 (10^2 copies per reaction for both) (DOI: 10.1155/2017/8403642) and for human mastadenovirus B, where real-time RPA correlated with real-time PCR (50 copies per reaction) (DOI: 10.1186/s12866-018-1365-7). It was slightly worse for pseudorabies virus (160 copies for the strip, 100 for real-time) (DOI: 10.1016/j.mcp.2017.03.005), peste des petits ruminants virus (150 against 100) (DOI: 10.1186/s12985-017-0688-6), Newcastle disease virus (10^2 copies/uL for RT-RAA-LFD against 10 for real-time RT-RAA, and 10-fold below PCR) (DOI: 10.1016/j.psj.2020.03.018), West Nile virus with an internal control (1,000 plasmid copies for the strip against 10 copies and 1.6 PFU in real time, sensitivity and specificity 100% against RT-qPCR) (DOI: 10.3967/bes2019.116), hepatitis E virus (LOD95 247 copies/uL for the strip against 25 by fluorescence) (DOI: 10.1128/jcm.01064-24), respiratory syncytial virus A and B (6.05 x 10^3 and 7.80 x 10^2 copies/uL by strip) (DOI: 10.3389/fcimb.2026.1838582), and large yellow croaker iridovirus (10^1 copies/uL by strip against 10^2 by RPA alone, so here the strip was the more sensitive readout) (DOI: 10.1111/jfd.13930). The strip was also better for capripoxviruses (10^1 against 10^2 copies per reaction, detecting 15/15 against 13/15 samples) (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2023.114788), for porcine encephalomyocarditis virus (10^1 copies for the biosensor against 10^2 for real-time, with 97.83% agreement with qRT-PCR on 92 samples) (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2022.114544), and for hepatitis E virus in raw pork livers (24 copies/uL for the strip against 154 for real-time RPA) (DOI: 10.3389/fcimb.2022.958990). Other pairs report a strip with similar detection to real time or to qPCR: porcine parvovirus (400 copies per reaction by strip, sensitivity 94.4% and specificity 100% against qPCR on 128 samples) (DOI: 10.1016/j.mcp.2016.08.011), goatpox and sheeppox virus (3 x 10^2 copies per reaction on 107 clinical samples) (DOI: 10.1186/s12985-017-0792-7), bovine rotavirus A (14 copies per reaction, positive rate 46.27% by strip against 45.52% real-time on 134 faecal samples) (DOI: 10.1186/s12917-022-03437-8), infectious myonecrosis virus (10^1 copies/uL for RT-ERA-LFD, against 10^0 for nested RT-PCR and 10^2 for real-time RT-PCR) (DOI: 10.1016/j.jip.2024.108143), cyprinid herpesvirus 3 (1.3 copies per reaction in real time; the strip value is not in the abstract) (DOI: 10.1111/jfd.13960), and monkeypox virus (14 copies by fluorescence and strip, with consistent detection at 45 copies) (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2026.115405). A dengue virus 2 RT-RPA-LFD reached LOD95 of 50.37 copies against 38.48 for the Twista fluorescence readout, on serum, culture and FTA cards (DOI: 10.3389/fcimb.2025.1578549).

**Reading more than one target.** The strategy is duplex or multiplex strips with several capture lines. For human viruses: a duplex RPA-LFD for influenza A and B reached 500 and 50 copies per reaction, with sensitivity 78.57% (A) and 87.50% (B) and specificity 100% against real-time PCR (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-019-01840-z); a RT-RPA-LFD for influenza A (matrix, H1 and H3) reached 123.6, 677.1 and 112.2 copies per reaction with sensitivity of 75%, 93.33% and 71.43% and specificity 100% (DOI: 10.1016/j.mcp.2018.10.004); a duplex RT-RPA integrated strip screened SARS-CoV-2 and influenza viruses at 10 viral RNA copies with 100% accuracy on 30 samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2021.113771); a duplex RAA-LFD for hepatitis B virus with an internal control reached sensitivity of 97.18% and 95.77% with specificity 100% against qPCR (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.25778); a duplex real-time and strip RT-RAA for enterovirus 71 and coxsackievirus A16 reached 91 copies per reaction by strip (DOI: 10.1186/s12985-019-1264-z); a duplex RT-RAA for Zika virus (8.5 copies) and Japanese encephalitis virus (1.1 copies; 110 copies when co-detected) (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2025.128195); and an RPA-LF for four human adenovirus species B serotypes (3, 7, 21 and 55) with 10 to 280 copies per reaction and sensitivity above 92% (DOI: 10.1155/2021/9957747). For animal viruses: serotype-specific RT-RPA-LFD for FMDV serotypes A, O and Asia1 (98.41% concordance with real-time PCR on 126 clinical samples, with two additional positives confirmed) (DOI: 10.1186/s12917-018-1644-4); a RPA-nucleic acid lateral flow immunoassay for Newcastle disease and infectious bronchitis viruses (10 genomic copies per reaction) (DOI: 10.1007/s11033-019-05085-y); an RPA-LFD co-detecting epizootic haemorrhagic disease virus and Palyam serogroup viruses (7.1 and 6.8 copies/uL, coincidence above 94.8% with qRT-PCR) (DOI: 10.1186/s12917-021-02977-9); a RT-RPA-LFD for bovine viral diarrhoea virus and bovine parainfluenza virus 3 (50 and 34 RNA molecules per reaction) (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2021.114343); a RAA strip for African swine fever virus and classical swine fever virus (10^3 copies/uL; 10 pg and 12 pg of viral nucleic acid) (DOI: 10.3389/fmolb.2021.811824); an RPA-LFD for bovine viral diarrhoea virus and infectious bovine rhinotracheitis virus (51 and 66.5 copies/uL, consistent with qPCR on 32 clinical samples) (DOI: 10.3389/fvets.2024.1360504); and RT-RAA-LFD for avian influenza H5, H7 and H9 (10^2 copies/uL by strip, 10 to 100 times better than gel-read RT-PCR, and 10^1 copies/uL in real time, coincidence above 95%) (DOI: 10.1186/s12917-024-04040-9). The improvement is the number of targets per strip; sensitivity per target is unchanged or slightly lower than single-plex where a comparison exists.

**Changing the reaction or the labelling.** The strategy is to change how the RPA product is designed, labelled or suppressed, to gain specificity or remove a source of false results. Betaine-assisted RPA for hepatitis B virus DNA reached 1,000 copies per 50 uL with sensitivity 90% and specificity 100% on serum (DOI: 10.1002/bab.1940). Graphene oxide and self-avoiding molecular recognition systems (Nano-SAMRS) suppressed non-specific amplification in an RPA strip for HPV16 and HPV18, detecting 10 copies per reaction with 14/14 positives and 75/75 negatives (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-020-04637-5). A peptide nucleic acid (TeaPNA) step suppressed false positives in a SARS-CoV-2 RPA strip, with 6.7 copies/uL of RNA, 90 copies/uL of virus, specificity 100% and sensitivity 100% at Ct below 31 (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.114989). A comparison of primer labelling against a nuclease-dependent labelled probe in RPA found a 22-fold lower half-maximal binding concentration for primer labelling, with 10^3 RNA copies in the reaction (a model RNA virus; scope check) (DOI: 10.3390/ijms222111885). Cost: primer design and non-specific background (addressed by betaine, PNA and Nano-SAMRS additions), and a tube-opening step before the strip.

## 3.5.4 CRISPR-Cas detection with a strip readout

CRISPR-Cas systems add a second, sequence-specific recognition step after, or instead of, amplification. A guide RNA directs a Cas enzyme to its target; Cas12 and Cas13 then cut a reporter molecule, which is read on a strip, while Cas9 and dCas9 variants hold labelled probes at the test line without cutting a reporter. The 93 studies differ in the enzyme, the amplification before it, and the workflow around it, and they are described below by what the authors changed and the improvement they report.

**First strip adaptations (2018-2020).** The earliest studies moved existing CRISPR diagnostics onto strips. SHERLOCK version 2 with a lateral-flow readout detected dengue and Zika virus RNA in patient samples, and an added Csm6 enzyme gave a 3.5-fold signal gain (DOI: 10.1126/science.aaq0179). A Cas13-based SHERLOCK gave a colorimetric lateral flow readout for white spot syndrome virus in shrimp at the single-copy level (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-019-56170-y), and Cas13a assays with fluorescent and strip readouts were built for Ebola and Lassa viruses (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-020-17994-9). Cas9 was used for African swine fever virus (ASFV): the CRISPR/Cas9 lateral flow assay (CASLFA) identified ASFV in 110 swine sera with 100% accuracy against RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.0c00022), a Cas12a-immunochromatographic strip detected 20 copies per reaction in 149 clinical samples with 100% agreement with real-time PCR (DOI: 10.1038/s42003-020-0796-5), and a Cas12a "universal" lateral flow biosensor detected 2.5 x 10^-15 M in swine blood (DOI: 10.3390/bios10120203). For human viruses, Cas12a on strips detected HPV16/18 DNA in plasma, with 0.24 fM sensitivity equal to PCR (HPV16 in 13 of 14 patients, HPV18 in 3 of 10) (DOI: 10.1016/j.tranon.2019.08.011), and Epstein-Barr virus with PCR pre-amplification at 7.1 x 10^-14 M (DOI: 10.1039/d0an00663g). The DETECTR assay was adapted to SARS-CoV-2 and compared with CDC RT-PCR on 36 patients and 42 patients with other viral infections, with positive percent agreement 95% and negative percent agreement 100% (DOI: 10.1038/s41587-020-0513-4); a later study reported higher analytical sensitivity for DETECTR than for qRT-PCR on 378 patient samples (DOI: 10.1093/infdis/jiaa641).

**SARS-CoV-2: the choice of enzyme.** The pandemic produced the largest cluster, and the studies can be read as a comparison of enzymes. With Cas13, a SHERLOCK-based assay detected 42 RNA copies per reaction on 154 swabs from Thailand, with sensitivity 97% and specificity 100% for the strip, against 100% and 100% by fluorescence, so the strip lost a little sensitivity (DOI: 10.1038/s41551-020-00603-x); CASSPIT combined an extraction-free saliva workflow with Cas13 and a strip, reaching 97% positive agreement with RNA extraction at about 200 copies (DOI: 10.3389/fcimb.2021.632646); CasRx (Cas13d) gave strip and fluorescence readouts at 45 copies/uL with 100% sensitivity (DOI: 10.1021/acssensors.1c01088); SHINEv.2 identified SARS-CoV-2 variants of concern with sensitivity 90.5% and specificity 100% against RT-qPCR (DOI: 10.1038/s41551-022-00889-z); another Cas13a strip was tested on 52 positive and 101 negative samples (values not in the abstract) (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.28096); and a Cas13a system with RPA, read by a fluorescence analyser and a strip, reached 0.68 fM (S gene) and 4.16 fM (Orf1ab) (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2022.123594). With Cas12, the ERASE strip reached 1 copy/uL and was tested on 649 blinded clinical samples (positive agreement 90.67%, negative agreement 99.21%) (DOI: 10.1089/crispr.2020.0138); CRICOLAP combined RT-LAMP and Cas12a on a strip with fluorescence, at 1 copy/uL, with sensitivity 97.5% for the S gene and 100% for the N gene and specificity 100% (DOI: 10.3390/bios12010011); OPERATOR combined rolling-circle amplification with FnCas12a in one pot at 1.625 copies per reaction (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2023.115179); and a magnetic-bead extraction preceded RT-LAMP and Cas12a at 6.2 x 10^2 copies/mL (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay00757c). With Cas9 and dCas9, which avoid a reporter-cleavage step, a dual-gene Cas9 assay read the E and Orf1ab genes on 64 swabs at 100 RNA copies per reaction (negative agreement 100%, positive agreement 97.14%) (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202014506), a Cas9 strip read synthetic SARS-CoV-2 sequences by lateral flow and fluorescence (no values) (DOI: 10.3390/bioengineering8020023), Vigilant used a VirD2-dCas9 complex after RT-RPA at 2.5 copies/uL with no cross-reactivity with SARS-CoV-1 or MERS (DOI: 10.1021/acs.nanolett.1c00612), Bio-SCAN used dCas9 on 86 swabs at 4 copies/uL (positive agreement 96%, negative agreement 100%) (DOI: 10.1021/acssynbio.1c00499), and a Cas9 strand-displacement strip reached 1 to 10 copies/uL for SARS-CoV-2 and H1N1 (DOI: 10.1021/acsabm.5c00307). FnCas9 (FELUDA) with a lateral flow readout reached sensitivity 100% and specificity 97% on clinical samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2021.113207), and a second FnCas9 strip discriminated variants with sensitivity 87% and specificity 97% against sequencing on 37 wild-type and 22 mutant samples (DOI: 10.7554/elife.67130). Multiplex designs read two targets at once: an orthogonal CRISPR-Cas lateral flow assay classified 76 of 76 swabs correctly at 10 copies per test (DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2022.132537), and a dual-enzyme platform read ORF1ab and N on 105 swabs at 200 copies/mL (agreement 100% for positives and 95.7% for negatives) (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.29090). A paper device combining RT-LAMP and Cas12a detected SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater at 25, 310 and 10 copies/mL for the N, E and S genes, with sensitivity 97.7% (DOI: 10.1021/acs.est.2c04727), and a Cas12a-strand displacement design gave three readouts including a strip at 1 pM, against 53.1 fM by fluorescence (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2024.125922). The improvement across these studies is mainly in specificity (variant calling, no cross-reactivity), in workflow and in detection limits of a few copies; the Cas13 strip against fluorescence comparison shows a small loss in the strip.

**Workflows that remove steps.** Several designs target sample handling and the number of tubes. Direct PCR was coupled to Cas12a to detect ASFV without nucleic-acid extraction at 4 copies/uL (DOI: 10.1007/s00253-022-11999-8). PLACID uses paper-based LAMP and CRISPR with smartphone reading on crude samples at 50 copies/uL (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2024.116292). FELICX adds a flap endonuclease to Cas12 to detect DNA and RNA at 0.6 copies/uL in simulated samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2022.115002). TESTOR uses phosphorothioate primers in a one-pot Cas12a assay and optimises the gold-particle strip, and was validated on cervical scrapes for HPV (DOI: 10.1016/j.bioactmat.2021.05.005). A G-triplex reporter for Cas12a reached one copy per reaction for HPV, with sensitivity 94.7% and specificity 100% on 27 PCR-amplified patient samples (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2021.113292). A PCR plus Cas12a strip detected influenza A at 10 copies/uL with kappa 0.95 against fluorescent PCR on 82 clinical samples (DOI: 10.46234/ccdcw2024.198). Two studies replace the dedicated strip with a commercial pregnancy test: CLIPON links CRISPR cleavage to hCG detection (DOI: 10.1002/anie.202115907), and a Cas12a method reached 1 copy/uL with 96.3% agreement with RT-qPCR (DOI: 10.1039/d3an00284e); both are flagged for scope because they repurpose a strip designed for another target. A negative result: a CRISPR-dCas9 lateral flow assay for HPV16 and HPV18 failed because the amplification step left excess biotin that blocked the strip (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-44828-3).

**Amplification-free strips.** Four studies skip the amplification step. A cascade of Cas13a and Cas12a reached 1 aM for SARS-CoV-2 (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.4c02726), a Cas13a method (MAD) detected human adenovirus in 167 paediatric samples with 99.4% concordance with qPCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2024.116918), amplification-free Cas13a strips detected SARS-CoV-2 and H3N2 in a multiplex format (9 positives each; values not in the abstract) (DOI: 10.1186/s12985-025-02765-z), and a NESBA-Cas13a method detected SARS-CoV-2 at 0.05 copies/uL with 100% sensitivity and specificity on 20 clinical samples (DOI: 10.1021/acssynbio.4c00605).

**Human viruses.** Pairing RPA, RAA or LAMP with Cas12 or Cas13 and a strip was applied to many human viruses, and the strip is usually a convenient end point rather than the most sensitive one. Hepatitis viruses: HBV with Cas12 at 1 copy/uL, sensitivity and specificity 100% against qPCR on 73 samples (DOI: 10.3390/ijms22094842), RAA-Cas13a at 10 copies/uL on 180 patients with specificity 100% (DOI: 10.1080/22221751.2023.2177088), ERA-Cas12f1 at 100 copies/uL with sensitivity 94.23% and specificity 100% (kappa 0.90) on 71 samples (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay02079k), pgRNA detection with a nanoparticle biosensor and Cas12b at 10 copies per reaction (DOI: 10.1186/s12866-026-04900-4), and hepatitis D RNA by RT-RAA Cas13a at 10 copies/uL, as sensitive as RT-qPCR and RT-ddPCR, on 144 patients (DOI: 10.1080/22221751.2023.2276337). HIV-1 by RT-RAA Cas13a reached 1 copy/uL (112 copies/mL as the lowest viral load) with sensitivity 91.81% (DOI: 10.1186/s12879-023-08492-6). Norovirus: GII.4 by RT-RPA-Cas12a (9.65 x 10^2 copies/mL), GII.4 by Cas13 (5 copies per reaction), GII.4 and GII.17 by RT-RAA-Cas12a (0.1 copies/uL; positive agreement 95.7% and 94.3%) and GII.2 on 60 food-contaminated samples (10 copies/uL) (DOI: 10.1016/j.virol.2021.09.008; DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2022.912315; DOI: 10.1186/s12985-022-01878-z; DOI: 10.33073/pjm-2024-023). Respiratory viruses: human metapneumovirus by RT-RPA-Cas12a (6.97 x 10^2 copies/mL; 28 samples) and RAA-Cas13a (1 copy/uL; 106 samples), human rhinovirus A and C (0.1 copy/uL, positive agreement 96.7% and negative agreement 100% on 80 samples, with the strip equal to real time) (DOI: 10.1016/j.virusres.2021.198568; DOI: 10.1002/jmv.70921; DOI: 10.1016/j.virusres.2022.199001). HPV16 and HPV18 were detected on a microfluidic platform by RPA with Cas12a, with the lateral flow readout reaching 1 x 10^-18 M of amplified plasmids (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.2c04713). Oseltamivir-resistant influenza was detected at about 20 copies per reaction by RPA-CRISPR/Cas12a (DOI: 10.3724/abbs.2022163). Measles virus: RT-RPA-Cas12a at 31 RNA copies with sensitivity 96% and specificity 100% on saliva (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics14050517), and RT-LAMP-Cas12b at about 10^4 copies by strip against 10^5 by fluorescence, so here the strip was the more sensitive readout (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2026.345081). Other viruses: mpox at 10^1 copies/uL by fluorescence and 10^4 by lateral flow (DOI: 10.1039/d2ay01998a), Nipah virus at 10^3 copies/uL of synthetic cDNA (DOI: 10.1016/j.virusres.2023.199130), Lassa virus at 10^2 copies/uL by strip and 10^1 by fluorescence (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-07071-w), chikungunya virus on 146 plasma samples (strip sensitivity 94.52% against 97.26% for fluorescence; specificity 100% for both) (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.02298-25), yellow fever virus at 1 copy/uL, 100 PFU/mL in serum (DOI: 10.1016/j.bsheal.2026.03.001), tick-borne encephalitis virus (sensitivity and specificity 100% on 15 patients and 10 healthy volunteers) (DOI: 10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2024.133720), severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome virus (2.5 copies/uL, sensitivity and specificity 100% on 40 sera) (DOI: 10.4014/jmb.2603.03014), and Epstein-Barr virus by a two-step Cas13a strip at 10^4 copies per reaction against 10 by fluorescence (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2026.1850310).

**Animal, aquatic and plant viruses.** For ASFV, CRISPR assays were built for the D117L target (2 gene copies, 100% coincidence with qPCR on 68 and on 200 clinical samples), the KP177R target (6.8 copies/uL, kappa 1.000) and genotype II (10^1 copies/uL, 100% coincidence on 83 samples), and an orthogonal Cas12b/Cas13a strip separates wild-type from variant virus in under 60 minutes (DOI: 10.3390/ani13233712; DOI: 10.1016/j.vetmic.2024.110073; DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2024.1358960; DOI: 10.3390/v14020179; DOI: 10.1016/j.isci.2024.109050); a combined RPA-CRISPR assay with fluorescence and strip found a single copy with 100% coincidence (DOI: 10.1007/s12250-020-00323-1). Other swine viruses: porcine parvovirus by ERA-Cas12a (3.75 x 10^2 copies/uL) (DOI: 10.3389/fcimb.2022.879887), porcine circovirus 4 by RPA-Cas13a (15 samples) (DOI: 10.3390/microorganisms11020354), a porcine circovirus-like virus by LAMP-Cas12a (10 copies) (DOI: 10.1007/s00216-023-05020-y), PEDV with a wild-type versus vaccine-strain assay (10^1 copies/uL) (DOI: 10.3389/fcimb.2022.976137), Japanese encephalitis virus (2 copies, 100% concordance) (DOI: 10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2024.134151) and pseudorabies virus (1 copy/uL, 17 of 50 pig samples positive) (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2024.342918). Poultry and waterfowl: fowl adenovirus 4 (10^1 copies/uL) (DOI: 10.1080/03079457.2023.2254253), avian leukosis virus subgroups A, B and J (50 copies, no cross-reactivity with ALV-C/D/E, reticuloendotheliosis virus or Marek's disease virus) (DOI: 10.3390/v16071168), duck hepatitis A virus 3 and novel duck reovirus (10^0 copy/uL by fluorescence, 10^1 by strip) (DOI: 10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2024.133246), H5 avian influenza (0.1 copy/uL, kappa 0.89 on 380 samples; and 1.9 copies/uL by blue light against 1.9 x 10^3 by strip, with an 80.70% detection rate on 81 samples) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2023.1283210; DOI: 10.3390/v16050753), pan-H1-H16 avian influenza (69 copies/uL by fluorescence, 690 by strip) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2023.1288951) and avian influenza (1 copy/uL, 1000 times better than gel PCR) (DOI: 10.3389/fvets.2023.1272612). Others: feline calicivirus at 5.5 copies/uL (scope check) (DOI: 10.3389/fvets.2022.913780), scale drop disease virus (40 copies per reaction) (DOI: 10.1111/jfd.13541), and three plant-virus studies: tobacco mosaic virus, tobacco etch virus and potato virus X with Cas12a/Cas13 (DOI: 10.1021/acssynbio.2c00090), maize chlorotic mottle virus (2.5 copies of the coat protein gene, 0.96 pg total RNA) (DOI: 10.3389/fpls.2023.1088544) and four tobamoviruses (pepper mild mottle virus, tomato brown rugose fruit virus, tomato mosaic virus, tomato mottle mosaic virus) in a multiplex one-step assay (DOI: 10.1021/acs.jafc.3c05268).

Limit addressed in 3.5.4: specificity of the signal, by a second sequence-specific recognition step, and in some designs the removal of the amplification step. What the abstracts show is detection limits of a few copies, specific variant calling, and clinical comparisons with qPCR; the strip is often less sensitive than a fluorescence readout of the same reaction (SARS-CoV-2 Cas13, avian influenza, mpox, Lassa, Epstein-Barr, hepatitis E; see Table E4b). Cost: an extra reaction, the need to open tubes between amplification and Cas cleavage, and in many designs a parallel fluorescence readout.

## 3.5.5 Other isothermal, enzyme-free and amplification-free nucleic-acid strips

This group collects the isothermal methods other than LAMP and RPA, and approaches that raise the signal without a polymerase or without amplification. They are described by what the authors changed and what improvement they report.

**Cross-priming, multiple cross displacement and multienzyme amplification.** Cross-priming amplification (CPA) with a strip detected grouper nervous necrosis virus (10 copies/uL of RNA, 10-fold below RT-PCR) (DOI: 10.1186/s12985-015-0374-5), porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (10^-6 plasmid dilution, the same as PCR) (DOI: 10.1038/srep24702), prunus necrotic ringspot virus (72 copies per reaction, with no cross-reaction with Prunus viruses and viroids) (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-017-16536-6) and scale drop disease virus (100 viral copies/uL, 10 pg of total DNA, 10-fold below PCR, with all 25 positives detected and a colorimetric option) (DOI: 10.1111/jfd.13448). For bean pod mottle virus the RT-CPA strip was less sensitive than TaqMan RT-PCR but performed better in field samples (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-021-03562-8), and an RT-CPA strip for infectious haematopoietic necrosis virus was validated (values not in the abstract) (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2024.115008). Multiple cross displacement amplification (MCDA) with a nanoparticle biosensor reached single-digit to tens of copies for hepatitis B virus (polymer nanoparticles) (DOI: 10.2147/idr.s297645), monkeypox virus (5 copies per reaction for plasmid and 12.5 for pseudovirus in blood) (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.28479), coxsackievirus A16 (below 40 copies, 46 of 220 positives all identified) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2023.1121930), Epstein-Barr virus (10 copies in a modified MCDA, and 7 copies with specificity 100%) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2023.1268572; DOI: 10.3389/fcimb.2023.1321394), hepatitis C virus (10 copies per test) (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.29481), enterovirus A71 (40 copies of plasmid and 50 of pseudovirus, 30 positives concordant with RT-qPCR) (DOI: 10.1002/jmv.70059), hepatitis B and C viruses together (10 copies per test) (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.01738-24), coxsackievirus A6 (33 copies per reaction, 100% concordance with qRT-PCR) (DOI: 10.1021/acsinfecdis.5c01056), and influenza H1N1, H3N2 and H7N9 in a multiplex that was more sensitive than RT-PCR (no value) (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2025.115235). Polymerase spiral reaction with a nanoparticle biosensor detected hepatitis B virus at 5.4 copies/mL, 10 times better than qPCR and LAMP, with 29 of 82 ELISA-positive samples all detected (DOI: 10.3389/fmolb.2020.578892). Multienzyme isothermal rapid amplification (MIRA) with a dipstick was applied to hepatitis B virus (10 pg) (DOI: 10.3389/fmolb.2021.763079), bovine coronavirus (kappa 0.982 against RT-qPCR) (DOI: 10.3389/fvets.2022.1059934), chicken chaphamaparvovirus (21.3 copies, 100-fold below nested PCR) (DOI: 10.1016/j.psj.2023.103144), duck hepatitis B virus (45.6 copies per reaction, about 10-fold below PCR) (DOI: 10.3390/vetsci11050191), human cytomegalovirus (50 copies/uL) (DOI: 10.3389/fcimb.2024.1430302), HTLV-1 proviral DNA (compared with qPCR; values not in the abstract) (DOI: 10.1186/s12967-025-06642-9) and SARS-CoV-2 in a duplex format (49.5 copies/mL for ORF1ab and 48.8 for N; kappa 1.00 against qPCR) (DOI: 10.3389/fcimb.2021.678703). A one-tube semi-nested RT-MIRA for chikungunya virus reached 1 copy/uL, 100-fold better than RT-MIRA and 10-fold better than RT-qPCR, with 18 of 21 samples detected against 17 of 21 by RT-qPCR (DOI: 10.1128/jcm.00383-24). The improvement in these studies is the detection limit against PCR (10- to 100-fold where stated) and the number of targets in the multiplexes.

**Other enzymatic amplification and signal chemistry.** Nucleic acid sequence-based amplification (NASBA) with a strip detected Macrobrachium rosenbergii nodavirus down to 1.0 fg (DOI: 10.1016/j.jip.2023.107921), and a NASBA lateral flow readout with sequencing detected SARS-CoV-2 below 50 RNA copies per reaction (95% LOD) in a population-scale scheme (DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.abe5054). A one-step RT with strand displacement amplification and a lateral flow biosensor detected respiratory RNA viruses in a multiplex at 20 copies/uL (DOI: 10.3390/bios14020103); exponential rolling-circle amplification coupled to a lateral flow device detected SARS-CoV-2 RNA at 500 aM with accuracy of 95 to 100% (DOI: 10.1002/anie.3766984); a CBT-Cys click ligation with rolling-circle amplification on a pregnancy-test strip detected hepatitis A virus at 3.2 fM (scope check: pregnancy test strip) (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2025.344357); and a RNA:DNA hybrid immunocapture strip with TMIRA detected SARS-CoV-2 RNA at 10 copies/uL with sensitivity 96.6% and specificity 100% against RT-qPCR (DOI: 10.1093/pnasnexus/pgad031). Tailed primers and amplicons were used for SARS-CoV-2 (25 RNA copies per reaction, sensitivity and specificity 100% on 20 samples) and for HPV16, 18 and 45 in a cartridge (50 to 500 copies per reaction) (DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0301234; DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-47582-y); isothermal amplification-coupled dipsticks detected SARS-CoV-2 at 6 copies/uL and influenza virus without values in the abstract (DOI: 10.1099/jmm.0.001519; DOI: 10.1007/s00216-022-04090-8); and an isothermal amplification with a target-mimicking internal control gave a quantitative lateral flow readout that separated HIV-1 DNA loads of 600 or less from 1400 or more copies at a 1000-copy threshold (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.1c03960).

**Enzyme-free amplification by DNA circuits.** The strategy is to amplify the signal with DNA strand-displacement circuits, so the strip needs no polymerase. Catalytic hairpin assembly (CHA) gave 2000 copies/mL with 100% positive and negative agreement for SARS-CoV-2 (DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2021.129899), 1 pM for influenza A and B and 5 pM for respiratory syncytial virus (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.2c00882), sensitivity of 81.8% at 35 C and 74% at 25 C for influenza A on a colloidal gold strip (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2023.124855), a dual-RNA SARS-CoV-2 strip with sensitivity comparable to RT-qPCR with upstream amplification (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c02019), an SFTSV RNA strip compatible with RT-PCR (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.02144-23), a norovirus gold strip at 10 pM against 100 fM for the paired fluorescent strip (sensitivity 73.3% against 86.7%, both with specificity 100%) (DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.6c00618), and a chikungunya virus strip at 1 pM against 100 fM for a time-resolved fluorescence readout (sensitivity 92% and AUC 0.984 against 94%) (DOI: 10.1016/j.aca.2026.345818). Hybridisation chain reaction (HCR) with helper oligonucleotides detected folded single-stranded dengue virus DNA in the nanomolar range (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2022.123588), and an HCR strip for a SARS-CoV-2 gene segment reached 10 fM by colour and 1 fM by a second readout (DOI: 10.1039/d4ay00074a). Entropy-driven amplification gave 1.43 pM for target DNA and 2.02 pM for H1N1 RNA (DOI: 10.1039/d0an02273j); duplex-specific nuclease with an isothermal amplification gave 829 fM for H1N1 (DOI: 10.1016/j.enzmictec.2025.110653); a primer extension isothermal amplification on an M13mp18 scaffold gave 10 pM by eye for H1N1, and a pre-amplified dsDNA-tag strip gave 1 pM, 100-fold better than the conventional strip, for H1N1 (DOI: 10.1007/s00604-025-07031-1; DOI: 10.1016/j.biochi.2026.01.006); a XNAzyme-based system (REVEALR) detected SARS-CoV-2 at 20 aM or less (about 10 copies/uL) with perfect accuracy on clinical samples (DOI: 10.1021/jacs.1c02664); peptide nucleic acid probes detected SARS-CoV-2 at 2 copies/uL and single-nucleotide variants (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.3c01809); and a lateral flow with logic determination read co-existing viral RNA fragments (scope check; no values) (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2024.116519). Where the paper compares readouts, the colour readout is less sensitive than a fluorescent one (norovirus and chikungunya virus, by a factor of 100).

**Amplification-free probes.** Some strips detect the nucleic acid directly. A gold-nanoparticle lateral flow biosensor detected four haemorrhagic fever viruses at 10 fM (DOI: 10.1039/d0ay01137a); a nucleic acid lateral flow immunoassay detected BK polyomavirus at 5 nM of synthetic ssDNA, or 10^7 copies/mL, with no cross-reactivity with JC virus or SV40 (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics10060403); nucleic acid probe-enabled strips detected influenza A H1N1 and H3N2 at attomolar sensitivity and discriminated infected from non-infected samples (DOI: 10.1002/cbic.202500216); an amplification-free clade-specific platform for mpox with ssDNA probes reached 10^2 copies/uL (DOI: 10.1039/d5cc06114h); an RNA extraction-free strip for SARS-CoV-2 with chemical probes and cysteamine-capped gold nanoparticles showed accuracy and specificity of about 99.99% against TaqPath (DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2021.113900); a hybridisation-based sandwich strip for RdRp/Orf1ab gave 100% sensitivity and specificity (DOI: 10.1016/j.jviromet.2023.114774); a probe design for the conserved N gene region (DOI: 10.1590/1414-431x2025e14761); a quantitative molecular lateral flow assay (DOI: 10.3390/bios12110926); and an allele-specific strip for SARS-CoV-2 mutations, with performance comparable to RT-qPCR (DOI: 10.3389/fcimb.2022.902914). The BZ COVID-19 NALF assay showed sensitivity 95.05% and specificity 99.03% against Allplex (DOI: 10.3390/diagnostics13061118), a target-independent nucleic acid device was developed for respiratory viruses (DOI: 10.1016/j.mex.2023.102372), and a dipstick was designed to identify and differentiate SARS-CoV-2 variants (abstract incomplete) (DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2024.1459644).

**Genotyping and drug-resistance mutations.** The strategy is oligonucleotide ligation or allele-specific probes on a strip to read single mutations. For HIV-1 drug resistance: a paper-based isothermal amplification with an oligonucleotide ligation assay detected 200 mutant copies in a background of 800 wild-type copies with sensitivity and specificity of 100% (DOI: 10.1016/j.ab.2017.12.008); OLA-Simple reached specificity 99.6% and sensitivity 98.2% against Sanger sequencing (DOI: 10.1016/j.ebiom.2019.11.002) and high sensitivity against MiSeq (DOI: 10.1097/qad.0000000000002524); a near-point-of-care assay detected 100 plasmid copies and was more sensitive than Sanger (DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2021.122378). A tunable duplex semiquantitative detection of hepatitis B virus used ligation and amplification with a visual strip (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.1c05039).

**Paper-based, pen and cartridge devices.** Several studies integrate extraction, amplification and strip in one device. A paper-based device for influenza A (H1N1) combined RNA extraction, in situ isothermal amplification and a lateral flow strip, detecting 10^6 copies/mL (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.5b01594). A palm-sized microfluidic device with commercial pregnancy strips read SARS-CoV-2 (DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.1c01829), a disposable pen-like sensor with a nucleic acid amplification strip reached 0.5 copies/uL with accuracy comparable to qPCR (DOI: 10.1016/j.biosx.2022.100248), nanotrap particle concentration with a closed paper strip monitored SARS-CoV-2 and pepper mild mottle virus in wastewater (DOI: 10.3390/bios14030115), and an integrated isothermal test detected HPV16 and HPV18 DNA at 1000 copies per test (DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.abn4768). A nanoparticle biosensor for HBV pgRNA reached 50 copies/mL (DOI: 10.1128/spectrum.01918-25). A comparison of an RNA isothermal amplification-gold probe strip with targeted next-generation sequencing for influenza A/B and Mycoplasma showed moderate agreement for influenza (scope check: bacterial co-target) (DOI: 10.3389/fped.2026.1741420).

Limit addressed in 3.5.5: dependence on enzymes and on amplification. What the abstracts show is a wide range of designs: the enzymatic isothermal methods reach single-digit to tens of copies, the enzyme-free circuits reach picomolar to femtomolar limits, and the amplification-free probes reach femtomolar to attomolar limits. Cost: lower sensitivity than amplified methods in most enzyme-free studies, and a need for fluorescence or time-resolved readouts to reach the lowest limits.

## 3.5.6 Integrated, one-pot and multiplex devices

Integrated and multiplex designs are described in the subsections above under the method they use: portable PCR and heated-strip devices (3.5.1), microfluidic, pen-type and closed-tube RPA devices (3.5.3), one-pot Cas12 and Cas13 reactions (3.5.4), and paper, pen and cartridge devices (3.5.5). Multiplex strips are covered under the amplification method that they use, for PCR, LAMP and RPA/RAA in particular. The common finding is that integration reduces steps and contamination risk, and that sensitivity is sometimes lower than in the open-tube version (for example the HIV-1 microRAAD device at 3 x 10^5 viral particles against 100 copies in a tube; DOI: 10.1039/c9lc00506d), so integration is a trade, not a free gain.

Limit addressed in 3.5.6: the number of tubes and steps between the sample and the strip; cost: more complex devices, and fewer clinical validations.

## 3.5.7 What the evidence supports

For the question of this review, nucleic-acid strips give the lowest detection limits in the whole review, in several studies single-digit copy numbers per reaction, and sensitivities equal to or above those of the reference PCR in the same paper (Table E4a). Where a paper states a comparison, the strip is typically between equal to and 1000 times more sensitive than the conventional PCR run beside it (for example 1000-fold for the duck Tembusu virus RT-RAA-LFD and for the avian influenza RAA-CRISPR-Cas13a-LFD, and 100-fold for several RT-RAA and RPA strips), and less sensitive in one case (the bean pod mottle virus RT-CPA-LFD, against TaqMan RT-PCR). These gains belong to the amplification step; the strip contributes a visual readout, and it is not itself sensitised.

A second finding bears directly on the colour signal. When the same reaction is read both by fluorescence and by a strip, the strip is often the less sensitive readout: the mpox virus assay reached 10^1 copies/uL by fluorescence and 10^4 by lateral flow, the avian influenza virus assay 69 against 690 copies/uL, the hepatitis E virus assay 25 against 247, the Lassa virus assay 10 against 100, and the Epstein-Barr virus assay 10 against 10^4 copies per reaction (Table E4b). In a few designs the strip equals or beats the real-time readout (capripoxvirus, human rhinovirus A and C). The colorimetric strip is therefore a convenient end point, and in many CRISPR designs it is the weakest link of the reaction.

Three cautions apply. First, "copies per reaction", "copies per microlitre" and "copies per millilitre" are not interchangeable, so LODs are not compared across studies. Second, reference PCRs differ in design, and positive agreement is sometimes counted over repeated tests of the same samples. Third, many abstracts do not report the sample matrix, number of samples or LOD definition (Table E2). Only one paper in this section was read in full (Table E3).

### Table E2. Strategy overview for nucleic-acid amplification with a strip readout

| Method | What it adds | Examples | Costs and limits seen | Table |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PCR, RT-PCR | established amplification | multiplex dipsticks for hepatitis, influenza, HPV, fish viruses | thermocycler | E1a |
| LAMP | single-temperature amplification | influenza, Ebola, Zika, HIV, animal viruses | complex primer sets; amplicon contamination | E1b |
| RPA, RAA | amplification at 37-42 C, often equipment-free | livestock, poultry, fish, plant and human viruses | amplicon contamination in open designs | E1c |
| CRISPR-Cas | sequence-specific detection, often after RPA or LAMP | African swine fever, SARS-CoV-2, Ebola, Lassa, HBV | extra reaction step; fluorescence often used alongside the strip | E1d |
| Other and amplification-free | alternative isothermal chemistries and probes | NASBA, CPA, rolling circle, XNAzyme, drug-resistance tests | fewer clinical data | E1e |
| Integrated or one-pot devices | fewer user steps | closed RPA-strip device, microfluidic, paper devices | few devices tested at scale | E1b-E1e |
